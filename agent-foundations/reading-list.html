Agent foundations: key papers

Every paper cited on the decks' Key papers slides, in the order the guide introduces them. Each entry links to the paper and to the decks that use it.

Building effective agents
Anthropic · Anthropic Engineering 2024 · cited in What an agent is, Planning and task decomposition

Workflows (predefined code paths) vs agents (LLMs directing their own process); patterns: prompt chaining, routing, parallelization, orchestrator-workers, evaluator-optimizer. Start with the simplest solution.

ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models
Yao et al. · ICLR 2023 · cited in What an agent is, ReAct: reasoning and acting

Interleaves reasoning traces with actions and observations, so reasoning guides tool use and observations ground reasoning.

Remember: +34 and +10 points absolute success on ALFWorld and WebShop
Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools
Schick et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · cited in What an agent is, Tool calling

A model trained self-supervised to decide which APIs to call, when, and with what arguments (calculator, search, QA, translation, calendar).

Tool use with Claude (overview)
Anthropic documentation · platform.claude.com 2026 · cited in Tool calling

Tools defined with input_schema; client tools return stop_reason "tool_use" and are executed by the application, which replies with tool_result; server tools run on Anthropic's side.

Function calling
OpenAI documentation · developers.openai.com 2026 · cited in Tool calling

Function tools defined by JSON Schema with optional strict mode, tool_choice (auto, required, forced) and parallel_tool_calls; results returned as function_call_output items.

Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning
Shinn et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · cited in ReAct: reasoning and acting

Agents write verbal reflections on failed attempts into an episodic memory and use them on the next trial, without weight updates.

Remember: 91% pass@1 on HumanEval vs 80% for GPT-4
AgentBench: Evaluating LLMs (large language models) as Agents
Liu et al. · ICLR 2024 · cited in ReAct: reasoning and acting

Eight interactive environments for LLM agents; poor long-term reasoning, decision-making and instruction following are the main failure causes.

Plan-and-Solve Prompting: Improving Zero-Shot Chain-of-Thought Reasoning by Large Language Models
Wang et al. · ACL 2023 · cited in Planning and task decomposition

First devise a plan that splits the task into subtasks, then carry them out; reduces missing-step errors of zero-shot chain-of-thought.

Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with Large Language Models
Yao et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · cited in Planning and task decomposition

Search over intermediate "thoughts" with self-evaluation, lookahead and backtracking instead of a single left-to-right chain.

Remember: Game of 24: 74% vs 4% with chain-of-thought (GPT-4)
HuggingGPT: Solving AI (artificial intelligence) Tasks with ChatGPT and its Friends in Hugging Face
Shen et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · cited in Planning and task decomposition

An LLM (large language model) controller plans subtasks, selects specialist models by their descriptions, executes them and summarises the results.

Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3)
Brown et al. · NeurIPS 2020 · cited in Prompting and in-context learning

Besides in-context learning, a systematic contamination study: 13-gram overlap flags and clean vs dirty scores.

Remember: 13-gram overlap; a filtering bug left some overlap in
Rethinking the Role of Demonstrations: What Makes In-Context Learning Work?
Min et al. · EMNLP 2022 · cited in Prompting and in-context learning

Ground-truth labels in demonstrations matter little; the label space, the distribution of input text and the format drive in-context learning.

Remember: random labels cost 0–5% absolute
Calibrate Before Use: Improving Few-Shot Performance of Language Models
Zhao, Wallace, Feng, Klein, Singh · ICML 2021 · cited in Prompting and in-context learning

Few-shot accuracy swings with format, examples and order because of majority label, recency and common token biases; contextual calibration with a content-free input corrects them.

Remember: up to 30.0% absolute accuracy gain (GPT-3, GPT-2)
Fantastically Ordered Prompts and Where to Find Them: Overcoming Few-Shot Prompt Order Sensitivity
Lu et al. · ACL 2022 · cited in Prompting and in-context learning

The order of the same few-shot examples can make the difference between near state-of-the-art and random-guess performance, across model sizes.

Remember: order alone: near state of the art to chance
Quantifying Language Models' Sensitivity to Spurious Features in Prompt Design
Sclar et al. · ICLR 2024 · cited in Prompting and in-context learning

Shows equivalent prompt formats (separators, casing, spacing) change accuracy dramatically, and proposes reporting the spread (FormatSpread).

Remember: up to 76 accuracy points (LLaMA-2-13B)
Many-Shot In-Context Learning
Agarwal et al. · NeurIPS 2024 · cited in Prompting and in-context learning

Hundreds or thousands of in-context examples give large gains over few-shot, can override pretraining biases and perform comparably to fine-tuning; Reinforced ICL uses model-generated rationales.

Remember: Gemini 1.5 Pro; gains across generative and discriminative tasks
DSPy: Compiling Declarative Language Model Calls into Self-Improving Pipelines
Khattab et al. · arXiv 2023 · cited in Prompting and in-context learning

Programs of declarative LM modules whose prompts and demonstrations are optimised (compiled) against a metric.

Remember: over 25% (GPT-3.5) and 65% (llama2-13b-chat) above standard few-shot prompting
Large Language Models as Optimizers (OPRO)
Yang et al. · ICLR 2024 · cited in Prompting and in-context learning

An LLM proposes new instructions given previous ones and their scores; the best prompts beat human-designed ones.

Remember: up to 8% on GSM8K and up to 50% on Big-Bench Hard
Prompting best practices (Claude documentation)
Anthropic · Claude platform docs 2026 · cited in Prompting and in-context learning

Be clear and direct, explain the reasons for rules, use 3–5 examples in example tags, structure with XML tags, put long documents first and the query last; prefilled responses are not supported from Claude 4.6 models (use structured outputs).

Remember: queries at the end: up to 30% better response quality on long, complex inputs
Effective context engineering for AI (artificial intelligence) agents
Anthropic · Anthropic Engineering 2025 · cited in Prompting and in-context learning

Context as a finite "attention budget" (context rot); just-in-time retrieval; compaction, structured note-taking and sub-agents for long-horizon tasks.
