Decoding: from probabilities to text

How the next token is chosen from the model's probabilities: greedy and beam search, temperature, top-k, top-p and min-p, repetition penalties, the order a serving engine applies them, and why temperature 0 is still not deterministic.

What is the problem?At every step the model gives a probability for each token in its vocabulary, and something has to pick one. Always taking the most likely token produces bland, repetitive text; sampling from the full distribution wanders into the unreliable tail and produces incoherent text.
Core ideaDecoding is a rule for choosing from the next-token distribution. Greedy and beam search maximise likelihood, which suits short, closed answers. For open-ended text, reshape the distribution with temperature, cut its tail with top-k, top-p (nucleus) or min-p, then sample. Penalties discourage repeats. Serving engines apply these steps in a fixed order, for every sequence in the batch.
1 · Problem

The model gives probabilities, not words

At every step the model outputs one score (logit) for every token in the vocabulary; softmax turns them into probabilities. Generation is a long chain of choices from these distributions.

A choice per token

Hundreds of choices for a paragraph, each from a distribution over the whole vocabulary.

Maximising is bland

Holtzman et al.: beam search (size 32) on GPT-2 Large produced degenerate repetition; the probability of a repeated phrase rises with each repetition.

Pure sampling is incoherent

The long tail of unlikely tokens has substantial mass in total, so full sampling regularly draws a token that derails the text.

2 · Limitation

Why one rule does not fit every step

Fixed k

Top-k with k = 10 (Fan et al. 2018, story generation) keeps ten tokens whether the model has one sensible choice or hundreds.

Temperature alone

Lowering temperature removes tail mass everywhere but also removes diversity; raising it brings the incoherence back.

Repetition loops

Once a phrase repeats, repeating it again becomes more likely (Holtzman et al., Figure 4), so greedy text gets stuck.

4 · Core idea

Reshape, cut the tail, sample

Greedy and beam search

Take the argmax, or keep the B most likely partial sequences by total log-probability. Good for short, closed outputs; degenerates on open-ended text.

Temperature T

Divide the logits by T before softmax, so probabilities become proportional to p^(1/T). T < 1 sharpens, T > 1 flattens, T → 0 is greedy.

Truncation

Top-k: the k most likely tokens. Top-p (nucleus): the smallest set with cumulative probability ≥ p. Min-p: tokens with p ≥ min_p × p_max, so the cut scales with the model's confidence.

Penalties

Repetition penalty (CTRL, θ ≈ 1.2 with greedy decoding): divide a seen token's positive logit by θ. Frequency and presence penalties subtract an amount per count and per occurrence.

5 · How it works

Inside a serving engine

Parameter vLLM default What it does
temperature 1.0 divides logits; below 1e-5 means greedy (then n must be 1)
top_k 0 (off) keep the k most likely tokens
top_p 1.0 (off) keep the smallest set with cumulative probability ≥ p
min_p 0.0 (off) keep tokens with p ≥ min_p × p_max
repetition_penalty 1.0 (off) scales logits of tokens seen in the prompt or the output
frequency_penalty, presence_penalty 0.0 (off) subtract per count / per occurrence, output tokens only (OpenAI definition)
seed none per-request random seed

Hosted reasoning models fix these knobs. On the Claude API, temperature, top_p and top_k are deprecated: models released after Claude Opus 4.6 reject any non-default value with a 400 error Legacy. OpenAI's latest-model guide says to remove temperature and top_p when reasoning effort is not none. The knobs matter most when you serve open models yourself.

Constrained decoding

Structured output (JSON (JavaScript Object Notation) schemas, grammars) is also a decoding step: before sampling, a grammar engine such as XGrammar sets the logits of every token that would break the grammar to −∞, so only valid continuations can be drawn. See the serving-for-agents deck in Visual Agents.

5 · How it works

Worked example: one step, four rules

Five candidate tokens with probabilities 0.50, 0.25, 0.15, 0.06 and 0.04 at temperature 1.

Worked example

  1. Top-k = 2 keeps 0.50 and 0.25 and renormalises them to 0.67 and 0.33.
  2. Top-p = 0.9: the cumulative sums are 0.50, 0.75, 0.90, so the first three survive (the token that reaches 0.9 is included), renormalised to 0.56, 0.28, 0.17.
  3. Min-p = 0.1: the threshold is 0.1 × 0.50 = 0.05, so 0.50, 0.25, 0.15 and 0.06 survive and 0.04 is dropped.
  4. Temperature 0.5 first: probabilities become proportional to p², giving 0.74, 0.18, 0.07, 0.01, 0.005. Now top-p = 0.9 keeps only two tokens (0.74 + 0.18 = 0.92).
Temperature changes the shape; truncation decides which tokens can be drawn at all. Because temperature comes first, it changes what top-p keeps.
6 · Why it works

Why truncation works, and what it cannot fix

The tail is unreliable

Each tail token is unlikely, but together they are drawn often; cutting the tail removes most incoherent draws (Holtzman et al.).

Adaptive cut

Top-p and min-p keep few tokens when the distribution is peaked and many when it is flat. In the notebook's 1,000-token example, as T goes from 0.5 to 1.6, top-p 0.9 keeps 4 → 739 tokens and min-p 0.1 keeps 2 → 28.

Disputed: min-p vs top-p

Nguyen et al. (ICLR 2025) report min-p improves quality and diversity at high temperature. Schaeffer et al. (2025) re-analysed the evidence and found no improvement once hyperparameter tuning is controlled. Treat it as one more option, not a default.

Greedy is not reproducible by default

Floating-point sums depend on batch size. With batch-invariant kernels, all 1,000 temperature-0 completions matched (Thinking Machines).

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Temperature 0 gives the same output every time."

Thinking Machines sampled 1,000 completions at temperature 0 and got 80 distinct ones; they first diverged at token 103. The Claude API documentation also notes temperature 0.0 is not fully deterministic.

"The most likely sequence is the best text."

Holtzman et al.: maximisation is an inappropriate objective for open-ended generation; beam search text is bland and repetitive.

"Min-p has been shown to beat top-p."

The original paper reports gains; a 2025 re-analysis found none when tuning effort is equalised. Sources disagree.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Temperature 0 means greedy. In vLLM any temperature below 1e-5 switches to argmax, and n must then be 1.
  • Adaptive beats fixed. A fixed k is too many tokens when the model is sure and too few when it is not; top-p and min-p follow the distribution.
  • Match the rule to the task. Greedy or beam search for short closed outputs (extraction, translation); sampling with truncation for open-ended text.

⚠ Traps

  • Expecting temperature 0 to be deterministic. 1,000 greedy runs of a Qwen3-235B model gave 80 distinct completions, because kernels depend on batch size.
  • Beam search for open-ended text. Maximising likelihood leads to bland, repetitive text (Holtzman et al.).
  • Strong repetition penalties. They also suppress tokens that should repeat (names, code identifiers); vLLM's repetition penalty counts prompt tokens too.

Check yourself

With vLLM's defaults (temperature 1.0, top_p 1.0, top_k 0, min_p 0.0), how is the next token chosen?

B. All truncation is off and temperature is 1, so it samples from the unmodified distribution. Set the parameters explicitly for your use case.

Why does lowering the temperature change what top-p keeps?

Temperature is applied before truncation. A sharper distribution reaches the cumulative threshold with fewer tokens.

With min-p = 0.1, what is the cut when the top token has probability 0.9? And 0.2?

Thresholds 0.09 and 0.02. When the model is confident only a few tokens survive; when it is unsure, many do.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Decoding turns probabilities into text: maximise for short closed answers; for open-ended text, reshape with temperature, cut the unreliable tail, then sample.
Say it in 30 seconds

At each step the model outputs a distribution over the vocabulary. Greedy decoding and beam search pick the most likely continuation, which is right for short closed answers but makes open-ended text bland and repetitive. Sampling instead divides the logits by a temperature and truncates the unreliable tail with top-k, top-p (the smallest set reaching cumulative probability p) or min-p (tokens above a fraction of the top probability). vLLM applies penalties, then temperature, then min-p, then top-k and top-p. Hosted reasoning models increasingly fix these parameters, and even temperature 0 is not deterministic without batch-invariant kernels.

Maximise: greedy and beam search, for closed tasks
Reshape: temperature: p^(1/T)
Truncate: top-k, top-p, min-p
In practice: fixed order in engines; hosted reasoning models fix these knobs

The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration
Holtzman et al. · ICLR 2020

Maximizing likelihood when decoding gives bland, repetitive text; nucleus (top-p) sampling samples from the smallest set of tokens holding probability p.

Hierarchical Neural Story Generation
Fan, Lewis, Dauphin · ACL 2018

Story generation with top-k random sampling: at each step sample from the k most likely tokens.

Remember: k = 10
CTRL: A Conditional Transformer Language Model for Controllable Generation
Keskar et al. · arXiv (Salesforce) 2019

Introduced penalized sampling (the repetition penalty): discount the scores of previously generated tokens.

Remember: greedy decoding with θ ≈ 1.2
Turning Up the Heat: Min-p Sampling for Creative and Coherent LLM (large language model) Outputs
Nguyen et al. · ICLR 2025

Min-p keeps tokens whose probability is at least a fraction of the top token's; reports better quality and diversity at high temperature.

Remember: threshold = min_p × p_max
Min-p, Max Exaggeration: A Critical Analysis of Min-p Sampling in Language Models
Schaeffer et al. · arXiv 2025

Re-analysis of the min-p paper's human, benchmark and LLM-judge evidence: min-p does not outperform baseline samplers when hyperparameters are controlled.

Remember: no improvement over top-p, top-k or basic sampling
