Disaggregated prefill and decode

Running prefill and decode on separate GPU (graphics processing unit) pools, each sized and configured for its phase, and moving the KV (key-value) cache between them (DistServe, OSDI 2024; Splitwise, 2024).

What is the problem?Prefill (compute-heavy) and decode (memory-heavy) interfere when they share GPUs, and one configuration can't be optimal for both.
Core ideaSplit the phases across machines. Prefill GPUs compute the prompt and its KV, the KV moves to decode GPUs, and each pool gets its own parallelism and scale to maximize goodput under SLOs (service-level objectives).
1 · Problem

Two phases with opposite needs

Prefill: "prompt computation"

Compute-intensive: many tokens per pass. Sets TTFT (time to first token).

Decode: "token generation"

Memory-intensive: one token per sequence per step. Sets TPOT (time per output token).

Splitwise describes these as a compute-intensive prompt phase and a memory-intensive token-generation phase.
2 · Limitation

Colocation forces compromises

Interference

A new prompt's prefill shares iterations with ongoing decodes and slows them, or waits behind them (deck 04).

One configuration for two jobs

The best parallelism and batch size for prefill and for decode differ, but colocation forces a single choice.

Coupled scaling

You can't add prefill capacity without also adding decode capacity, even if only one is short.

4 · Core idea

Prefill pool → KV transfer → decode pool

Prefill pool

Sized and parallelized for TTFT, batching prompts for compute efficiency.

KV transfer

The prompt's KV cache moves to a decode GPU. Placement and interconnect bandwidth decide how much this costs.

Decode pool

Sized for TPOT and KV capacity, running large continuous batches.

At a glance

Each pool gets its own GPUs, parallelism and scale, so prefill bursts don't slow ongoing decodes.

5 · How it works

Worked example: what does moving the KV cost?

Llama-3-70B KV cache: 320 KiB per token (deck 02). A 4,000-token prompt.

Worked example

  1. KV to move: 4,000 × 320 KiB = 1.22 GiB (1.31 GB).
  2. Over a 400 Gb/s NIC (network interface card, 50 GB/s): 26 ms.
  3. Over NVLink at 900 GB/s (H100): 1.5 ms.
  4. Transfer can overlap with prefill layer by layer, so much of it can be hidden.
Disaggregation pays when the interference it removes costs more than ~26 ms of transfer per request, which is typical with tight TPOT SLOs and long prompts.
6 · Why it works

Why it works, and what it costs

No interference

Long prompts never stall decode iterations, and decode batches never slow prefill.

Independent tuning

Each pool picks its own parallelism, batch size and GPU count for its own SLO, so goodput goes up.

Measured

DistServe: 7.4× more requests or 12.6× tighter SLOs. Splitwise: 1.4× throughput at 20% lower cost, or 2.35× throughput at the same cost and power.

Cost

KV transfer needs fast interconnect and smart placement, and the pools add operational complexity.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Disaggregation is always better than chunked prefill."

It adds KV transfer and operational complexity. Chunked prefill (deck 04) also reduces interference on shared GPUs. Which wins depends on workload, scale and interconnect.

"Prefill and decode pools must use the same hardware."

Splitwise explicitly provisions different machines for each phase, and heterogeneous pools are part of the appeal.

"The KV transfer is negligible."

For long prompts on large models it is gigabytes per request. It needs NVLink/RDMA-class (RDMA = remote direct memory access) links or overlapping with prefill.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Transfer time = KV bytes ÷ link bandwidth. Compute it before deciding; GQA (grouped-query attention) and FP8 (8-bit floating point) KV shrink it.
  • Size pools by demand. Ratio of prefill GPUs to decode GPUs follows the prompt/output mix.
  • Optimize per phase. Different parallelism and even different GPU types per pool (Splitwise).

⚠ Traps

  • Short prompts, small models. Little interference to remove; the extra hop may cost more than it saves.
  • Slow links. Without fast interconnect the KV transfer dominates TTFT.
  • Pool imbalance. A shift in traffic mix leaves one pool idle and the other overloaded.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Disaggregated serving runs prefill and decode on separate, separately tuned GPU pools and moves the KV cache between them. That removes interference and raises goodput, at the price of fast KV transfer.
Say it in 30 seconds

Prefill is compute-bound and decode is memory-bound, and sharing GPUs makes them interfere and forces one configuration onto both. DistServe and Splitwise run the phases on different machines: the prefill pool computes the prompt and its KV cache, the KV moves over a fast interconnect, and the decode pool streams tokens. Each pool is sized for its own SLO. DistServe reported 7.4× more requests or 12.6× tighter SLOs, and Splitwise 1.4× throughput at 20% lower cost. The cost is gigabytes of KV transfer for long prompts.

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

DistServe: Disaggregating Prefill and Decoding for Goodput-optimized Large Language Model Serving
Zhong et al. · OSDI 2024

Defines serving quality as goodput, the request rate served within both TTFT and TPOT targets, and runs prefill and decode on separate GPUs so they stop interfering.

Remember: 7.4× more requests or 12.6× tighter SLO than colocated systems
Splitwise: Efficient Generative LLM Inference Using Phase Splitting
Patel et al. · ISCA 2024

Characterizes prompt computation as compute-intensive and token generation as memory-intensive, and splits them onto separate machines, possibly of different GPU types.

Remember: 1.4× throughput at 20% lower cost, or 2.35× throughput at the same cost and power
