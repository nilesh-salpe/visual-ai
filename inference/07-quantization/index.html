Quantization for inference

Fewer bits per weight, activation and KV entry: INT8 and INT4 scales, the activation-outlier problem and SmoothQuant's fix, weight-only methods (GPTQ, AWQ), and FP8.

What is the problem?Decode speed and capacity are limited by bytes: weights and KV cache read every step. 16-bit numbers are expensive to move.
Core ideaStore numbers in 8, 4 or fewer bits with scales, chosen per tensor, per channel or per group, and handle outliers so accuracy survives.
1 · Problem

Bytes, not FLOPs, set decode speed

At small batch, every decode step streams the weights (and the KV cache) from HBM. Halve the bytes and you roughly halve that time, and twice as much fits in memory.

Weight bytes for a 70B-parameter model at different precisions.

2 · Limitation

Naive rounding fails on LLM activations

One scale for everything

A per-tensor scale must cover the largest value. Everything else gets only a few effective levels.

Activation outliers

A few activation channels are far larger than the rest, so per-tensor INT8 on activations loses the small values (the problem SmoothQuant targets).

Very low bits

Naive 3–4-bit rounding of weights loses accuracy without smarter error handling.

3 · Your turn

Deal with the outlier channel

A few activation channels have huge values, which breaks per-tensor INT8. Which fix keeps the matmul result mathematically the same?

B is SmoothQuant. X·W = (X·diag(s)⁻¹)·(diag(s)·W), so dividing activation channel j by sj and multiplying weight row j by sj moves the difficulty into the weights, which are easier to quantize, with no change to the result.
4 · Core idea

Scales, granularity, and where the bits go

q = clip(round(x / s), −qmax, qmax),   x̂ = q · s,   s = max|x| / qmax

Granularity

Per tensor → per channel → per group: smaller groups mean tighter ranges and less error, at the cost of storing more scales.

W8A8 (SmoothQuant)

INT8 weights and activations via smoothing: up to 1.56× speed and 2× less memory with negligible accuracy loss.

Weight-only, 3–4 bits

GPTQ: second-order one-shot quantization; a 175B model in ~4 GPU-hours; 3.25× (A100) and 4.5× (A6000) over FP16. AWQ: protect the ~1% salient weights found from activations; >3× over HF FP16.

FP8

E4M3 and E5M2 floating point, with per-tensor or per-block scales. It matched 16-bit training quality in Micikevicius et al. (2022).

6 · Why it works

Why it works

Fewer bytes, faster decode

Decode is memory-bound, so moving fewer bytes per step speeds it up directly and frees memory for more KV.

Finer scales, smaller ranges

Error is about half a step, and the step is range / levels, so finer granularity means smaller error.

Outliers moved, not removed

SmoothQuant's transform is exactly equivalent and balances activation and weight ranges.

Error-aware rounding

GPTQ compensates rounding errors using second-order information, and AWQ protects activation-salient weights.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Quantization always costs a lot of accuracy."

With good scales and outlier handling, INT8 (SmoothQuant) and 3–4-bit weight-only (GPTQ, AWQ) report small or negligible losses on large models.

"Weight-only INT4 speeds up everything equally."

It reduces weight bytes, which helps memory-bound decode the most. Compute-bound prefill gains less.

"Per-tensor scales are fine for activations."

LLM activations have outlier channels, so per-tensor activation INT8 needs SmoothQuant-style smoothing or finer scales.

What is the maximum normal value of FP8 E4M3?

448 (E5M2: 57,344).

Why is SmoothQuant's transform "free" at inference?

X·W = (X·diag(s)⁻¹)(diag(s)·W). The weight scaling is folded in offline and the activation division can be fused into the previous layer, so the result is unchanged and nothing is added at runtime.

What fraction of weights does AWQ find to be salient?

"Protecting only 1% salient weights can greatly reduce quantization error".
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Quantization cuts the bytes that bound decode. Scales at the right granularity, outlier handling (SmoothQuant), error-aware weight methods (GPTQ, AWQ) and FP8 keep accuracy while moving 2–4× fewer bytes.
Say it in 30 seconds

Decode is memory-bound, so storing weights, activations and KV in fewer bits speeds it up and frees memory. Quantization maps values to integers with a scale. Finer scales (per channel, per group) cut error, but LLM activations have outlier channels that break per-tensor INT8. SmoothQuant moves that difficulty into the weights with an exact rescaling, reporting up to 1.56× speed and 2× less memory. GPTQ and AWQ push weights to 3–4 bits. FP8 (E4M3, max 448; E5M2, max 57,344) uses floating point with scales.
