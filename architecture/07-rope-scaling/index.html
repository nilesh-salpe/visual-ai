Long context I: stretching RoPE

Why a model trained on 4K tokens falls apart at 32K, and how Position Interpolation, NTK-aware (NTK = neural tangent kernel) scaling, YaRN (Yet another rotary-position-embedding extension method) and Llama 3's long-context training fix it.

What is the problem?A RoPE model trained at length L sees angles m·θᵢ only for m < L. Beyond L, the slow pairs reach angles the model has never seen, and attention scores blow up.
Core ideaRemap the rotation frequencies so long positions land on angles seen in training. PI (Position Interpolation) slows every pair by s. NTK-aware raises the base. YaRN slows only the slow pairs and adds a temperature. Then fine-tune briefly, or train long from the start.
1 · Problem

Fast pairs have seen every angle; slow pairs haven't

Each RoPE pair turns at its own speed. During training at L = 4,096 (d = 128, base 10,000), a fast pair goes round the circle hundreds of times, so every angle is familiar. A slow pair covers only part of the circle. Pick a pair and an extension factor: green is the arc seen in training, red is new territory at s·L.

2 · Limitation

Why the obvious options fail

Just run longer (extrapolate)

Unseen angles in the slow pairs produce what PI calls "catastrophically high attention scores". Perplexity shoots up as soon as you pass L.

Train long from the start

Attention costs grow with sequence length, and most pretraining documents are short anyway. Paying for 128K context on every step is wasteful.

Squeeze naively

Dividing every position by s keeps angles in range, but neighbouring tokens become s× closer in the fast pairs. Local order gets blurry, which costs short-context quality unless you fine-tune.

3 · Your turn

What does Position Interpolation do?

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

A LLaMA model trained at 2,048 tokens should handle 8,192. What does Position Interpolation (Chen et al. 2023) change?

B. PI "linearly down-scales the input position indices to match the original context window size". Every angle stays inside the trained range. About 1,000 fine-tuning steps were enough to reach 32,768 tokens on LLaMA 7B–65B, and the authors bound interpolation's error at about 600× smaller than extrapolation's.
4 · Core idea

Three ways to remap frequencies

PI: slow everything by s

θᵢ → θᵢ / s for every pair. Simple and safe, but it compresses local resolution by s in the fast pairs.

NTK-aware: change the base

base → base · sd/(d−2). The fastest pair is unchanged, the slowest is slowed by exactly s, and those in between are slowed smoothly. It keeps local order sharp, but most slow pairs still slightly overshoot.

YaRN: by parts, plus temperature

Count each pair's turns within L: r = L/λᵢ. If r < α (=1): interpolate fully (θ/s). If r > β (=32): leave alone. Blend linearly in between. Scale logits by √(1/t) = 0.1·ln s + 1.

Why YaRN adds a temperature

Stretching the context spreads attention over many more tokens, so the softmax gets flatter (higher entropy). YaRN scales q and k so the logits are multiplied by (0.1·ln s + 1)²; at s = 8, √(1/t) ≈ 1.208. The coefficient was fit empirically on LLaMA models. YaRN reports needing about 10× fewer tokens and 2.5× fewer training steps than previous methods: 400 steps, about 0.1% of the original pretraining data, and it was the first method to extend Llama 2's effective context to 128K.

5 · How it works

Or train long directly: the Llama 3 recipe

A larger RoPE base from the start

Llama 3 sets the RoPE base to 500,000 (Llama 2: 10,000) "to better support longer contexts". Every pair except the first rotates more slowly, so more of the spectrum stays distinguishable at long range.

Grow the context in stages

Pretrain at 8K, then extend in six stages up to 128K using about 800B tokens. Each stage continues until short-context performance has fully recovered and needle-in-a-haystack retrieval is solved perfectly at that length.

5 · How it works

Worked example: extending 4K to 32K (s = 8)

d = 128, base 10,000, trained length L = 4,096.

Worked example

  1. PI: every θ ÷ 8. Neighbouring tokens differ by only 1/8 rad in pair 0 (was 1 rad).
  2. NTK-aware: new base = 10,000 × 8128/126 = 82,685; pair 0 unchanged, pair 63 slowed by exactly 8.
  3. YaRN: pairs with fewer than 1 turn in 4,096 tokens get θ/8; more than 32 turns are left alone; a ramp in between.
  4. YaRN logit temperature: √(1/t) = 0.1·ln 8 + 1 = 1.208.
All three keep slow pairs inside the trained angle range; they differ in how much local resolution they keep and how much fine-tuning they need.
6 · Why it works

Why remapping works

Attention only needs familiar angles

The model learned what each angle in each pair means. Keep every (pair, angle) combination inside the trained range and attention behaves normally, even at positions it never saw.

Fast pairs don't need help

A pair that turned hundreds of times during training has seen every angle, so its behaviour at long range is not new. Leaving it alone (NTK, YaRN) keeps fine local resolution.

Slow pairs do

Pairs with wavelength longer than L only ever covered part of the circle. They are where unseen angles come from, so they get the full s× slow-down.

Fine-tuning closes the gap

Any remapping shifts the distribution a little. A short fine-tune at the new length lets the model adapt; the methods differ mostly in how short that can be.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"A 128K context window means the model uses all 128K tokens well."

The window is what it accepts. How well it retrieves and reasons over distant tokens has to be measured (needle-in-a-haystack, long-document tasks). Llama 3 used both as stage criteria.

"Position Interpolation needs no training."

PI keeps angles in range but compresses local resolution. The paper fine-tunes for about 1,000 steps.

"Raising the RoPE base is all you need."

A bigger base changes the frequencies the model was trained on. It has to be trained (or fine-tuned) at long lengths with that base, which is what Llama 3's staged continued pretraining does.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Find the at-risk pairs. Pairs with wavelength 2π/θᵢ > L never completed a turn in training.
  • NTK base: b·s^(d/(d−2)). Keeps the fastest pair, slows the slowest by s.
  • YaRN defaults for Llama: α = 1, β = 32. Plus temperature 0.1·ln s + 1.

⚠ Traps

  • Skipping fine-tuning. PI and YaRN both fine-tune; extrapolation without it fails.
  • Trusting the advertised window. Check retrieval and long-document quality at the length you need.
  • Mismatched rope_scaling at inference. Serving must apply the same scaling as fine-tuning.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Long context means keeping every RoPE angle inside the range seen in training: slow the slow pairs (PI, NTK, YaRN) or train long with a bigger base (Llama 3).
