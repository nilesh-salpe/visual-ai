The Modern LLM · 05

MQA, GQA and MLA: fewer keys and values

The modelling side of shrinking the KV (key-value) cache: sharing key/value heads (MQA (multi-query attention), GQA (grouped-query attention)), compressing them into a latent (MLA (multi-head latent attention)), and why MLA needs "decoupled" RoPE (rotary position embedding).

What is the problem?During generation every layer stores keys and values for every past token and head. That cache, not compute, limits batch size and context length.
Core ideaShare K/V heads across groups of query heads (MQA: 1 group, GQA: g groups), or compress all heads' K/V into one small latent per token and re-expand on the fly (MLA).
1 · Problem

The cache scales with K/V heads

The KV cache stores 2 × layers × K/V heads × head_dim numbers per token. With standard multi-head attention (MHA), every query head has its own K and V. This deck is the architecture view; memory sizes in GB and serving effects are in Inference deck 02.

2 · Limitation

Why not just use one K/V head?

MQA can lose quality

Multi-query attention (Shazeer 2019) shares one K/V head across all query heads. Ainslie et al.: MQA "can lead to quality degradation and training instability".

Training a second model is expensive

Pretraining a separate MQA model just for faster inference is costly. Ideally you could convert an existing MHA checkpoint.

Tensor parallelism wants ≥ 1 K/V head per GPU

With one K/V head and 8-way tensor parallelism, that head is replicated on every GPU (graphics processing unit), which wastes the saving. A few groups split cleanly across GPUs.

4 · Core idea

Share or compress

MQA (2019)

All query heads share one K and one V. Smallest cache (2·d_h per layer per token); PaLM used it. Risk: quality loss.

GQA (2023)

Query heads split into g groups; each group shares one K/V head. g = 1 is MQA; g = heads is MHA. "Quality close to multi-head attention with comparable speed to multi-query." Llama 2 (34B, 70B), Mistral, Llama 3.

MLA (2024)

Cache one latent vector c (d_c = 512 in DeepSeek-V2) per token and layer, and up-project to every head's K and V when needed. A small extra slice carries RoPE.

Converting MHA into GQA

Ainslie et al. convert a trained multi-head checkpoint: the key and value projection matrices within each group are mean-pooled into one head (mean pooling worked better than picking one head or random init), then the model is uptrained for about 5% of its original pretraining compute. Uptrained GQA reached quality close to MHA with speed close to MQA.

At a glance

MQA: g = 1. GQA: 1 < g < n_h. MHA: g = n_h. MLA compresses all heads into one latent instead.

5 · How it works

Cache per token, per layer: the numbers

DeepSeek-V2's comparison (Table 1) in numbers of elements per token per layer, with its dimensions: n_h = 128 heads, d_h = 128, d_c = 512, RoPE slice d_hR = 64.

Reading the table

MHA: 2·n_h·d_h. GQA: 2·n_g·d_h. MQA: 2·d_h. MLA: d_c + d_hR ≈ (9/2)·d_h, "equal to GQA with only 2.25 groups", while the paper reports stronger performance than MHA. Against DeepSeek 67B, DeepSeek-V2 reports a 93.3% smaller KV cache and 5.76× higher maximum generation throughput (that comparison also includes other changes, such as MoE (Mixture of Experts) and FP8 (8-bit floating point) KV).

5 · How it works

Worked example: cache per token per layer, DeepSeek-V2 dimensions

128 heads of dimension 128; MLA latent d_c = 512 plus a 64-dim decoupled RoPE key. Numbers are cached elements; multiply by 2 for BF16 (bfloat16, a 16-bit floating-point format with the exponent range of 32-bit floats) bytes.

Worked example

  1. MHA: 2 × 128 × 128 = 32,768 elements.
  2. GQA, 8 groups: 2,048 (6.2% of MHA).
  3. MLA: 576 (1.8% of MHA).
  4. MQA: 256 (0.8% of MHA).
  5. MLA ≈ 4.5 head-dims: about GQA with 2.25 groups, with quality reported above MHA.
Sharing (GQA) or compressing (MLA) cuts the cache 16× and 57× here, which converts directly into more concurrent sequences or longer contexts.

Why sharing barely hurts

Decoding is memory-bound: each step reads the whole KV cache to produce one token per sequence. Query heads still differ, and each one attends to the shared keys in its own way, so the model keeps most of its capacity to look for different things. What it loses is the ability to store different keys per head. GQA's groups and MLA's per-head up-projections recover much of that.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"MQA means attention with a single head."

There are still many query heads, each attending differently. Only the keys and values are shared.

"GQA mainly saves FLOPs (floating-point operations)."

The FLOPs for attention scores barely change, because every query head still computes its scores. GQA saves KV-cache memory and memory bandwidth, which is what limits decoding.

"MLA is just low-rank GQA, and RoPE works the same."

MLA's trick depends on absorbing W_UK into W_Q, and RoPE's position-dependent rotation sits between them and breaks that. DeepSeek-V2 needs a separate decoupled RoPE slice with a key shared across heads.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Cache ∝ KV heads. Shrink factor = n_heads / n_kv_heads.
  • Convert MHA → GQA cheaply. Mean-pool the K and V heads in each group, then uptrain with ~5% of the pretraining compute.
  • MLA absorbs W_UK into W_Q. Keys are never materialized at inference.

⚠ Traps

  • Expecting GQA to cut FLOPs. Score FLOPs barely change; the saving is memory and bandwidth.
  • Plain RoPE with MLA. The position rotation breaks absorption; MLA needs a decoupled RoPE slice.
  • TP (tensor parallelism) degree above KV heads. KV heads then get replicated across GPUs, wasting cache.

Check yourself

GQA with g = 1 is…

MQA: one group, so one K/V head shared by every query head.

How do you turn a trained MHA model into a GQA model cheaply?

Mean-pool the K and V projection matrices of the heads in each group into one head, then uptrain for about 5% of the original pretraining compute (Ainslie et al., 2023).

Why can't MLA simply apply RoPE to its reconstructed keys?

Inference relies on precomputing W_Q·W_UKᵀ so keys never get decompressed. RoPE inserts a rotation that depends on both positions between those matrices, so no single precomputed matrix works. DeepSeek-V2 carries position in a separate small RoPE query/key slice instead.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

The KV cache scales with K/V heads: share them (MQA, GQA) or compress them into a latent (MLA). MLA needs decoupled RoPE to keep its absorption trick.
Say it in 30 seconds

During decoding, memory, not compute, is the limit, and the KV cache scales with the number of key/value heads. MQA shares one K/V head across all query heads; it is smallest but can lose quality. GQA uses a few groups, gets quality close to MHA at near-MQA speed, and an existing model can be converted by mean-pooling heads and uptraining for about 5% of the original compute. MLA, from DeepSeek-V2, caches one small latent per token and re-expands it per head; at inference the up-projection is absorbed into the query projection so keys are never materialized. RoPE would break that absorption, so MLA adds a small decoupled RoPE slice.

Problem: KV cache per token = 2 × layers × K/V heads × head_dim.
Share: MQA (1 group) and GQA (g groups): cache shrinks by heads/g; convert by mean-pool + 5% uptraining.
Compress: MLA caches d_c + d_R ≈ 4.5·d_h; absorption + decoupled RoPE.
