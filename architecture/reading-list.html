The Modern LLM Architecture: key papers

Every paper cited on the decks' Key papers slides, in the order the guide introduces them. Each entry links to the paper and to the decks that use it.

Attention Is All You Need
Vaswani et al. · NeurIPS 2017 · cited in What changed since 2017

The Transformer: attention-only encoder-decoder with post-norm residual blocks, sinusoidal positions and a ReLU FFN (feed-forward network). The baseline every later change is measured against.

Remember: 28.4 BLEU (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy, an n-gram overlap score for translation) on WMT (Workshop on Machine Translation) 2014 English-German
LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models
Touvron et al. · arXiv 2023 · cited in What changed since 2017, RoPE, SwiGLU, Putting it together

The recipe most open models followed: pre-normalization with RMSNorm, SwiGLU with a ⅔·4d hidden size, and rotary embeddings.

Remember: trained on 1.0–1.4T tokens
The Llama 3 Herd of Models
Llama Team, AI (artificial intelligence) @ Meta · arXiv 2024 · cited in What changed since 2017, RoPE, Long context I: stretching RoPE, Putting it together

Dense Transformer with GQA (8 KV heads), RoPE base 500,000, a 128K vocabulary and a document mask; staged long-context pretraining to 128K; post-training with SFT (supervised fine-tuning), rejection sampling and DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) in six rounds.

Remember: 128K context after six stages over ~800B tokens
On Layer Normalization in the Transformer Architecture
Xiong et al. · ICML 2020 · cited in Pre-norm and RMSNorm

Mean-field analysis showing post-norm has large gradients near the output at initialization, so it needs learning-rate warm-up; pre-norm does not.

Remember: pre-LN trains without warm-up
Root Mean Square Layer Normalization
Zhang & Sennrich · NeurIPS 2019 · cited in Pre-norm and RMSNorm

Drops LayerNorm's mean-centring and keeps only re-scaling by the root mean square, with comparable quality.

Remember: 7–64% less running time
Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners (GPT-2)
Radford et al. · OpenAI 2019 · cited in Pre-norm and RMSNorm, Decoder-only, and why it won

Decoder-only language model that moved layer normalization to the input of each sub-block (pre-norm) and added a final norm.

Remember: context 1,024 tokens, vocabulary 50,257
RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding
Su et al. · arXiv 2021 · cited in RoPE

Rotates query and key pairs by position-dependent angles so the attention score depends only on relative position, with a long-term decay property.

Remember: θᵢ = 10000^(−2i/d)
GLU Variants Improve Transformer
Shazeer · arXiv 2020 · cited in SwiGLU

Replaces the Transformer FFN with gated linear units (ReGLU, GEGLU, SwiGLU…) at equal parameters by shrinking the hidden size to ⅔.

Remember: log-perplexity 1.677 (ReLU) → 1.636 (SwiGLU)
Language Modeling with Gated Convolutional Networks
Dauphin et al. · ICML 2017 · cited in SwiGLU

Introduces the gated linear unit (GLU): one projection gates another through a sigmoid.

Fast Transformer Decoding: One Write-Head is All You Need
Shazeer · arXiv 2019 · cited in MQA, GQA and MLA

Multi-query attention: all query heads share one key/value head, cutting decode memory bandwidth with minor quality loss.

Remember: 1 K/V head instead of h
GQA: Training Generalized Multi-Query Transformer Models from Multi-Head Checkpoints
Ainslie et al. · EMNLP 2023 · cited in MQA, GQA and MLA

Grouped-query attention plus a recipe to convert multi-head checkpoints by mean-pooling key/value heads and uptraining.

Remember: uptraining with 5% of the original pretraining compute
DeepSeek-V2: A Strong, Economical, and Efficient Mixture-of-Experts Language Model
DeepSeek-AI · arXiv 2024 · cited in MQA, GQA and MLA, Mixture of Experts

Multi-head latent attention (MLA) with decoupled RoPE, and DeepSeekMoE with shared and fine-grained routed experts.

Remember: KV cache −93.3%; 236B total / 21B active parameters
What Language Model Architecture and Pretraining Objective Work Best for Zero-Shot Generalization?
Wang et al. · ICML 2022 · cited in Decoder-only, and why it won

Compares causal decoders, non-causal decoders and encoder-decoders with several objectives at scale.

Remember: causal decoders best zero-shot after pretraining alone
Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer (T5)
Raffel et al. · JMLR 2020 · cited in Decoder-only, and why it won

Systematic study of encoder-decoder pretraining with a span-corruption objective, framing every task as text-to-text.

Remember: corrupts 15% of tokens
BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding
Devlin et al. · NAACL 2019 · cited in Decoder-only, and why it won

Encoder-only model pretrained with masked language modelling; bidirectional context, but not built to generate text.

Remember: masks 15% of tokens
Extending Context Window of Large Language Models via Positional Interpolation
Chen et al. · arXiv 2023 · cited in Long context I: stretching RoPE

Linearly down-scales position indices so a RoPE model sees only trained angles at longer lengths, then fine-tunes briefly.

Remember: to 32,768 tokens with ~1,000 fine-tuning steps
YaRN: Efficient Context Window Extension of Large Language Models
Peng et al. · ICLR 2024 · cited in Long context I: stretching RoPE

Interpolates only low-frequency RoPE pairs (a ramp between α and β) and adds an attention temperature.

Remember: 10× fewer tokens, 2.5× fewer steps; Llama 2 to 128K
Mistral 7B
Jiang et al. · arXiv 2023 · cited in Long context II: sliding window and ALiBi, Putting it together

A 7B model with GQA (grouped-query attention) and sliding-window attention (W = 4,096) plus a rolling-buffer KV cache.

Remember: ~131K theoretical span; 8× less cache at 32K
Longformer: The Long-Document Transformer
Beltagy et al. · arXiv 2020 · cited in Long context II: sliding window and ALiBi

Combines sliding-window local attention with a few global tokens so attention cost grows linearly with length.

Train Short, Test Long: Attention with Linear Biases Enables Input Length Extrapolation
Press et al. · ICLR 2022 · cited in Long context II: sliding window and ALiBi

No position embeddings: each head adds a fixed penalty proportional to query-key distance, with geometric slopes.

Remember: train on 1,024, test on 2,048; 11% faster, 11% less memory
FlashAttention: Fast and Memory-Efficient Exact Attention with IO-Awareness
Dao et al. · NeurIPS 2022 · cited in FlashAttention internals

Tiles attention so scores never leave on-chip SRAM, using an online softmax and recomputation in the backward pass. Exact, with memory linear in sequence length.

Remember: GPT-2 medium: 40.3 → 4.4 GB of memory traffic, 41.7 → 7.3 ms
FlashAttention-2: Faster Attention with Better Parallelism and Work Partitioning
Dao · ICLR 2024 · cited in FlashAttention internals

Fewer non-matmul FLOPs, parallelism across the sequence (outer loop over query blocks) and better work split across warps.

Remember: ~2× FA1; 50–73% of peak on A100
FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision
Shah et al. · NeurIPS 2024 · cited in FlashAttention internals

Uses Hopper features: asynchronous Tensor Cores and TMA, warp specialization, interleaved matmul and softmax, and FP8 with block quantization.

Remember: 740 TFLOP/s FP16 (~75% of H100), ~1.2 PFLOP/s FP8
Online normalizer calculation for softmax
Milakov & Gimelshein · arXiv 2018 · cited in FlashAttention internals

Computes the softmax normalizer in a single pass with a running max and a rescaled running sum, the trick FlashAttention builds on.

Remember: up to 1.3× faster softmax
Switch Transformers: Scaling to Trillion Parameter Models with Simple and Efficient Sparsity
Fedus et al. · JMLR 2022 · cited in Mixture of Experts

Top-1 expert routing, an auxiliary load-balancing loss α·N·Σfᵢ·Pᵢ, expert capacity, and tricks for stable bfloat16 training.

Remember: up to 7× faster pretraining; α = 10⁻²
Mixtral of Experts
Jiang et al. · arXiv 2024 · cited in Mixture of Experts

A sparse MoE with 8 SwiGLU experts per layer and top-2 routing on the Mistral 7B architecture, with dense 32K context.

Remember: 47B total, 13B active parameters
Outrageously Large Neural Networks: The Sparsely-Gated Mixture-of-Experts Layer
Shazeer et al. · ICLR 2017 · cited in Mixture of Experts

The modern sparsely gated MoE layer: a trainable gate picks a few experts per example, decoupling parameters from compute.

Llama 2: Open Foundation and Fine-Tuned Chat Models
Touvron et al. · arXiv 2023 · cited in Putting it together

Adds grouped-query attention for the 34B and 70B models and documents the chat post-training: SFT (supervised fine-tuning), two reward models and iterative RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) with rejection sampling and PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization).

Remember: 27,540 SFT annotations; over 1M human comparisons
