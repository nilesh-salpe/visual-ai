The Modern LLM · 04

SwiGLU feed-forward layers

Why modern LLMs (large language models) replaced the ReLU (rectified linear unit) FFN (feed-forward network) with a gated unit that has three weight matrices, and how its odd hidden sizes (11,008, 14,336) are derived.

What is the problem?The FFN holds most of a Transformer's parameters and FLOPs (floating-point operations). The 2017 ReLU FFN is a plain two-layer MLP (multilayer perceptron), and a better design at the same cost improves the whole model.
Core ideaUse a gated linear unit: one projection, passed through Swish/SiLU (Sigmoid Linear Unit), gates another projection element by element. FFN(x) = (SiLU(xWgate) ⊙ xWup)Wdown. Shrink the hidden size to ⅔·4d to keep the parameter count.
1 · Problem

Most of the model is FFN

Count the weights in one Llama 3 8B layer: attention (Wq, Wk, Wv, Wo with GQA (grouped-query attention)) vs the FFN. The FFN is where most parameters and most per-token FLOPs live, so its design matters more than any other sub-layer's.

Llama 3 8B: d = 4,096, 8 K/V heads of 128, FFN hidden 14,336. Attention = 2·d² + 2·d·1,024; FFN = 3·d·14,336.

2 · Limitation

What a plain ReLU FFN leaves on the table

Hard zeros

ReLU outputs exactly 0 for all negative inputs, so about half the hidden units are off for any token, and their gradient is zero too.

No multiplicative interaction

Each hidden unit is a single linear function passed through a fixed nonlinearity. Units can't modulate one another, so the layer can't directly compute "feature A, but only when feature B is present".

Smoother activations help only a little

GELU (Gaussian Error Linear Unit) and Swish soften ReLU's kink. In Shazeer's ablation they barely move the result: 1.983 and 1.994 log-perplexity vs ReLU's 1.997 at 65K steps.

3 · Your turn

Same budget, three matrices

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

A ReLU FFN with d = 768 and hidden 3,072 has 2 matrices. A SwiGLU (Swish-gated linear unit) FFN has 3. What hidden size keeps the parameter count the same?

B. 2 · 768 · 3,072 = 3 · 768 · h, so h = ⅔ · 3,072 = 2,048. That is exactly what Shazeer used: "we reduce the hidden layer to d_ff = 2048, so as to maintain the same parameter and operation counts as the base model."
4 · Core idea

Gate × value

Gate path

g = SiLU(x W_gate), where SiLU(z) = z·σ(z), also called Swish₁. A smooth "how much to let through" signal per hidden unit, slightly negative for negative inputs.

Value path

u = x W_up: a plain linear projection with no nonlinearity. It carries what gets let through, and its sign can be either way.

Combine and project

FFN(x) = (g ⊙ u) W_down. The element-wise product lets one projection modulate another. That multiplicative interaction is what a ReLU FFN lacks.

FFNSwiGLU(x) = (Swish₁(xW) ⊗ xV) W₂ (Shazeer 2020, eq. 6, biases omitted)

The GLU family

GLU (Dauphin et al. 2017) uses a sigmoid gate: σ(xW) ⊗ xV. Shazeer tries several gates: ReGLU (ReLU), GEGLU (GELU), SwiGLU (Swish) and Bilinear (no nonlinearity at all). All gated versions beat the non-gated baselines in his Table 1; GEGLU and SwiGLU are best. PaLM and LLaMA chose SwiGLU. Llama's code calls the matrices w1 (gate), w3 (up) and w2 (down).

At a glance

Three matrices: gate, up, down. The element-wise product is the "gated linear unit".

5 · How it works

Worked example: the hidden size and the parameter budget

d = 4,096. A ReLU FFN with hidden 4d has 2 matrices; SwiGLU has 3.

Worked example

  1. ReLU FFN: 2 × 4,096 × 16,384 = 134.2M parameters.
  2. Equal budget: hidden = ⅔ × 16,384 = 10,922.7 → LLaMA rounds up to a multiple of 256: 11,008.
  3. SwiGLU params: 3 × 4,096 × 11,008 = 135.3M (≈ the same budget).
  4. Llama 3 8B multiplies by 1.3 and rounds to 1,024: 14,336 = 3.5·d, spending more on the FFN.
The ⅔ rule keeps SwiGLU cost-neutral; odd hidden sizes come from rounding up to hardware-friendly multiples.
6 · Why it works

Why it works: the evidence, and the honest answer

Shazeer 2020, Table 1: held-out log-perplexity on C4 for T5-base after 524,288 steps (lower is better). Every variant has the same parameters and operations.

Same cost, consistently better

Gated variants win at both training lengths and also on downstream GLUE (General Language Understanding Evaluation benchmark) and SuperGLUE fine-tuning in the same paper. That consistency is why PaLM and LLaMA adopted SwiGLU.

Why, exactly? Nobody fully knows

The paper's conclusion: "We offer no explanation as to why these architectures seem to work; we attribute their success, as all else, to divine benevolence." The usual intuition is that multiplicative gating adds expressiveness, but this is empirical.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"SwiGLU makes the FFN bigger."

At the ⅔·4d hidden size it has the same parameters and FLOPs as the 4d ReLU FFN. Llama 3's 1.3 multiplier is a separate decision to spend more parameters there.

"Swish and SwiGLU are the same thing."

Swish (SiLU) is the activation function. SwiGLU is the whole gated unit: Swish of one projection times another projection, then a down projection. FFNSwish alone scored 1.994, about the same as ReLU.

"Gating was introduced to save compute."

It costs the same compute. It was introduced for quality: lower perplexity at equal parameters.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Hidden ≈ ⅔·4d. Then round up to a multiple of 256 (LLaMA) or 1,024 (Llama 3).
  • FFN params = 3·d·hidden. About 70% of a dense Llama's parameters.
  • SiLU = Swish₁ = x·σ(x). hidden_act: "silu" in configs means SwiGLU.

⚠ Traps

  • Counting two matrices. Gated FFNs have three: gate, up, down.
  • Assuming 4d hidden. Real models use ⅔·4d or a tuned multiplier (Llama 3: 3.5·d).
  • Confusing Swish with SwiGLU. Swish alone (no gate) performed like ReLU in Shazeer's table.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

SwiGLU gates one projection with SiLU of another: three matrices at ⅔ the width, same cost as the ReLU FFN, consistently lower perplexity.
Say it in 30 seconds

The FFN is most of a Transformer's parameters. The 2017 version is a ReLU MLP with hidden size 4d. SwiGLU computes SiLU of x times W-gate, multiplies that element-wise by x times W-up, and projects back down with W-down. The gate lets one projection modulate the other. Three matrices instead of two means the hidden size drops to two-thirds of 4d to keep parameters equal, which real models round up to hardware-friendly multiples, giving 11,008 for LLaMA-7B and 14,336 for Llama 3 8B. Shazeer's ablation shows lower perplexity at the same cost, and PaLM and LLaMA adopted it, although nobody has a full explanation of why it works.

Problem: the FFN dominates parameters; the ReLU MLP is not the best use of them.
Core idea: (SiLU(xW_gate) ⊙ xW_up)W_down; hidden ≈ ⅔·4d keeps cost equal.
Evidence: Shazeer Table 1: 1.677 → 1.636 log-ppl at the same parameters and operations.
