Beyond attention: state space models, linear attention and hybrids

Layers that keep a fixed-size state instead of a growing KV (key-value) cache: linear attention, selective state space models (Mamba, Mamba-2), the gated delta rule, why they struggle to copy and recall, and the hybrid stacks now shipping in open models.

What is the problem?Attention compares each token with every earlier token. Training cost grows quadratically with sequence length, and at inference every attention layer caches keys and values for every past token, so long contexts, long reasoning traces and agents make decoding memory-bound and expensive.
Core ideaReplace most attention layers with layers that keep a fixed-size state updated once per token: linear attention (a running sum of key-value outer products), selective state space models such as Mamba (an input-dependent recurrence), and delta-rule layers such as Gated DeltaNet (overwrite the value stored for a key, decay the rest). A fixed state cannot hold every token exactly, so these layers are weak at copying and in-context recall. Shipping models are therefore hybrids: mostly linear or SSM layers plus a few full-attention layers (1 in 4 to 1 in 8).
1 · Problem

Attention gets expensive as context grows

Long documents, long reasoning traces and agents that run for hundreds of turns all push context lengths up. Attention's costs grow with them.

Quadratic training cost

Each token attends to every earlier token, so compute per sequence grows with N².

A cache that grows

At inference each attention layer stores keys and values for every past token; memory per sequence grows linearly with context.

Decoding is memory-bound

Each new token re-reads the whole KV cache, so long contexts lower throughput and the batch size that fits (KV cache deck).

2 · Limitation

Earlier fixes kept full attention

Fewer KV heads

GQA (grouped-query attention) and MLA (multi-head latent attention) shrink the cache by a constant factor; it still grows with every token.

Local windows

Sliding-window attention caps memory, but a layer cannot see past its window.

Classic RNNs

A fixed-size state is cheap at inference, but LSTMs (long short-term memory networks) train sequentially and lagged transformers in quality.

4 · Core idea

A fixed-size state, updated selectively

Linear attention

Replace softmax(q·k) with a kernel φ(q)·φ(k). Attention becomes a running sum S ← S + φ(k)vᵀ read as φ(q)ᵀS: parallel in training, an RNN (recurrent neural network) at inference (up to 4000× faster autoregressive prediction on very long sequences, Katharopoulos et al.). RWKV and RetNet are related designs.

Selective SSMs (Mamba)

Structured state space models (S4) with parameters that depend on the input, so each token decides what to keep or forget. Mamba: 5× the generation throughput of transformers; Mamba-3B matched transformers twice its size. Mamba-2 (state space duality) is 2 to 8× faster.

Delta rule and gating

Gated DeltaNet overwrites the value stored for a key (the delta rule) and decays old memory (gating); it beat Mamba-2 and DeltaNet on language modelling, in-context retrieval and long-context tasks.

Hybrids

Keep a few full-attention layers for exact recall. An 8B Mamba-2-Hybrid (24 Mamba-2, 4 attention, 28 MLP (multilayer perceptron) layers) beat a matched 8B transformer on all 12 standard tasks (+2.65 points) and is predicted to generate up to 8× faster.

Why a fixed state cannot copy everything

Jelassi et al. prove that a two-layer transformer can copy strings of length exponential in its size, while any model with a fixed-size state is limited by that state, and confirm it empirically: transformers learned copying and retrieval from context far more efficiently. Zoology found the same gap on real language (associative recall) and showed that adding sparse, input-dependent attention closed 97.4% of it.

5 · How it works

Worked example: decode memory in a 3 : 1 hybrid

Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B (model card): 48 layers, 12 with gated attention (2 KV heads of dimension 256) and 36 with Gated DeltaNet (32 value heads, head dimension 128). BF16 (bfloat16, a 16-bit floating-point format with the exponent range of 32-bit floats), one sequence of 262,144 tokens.

Worked example

  1. KV cache per token per attention layer: 2 (K and V) × 2 heads × 256 × 2 bytes = 2,048 bytes.
  2. If all 48 layers were attention: 48 × 2,048 = 96 KiB per token, so 24 GiB for 262,144 tokens.
  3. With 12 attention layers: 24 KiB per token, so 6 GiB: a quarter, because only a quarter of the layers cache keys and values.
  4. The 36 DeltaNet layers each keep a state of 32 × 128 × 128 values: about 36 MiB in total in BF16, the same at 1K or 262K tokens.
Memory at long context is set by the attention layers; the linear layers add a small constant. (Our calculation from the published configuration.)
6 · Why it works

Why hybrids work

Most tokens need a summary

Much of language modelling needs a compressed running summary of the past, which a fixed state provides cheaply.

Some tokens need exact lookup

Copying, retrieval from documents and few-shot examples need attention; a few layers are enough to supply it.

Decoding is bound by memory

Cutting KV-cache memory 4 to 8 times raises the batch size and throughput at long context; Kimi Linear reports up to 6× decoding throughput at 1M tokens.

They train like transformers

Linear attention, SSD (Mamba-2) and Gated DeltaNet have parallel or chunkwise algorithms for GPUs (graphics processing units); the recurrence is only used when generating.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Mamba replaced transformers."

The released models in the table keep some full attention. At 8B, pure Mamba and Mamba-2 lagged transformers on tasks needing copying or in-context learning (Waleffe et al.).

"Linear attention is just a faster approximation of attention."

It changes what the model can do: its memory is a fixed-size summary, so exact recall of a distant token is no longer guaranteed.

"Recurrent layers cannot be trained in parallel."

Linear attention, Mamba-2 (SSD) and Gated DeltaNet all train with parallel or chunkwise algorithms; only generation runs step by step.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • State, not cache. A linear or SSM layer stores a fixed-size matrix per layer; an attention layer stores K and V for every token.
  • Count the attention layers. In a hybrid, KV-cache memory scales with the number of full-attention layers.
  • Recall is the hard part. Zoology: 82% of the perplexity gap between gated convolutions and attention came from associative recall.

⚠ Traps

  • "Attention-free" means "no attention needed". Pure SSMs lagged transformers on five-shot MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding), phonebook lookup and long-context reasoning at 8B (Waleffe et al.).
  • Assuming quadratic cost dominates. At inference the pain is KV-cache memory and bandwidth, which grow linearly per token, not the N² of training.
  • Comparing models without matching data. Architecture claims need the same data and token budget; Waleffe et al. trained all 8B variants on the same 3.5T tokens.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Swap most attention layers for fixed-size-state layers to make long context cheap, and keep a few attention layers so the model can still look things up exactly.
Say it in 30 seconds

Attention's KV cache grows with every token, which makes long contexts memory-bound. Linear attention, selective state space models (Mamba) and gated delta-rule layers keep a fixed-size state instead, so decode memory is constant per layer and throughput rises (Mamba reported 5×). The cost is exact recall: fixed-state models lag on copying, lookup and in-context learning. Hybrids fix this with a few full-attention layers: an 8B Mamba-2-Hybrid beat a matched transformer on 12 standard tasks, and Jamba, MiniMax-01, Nemotron-H, Qwen3-Next and Kimi Linear all ship hybrid stacks with one attention layer per 4 to 8 layers.

Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Transformers are RNNs: Fast Autoregressive Transformers with Linear Attention
Katharopoulos, Vyas, Pappas, Fleuret · ICML 2020

Expresses attention as a dot product of kernel feature maps; associativity reduces cost from O(N²) to O(N) and turns autoregressive attention into an RNN.

Remember: up to 4000× faster autoregressive prediction on very long sequences
Efficiently Modeling Long Sequences with Structured State Spaces (S4)
Gu, Goel, Ré · ICLR 2022

Structured state space sequence model with an efficient parameterisation; strong on long-range benchmarks.

Remember: linear scaling in sequence length
Mamba: Linear-Time Sequence Modeling with Selective State Spaces
Gu, Dao · arXiv 2023

SSM parameters become functions of the input (selection), with a hardware-aware parallel scan; an attention-free, MLP-free block.

Remember: 5× generation throughput; Mamba-3B matches transformers twice its size
Transformers are SSMs: Generalized Models and Efficient Algorithms Through Structured State Space Duality (Mamba-2)
Dao, Gu · ICML 2024

State space duality connects SSMs and attention through structured semiseparable matrices; Mamba-2's core layer is a faster selective SSM.

Remember: 2–8× faster than Mamba's layer
RWKV: Reinventing RNNs for the Transformer Era
Peng et al. · arXiv 2023

A linear-attention-style RNN that trains in parallel like a transformer and infers like an RNN.

Remember: models up to 14B parameters
Retentive Network: A Successor to Transformer for Large Language Models
Sun et al. · arXiv 2023

Retention supports parallel training, O(1) recurrent inference and chunkwise recurrence for long sequences.

Remember: O(1) inference per token
Gated Delta Networks: Improving Mamba2 with Delta Rule
Yang, Kautz, Hatamizadeh · ICLR 2025

Combines gating (fast memory erasure) with the delta rule (targeted updates) in a hardware-efficient parallel algorithm; hybrids with sliding-window attention or Mamba-2 improve further.

Remember: beats Mamba-2 and DeltaNet on LM (language model), retrieval and long context
Zoology: Measuring and Improving Recall in Efficient Language Models
Arora et al. · arXiv 2023

Gated-convolution models trail attention mainly on associative recall; multi-query associative recall (MQAR) captures it; sparse attention hybrids close most of the gap.

Remember: 82% of the gap from recall; 70M attention beats 1.4B gated convolution on recall; hybrids close 97.4%
Repeat After Me: Transformers are Better than State Space Models at Copying
Jelassi, Brandfonbrener, Kakade, Malach · arXiv 2024

A two-layer transformer can copy strings of exponential length; fixed-state models are fundamentally limited; empirical gaps on copying and retrieval.

Remember: copying needs state that grows with the string
An Empirical Study of Mamba-based Language Models
Waleffe et al. (NVIDIA) · arXiv 2024

8B Mamba, Mamba-2, transformer and hybrid models on the same 3.5T tokens: pure SSMs lag on copying and in-context learning; the hybrid wins.

Remember: Mamba-2-Hybrid (24 Mamba-2, 4 attention, 28 MLP): +2.65 points on 12 tasks, up to 8× faster generation (predicted)
Jamba: A Hybrid Transformer-Mamba Language Model
Lieber et al. (AI21) · arXiv 2024

Interleaves Transformer and Mamba layers with MoE; fits on a single 80GB GPU.

Remember: 1 : 7 attention-to-Mamba layers; up to 256K context
MiniMax-01: Scaling Foundation Models with Lightning Attention
MiniMax · arXiv 2025

Lightning (I/O-aware linear) attention with one softmax-attention block after every seven, plus MoE.

Remember: 456B total, 45.9B active; 1M-token training context, 4M at inference
Nemotron-H: A Family of Accurate and Efficient Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Models
NVIDIA · arXiv 2025

Replaces most self-attention layers with Mamba-2 layers (about 8% attention, evenly spaced); comparable accuracy to similarly sized transformers.

Remember: up to 3× faster inference; 4 of 52 layers are attention in 8B
Kimi Linear: An Expressive, Efficient Attention Architecture
Kimi Team · arXiv 2025

Kimi Delta Attention (finer-grained gating on Gated DeltaNet) interleaved 3:1 with MLA; outperforms full MLA under the same training recipe.

Remember: KV cache up to 75% smaller; up to 6× decoding throughput at 1M context
Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct model card
Qwen Team · Hugging Face 2025

Hybrid layout 12 × (3 × Gated DeltaNet + 1 × gated attention), each followed by a high-sparsity MoE; 80B total, 3B active.

Remember: 48 layers; 10× throughput beyond 32K tokens vs Qwen3-32B; 262,144-token native context
