Optimization basics

How gradient descent moves through a loss landscape, why curvature and the learning rate decide whether it converges, and what momentum and Adam fix.

What is the problem?We can compute gradients of the loss. Now we need to use them to find good weights, in a landscape with billions of dimensions, steep and flat directions, and saddle points.
Core ideaGradient descent steps against the gradient: w ← w − η∇L. On a quadratic it is stable only if η < 2/λ_max, and the flattest direction sets the speed, so ill-conditioning (κ = λ_max/λ_min) slows it. Momentum accumulates velocity; Adam rescales each coordinate by its recent gradient size.

2 · Limitation

Why plain gradient descent struggles

Ill-conditioning

The steep direction caps the learning rate at 2/λ_max; along the flat direction progress is then tiny.

Saddle points

High-dimensional losses have far more saddles than bad local minima, surrounded by slow plateaus.

Noisy gradients

Mini-batches give noisy estimates (SGD (stochastic gradient descent), stochastic gradient descent), so steps jitter.

3 · Your turn

What happens above the limit?

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

For f = ½·100·y², gradient descent uses η = 0.025. What happens to y?

C. Each step multiplies y by (1 − ηλ) = 1 − 2.5 = −1.5: sign flips and the size grows 1.5× per step. Stability needs |1 − ηλ| < 1, i.e. η < 0.02.
4 · Core idea

Three update rules

Gradient descent

w ← w − η∇L. Each eigen-direction shrinks by |1 − ηλᵢ| per step.

Momentum

v ← βv + ∇L, w ← w − ηv. Oscillations cancel across steps; consistent directions accelerate.

Adam

Running averages of g and g², bias-corrected; step ∝ m̂/(√v̂ + ε). Each coordinate gets its own effective rate.

stable iff η < 2 / λ_max · speed set by κ = λ_max / λ_min

At a glance

Learning rate × curvature decides stability; the condition number decides speed.

5 · How it works

Worked example: an ill-conditioned bowl

f(x, y) = ½(x² + 100·y²): curvatures 1 and 100, condition number κ = 100. Start at (1, 1). (Notebook 06.)

Worked example

  1. Stability: η must be below 2/λ_max = 2/100 = 0.02; at η = 0.021 the y-coordinate grows every step.
  2. At the best fixed rate η = 2/(1 + κ), plain gradient descent needs 346 steps to cut the loss by 10⁶.
  3. Momentum with tuned β needs 58 steps; at κ = 1,000 it is 3,454 vs 204.
  4. Adam with η = 0.05 converges at κ = 100 and at κ = 10,000 with the same settings, because it normalises each coordinate.
The steep direction limits the learning rate; the flat direction then limits progress. Momentum and adaptive methods attack exactly that gap.
6 · Why it works

Why it works despite non-convexity

Locally quadratic

Near any point the loss looks like a quadratic, so curvature analysis predicts stability and speed.

Saddles are escapable

Noise and momentum push off saddle points along negative-curvature directions.

Many good minima

Large networks have many equally good solutions; the job is reaching one efficiently.

Adaptivity helps

Adam's per-parameter scaling partly corrects ill-conditioning without computing the Hessian.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Deep learning gets stuck in bad local minima."

In high dimensions most critical points are saddles; Dauphin et al. argue plateaus around saddles are the real obstacle.

"A larger learning rate always trains faster."

Past 2/λ_max the steepest direction diverges; LLM (large language model) runs use warmup and schedules for this reason.

"Adam needs no tuning."

Its defaults are robust, but the learning rate, β₂ and warmup still matter at scale.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • η < 2/λ_max. Above it, GD (gradient descent) diverges along the steepest direction.
  • Momentum ≈ √κ speed-up. On quadratics (Goh, Distill).
  • Adam defaults: β₁ 0.9, β₂ 0.999, ε 10⁻⁸. LLMs often use β₂ ≈ 0.95.

⚠ Traps

  • Too high a learning rate. Loss spikes or NaNs (not a numbers); warm up the rate early in training.
  • Fearing local minima. In high dimensions saddle points and plateaus are the bigger problem (Dauphin et al.).
  • L2 penalty inside Adam. Not the same as weight decay; use decoupled AdamW.
8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Answer from memory. If you can't, revisit the slide named in the answer.

Curvatures 2 and 50. Largest stable learning rate for plain GD?

2/λ_max = 2/50 = 0.04.

What does the condition number κ control?

How slowly the flattest direction converges once the learning rate is limited by the steepest: GD needs ~κ steps per e-fold; momentum ~√κ.

What two running statistics does Adam keep, and why bias-correct them?

The mean of gradients (m) and of squared gradients (v); both start at 0 so early estimates are biased toward 0, corrected by dividing by 1 − βᵗ.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Gradient descent is stable only for η < 2/λ_max and is slowed by the condition number; momentum and Adam reduce that dependence, with warmup and schedules keeping big runs stable.
Say it in 30 seconds

Gradient descent steps against the gradient, scaled by a learning rate. Near any point the loss looks like a quadratic bowl, and along each direction the error is multiplied by one minus the learning rate times the curvature, so the step must stay below two over the largest curvature or training diverges. With that cap, the flattest direction moves slowly, so the ratio of largest to smallest curvature, the condition number, sets the speed. Momentum averages steps to cancel oscillations and roughly takes the square root of that ratio; Adam scales each coordinate by its own gradient size.

Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization
Kingma & Ba · ICLR 2015

Per-parameter step sizes from bias-corrected running averages of the gradient and its square; invariant to diagonal rescaling of the gradients.

Introduces the gated linear unit (GLU): one projection gates another through a sigmoid.

Goh · Distill 2017

Interactive analysis of momentum on quadratics: it damps oscillation across steep directions and gives a square-root speed-up in the condition number.
