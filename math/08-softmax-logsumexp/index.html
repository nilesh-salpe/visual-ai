Softmax and log-sum-exp

The function that turns scores into probabilities in every attention head and output layer, why it overflows, and the max-trick and online form that make it safe and fast.

What is the problem?Softmax needs exp of each score. exp overflows FP16 (16-bit floating point) above about 11 and FP32 (32-bit floating point) above about 88.7, and attention logits often exceed those. Computing it also seems to need two passes over the data.
Core ideaSoftmax is shift-invariant: subtract the maximum before exponentiating and every exponent is ≤ 0. Log-sum-exp LSE(z) = m + log Σ e^(zᵢ − m) gives stable log-probabilities. The online softmax keeps a running max and a rescaled running sum, so it works block by block in one pass.
1 · Problem

When does softmax break?

Three logits; move the largest one. The naive softmax (exp, then normalise) is computed in a simulated FP16 or FP32; the stable version subtracts the max first.

naive

stable (subtract max)

Logits are (z − 2, z − 1, z). exp overflows FP16 above ln 65,504 ≈ 11.09 and FP32 above ≈ 88.72.

2 · Limitation

What goes wrong naively

Overflow

exp of a large logit becomes ∞; ∞/∞ is NaN (not a number) and poisons the whole step.

Underflow in logs

log(softmax) of a very unlikely token is log(0) = −∞, making the loss infinite.

Two passes

The textbook form needs the max and the sum before any output, i.e. extra reads of a long row from memory.

Why can we subtract max(z) from every logit before the softmax?

B. exp(zᵢ − m) / Σ exp(zⱼ − m) = e^(−m) exp(zᵢ) / (e^(−m) Σ exp(zⱼ)): mathematically identical, numerically safe.
4 · Core idea

Three stable forms

Stable softmax

exp(z − m) / Σ exp(z − m), with m = max z. All exponents ≤ 0.

Log-sum-exp

LSE(z) = m + log Σ exp(z − m): the log normaliser, used directly in losses.

Online softmax

Stream blocks keeping (m, d); when a block raises the max, multiply d by e^(m_old − m_new) (Milakov & Gimelshein, 2018).

LSE(z) = m + log Σᵢ e^(zᵢ − m), log softmax(z)ᵢ = zᵢ − LSE(z)

At a glance

Online version: update m and the sum as blocks arrive, rescaling the old sum by e^(m_old − m_new).

5 · How it works

Online softmax, block by block

Twelve logits read in blocks of three. Step through: the running max m and running sum d update, and d is rescaled whenever m grows.

From online softmax to FlashAttention

FlashAttention applies the same rescaling to the attention output as well as the sum, so it can process keys and values in tiles held in fast on-chip memory without ever writing the full score matrix. See Architecture deck 09.

5 · How it works

Worked example: softmax of (1000, 1001, 1002)

Naively, e¹⁰⁰⁰ overflows to infinity in FP32 and FP64 (64-bit floating point), and the result is ∞/∞ = NaN (not a number).

Worked example

  1. Subtract m = 1002: (−2, −1, 0).
  2. Exponentiate: (0.1353, 0.3679, 1); sum = 1.5032.
  3. Divide: (0.090, 0.245, 0.665), the same as softmax(0, 1, 2).
  4. Online, in two blocks [1000] then [1001, 1002]: after block 1, m = 1000, d = 1. Block 2 raises m to 1002, so d = 1·e⁻² + e⁻¹ + 1 = 1.503: the same sum.
Shift by the max for safety; rescale the running sum when the max changes to do it in one pass.
6 · Why it works

Why it matters in practice

Exactness

The stable and online forms give the same answer as the textbook formula, only without overflow.

Fewer memory passes

Milakov & Gimelshein measured softmax up to 1.3× faster, and fused softmax + top-k up to 5× faster.

Enables long context

Tiled attention with online softmax never materialises the n × n score matrix.

Safe losses

Cross-entropy from logits via LSE stays finite even for probabilities like 10⁻³⁰⁰.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Subtracting the max changes the probabilities slightly."

It changes nothing mathematically; it only changes the floating-point path.

"Online softmax is an approximation used for speed."

It is exact; the rescaling exactly corrects for the earlier, smaller max.

"Softmax only appears at the output layer."

Every attention head applies a softmax to every query's scores.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • softmax(z + c) = softmax(z). The max-trick is free.
  • log softmax = z − LSE(z). Use for losses.
  • Online: d ← d·e^(m − m′) + Σ e^(z − m′). The basis of FlashAttention.

⚠ Traps

  • log(softmax(z)). Tiny probabilities underflow to 0 and give −∞.
  • Softmax in FP16. exp overflows above ≈ 11.09; compute in FP32.
  • Masking with −∞ on a fully masked row. Max is −∞ and you get NaN; handle empty rows explicitly.
8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Answer from memory. If you can't, revisit the slide named in the answer.

Above roughly which logit does exp overflow in FP16?

ln 65,504 ≈ 11.09. (88.72 is FP32, 709.8 is FP64.)

Running state (m = 3, d = 2.5). A new block has max 5. How is d updated?

d ← 2.5·e^(3 − 5) + Σ e^(z − 5) over the new block; m ← 5.

Why compute log-softmax as z − LSE(z) rather than log(softmax(z))?

Very small probabilities underflow to 0 in softmax, giving log 0 = −∞; z − LSE stays finite.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Subtract the max before exponentiating, use log-sum-exp for log-probabilities, and keep a rescaled running max and sum to compute softmax exactly in one pass.
Say it in 30 seconds

Softmax exponentiates scores and normalises them. Exponentials overflow quickly: above about 11 in FP16 and 89 in FP32. Because softmax does not change when you add a constant to every score, you subtract the maximum first and all exponents become non-positive. Log-sum-exp, the maximum plus the log of the shifted sum, gives stable log-probabilities for the loss. The online softmax processes the scores in blocks, keeping a running maximum and a running sum that is rescaled whenever the maximum grows; it is exact and is what lets FlashAttention work tile by tile.

Problem: exp overflows; naive softmax needs two passes.
Core idea: shift by max; LSE; online (m, d) with rescaling.
Payoff: stable losses, fused kernels, FlashAttention.
