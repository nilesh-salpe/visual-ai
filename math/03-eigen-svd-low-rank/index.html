Eigenvalues, SVD and low rank

How any matrix splits into a few important directions, why big weight matrices and their updates are often nearly low rank, and how LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) exploits it.

What is the problem?A 4,096 × 4,096 weight matrix has 16.8 million numbers. Fine-tuning all of them for every task is expensive, yet much of a matrix's action is often carried by a few directions.
Core ideaThe singular value decomposition (SVD) writes any matrix as W = UΣVᵀ, a sum of rank-1 pieces σᵢuᵢvᵢᵀ ordered by size. Keeping the top r gives the best rank-r approximation. LoRA learns a fine-tuning update directly in that low-rank form, BA.

Illustrative spectrum: true components decay slowly, noise adds a small flat floor.

2 · Limitation

Why not just store or train the full matrix?

Memory

Full fine-tuning stores gradients and Adam state for every weight: 16 bytes per parameter.

Many tasks, many copies

Each fine-tuned variant of a 7B model is another 14 GB of weights.

Most of it is redundant

Aghajanyan et al. found fine-tuning works in a tiny subspace: 200 parameters reached 90% of full fine-tuning on one task.

3 · Your turn

How big is a rank-r factorisation?

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

Storing an m × n matrix as a rank-r product (m × r)(r × n) takes how many numbers?

C. m·r + r·n. For m = n = 4,096 and r = 8, that is 65,536 instead of 16.8M.
4 · Core idea

SVD: rotate, stretch, rotate

Vᵀ

An orthonormal change of basis on the input: finds the input directions the matrix cares about.

Σ

Stretches each direction by a singular value σᵢ ≥ 0, sorted largest first.

U

Rotates the result into output directions.

W = U Σ Vᵀ = Σᵢ σᵢ uᵢ vᵢᵀ · best rank r: keep i ≤ r

At a glance

Eigenvectors are the special case for square symmetric matrices: W v = λ v.

5 · How it works

LoRA: learn the update in low-rank form

1 · freeze W

The pretrained weight stays fixed: no gradients or optimizer state for it.

2 · add BA

y = xW + x·(BA)·(α/r), with B (d × r) zero-initialised and A (r × k) random.

3 · train B, A

Only r(d + k) parameters get gradients and Adam state.

4 · merge or swap

W' = W + BA for deployment, or keep many small adapters for one base model.

Eigenvalues in one line

For a square matrix, an eigenvector keeps its direction: Wv = λv. For symmetric matrices the eigenvectors are orthogonal and the SVD and eigendecomposition coincide (up to signs). Eigenvalues of the loss Hessian set optimization speed (Optimization basics deck).

5 · How it works

Worked example: LoRA's parameter savings

Adapt one 4,096 × 4,096 projection with a rank-8 LoRA update ΔW = BA, B being 4,096 × 8 and A 8 × 4,096.

Worked example

  1. Full update: 4,096 × 4,096 = 16,777,216 parameters.
  2. LoRA: 8 × (4,096 + 4,096) = 65,536 parameters, 0.39% of the full update.
  3. B starts at zero, so at step 0 the model is exactly the pretrained one; after training BA can be merged into W at no inference cost.
  4. Notebook 02: a 256 × 256 matrix of true rank 16 plus noise is reconstructed to about 1% error at r = 16 using 12.5% of the storage.
If the important change is low rank, a few thousand times fewer trainable parameters suffice; the LoRA paper reports 10,000× fewer on GPT-3 175B.
6 · Why it works

Why low rank works so often

Optimal truncation

Eckart–Young: no rank-r matrix is closer to W than the truncated SVD.

Updates are structured

Task adaptation changes a few directions of a pretrained model's features; LoRA matched full fine-tuning quality with tiny ranks.

Smaller is cheaper

Fewer trainable parameters means less optimizer memory and tiny checkpoints.

Same tool, many uses

Compression, PCA (principal component analysis), conditioning analysis and low-rank KV (key-value) projections all use the SVD.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Low rank means the matrix has few non-zero entries."

Rank counts independent directions; a low-rank matrix can be completely dense.

"LoRA approximates the pretrained weights."

It leaves W untouched and constrains only the update ΔW to be low rank.

"Singular values are the same as eigenvalues."

Singular values are always ≥ 0 and exist for any shape; eigenvalues need a square matrix and can be negative or complex.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Rank-r storage = r(m + n). Instead of m·n.
  • Error² = sum of the discarded σᵢ². Eckart–Young.
  • Energy plot. Cumulative σ² shows how many directions matter.

⚠ Traps

  • Confusing eigenvalues with singular values. They coincide only for symmetric positive semi-definite matrices.
  • Assuming every matrix is low rank. Random matrices spread energy over all directions; pretrained updates are the ones that compress.
  • Merging LoRA before you are done. Merge for deployment; keep separate to swap adapters.

Why is B initialised to zero in LoRA?

So BA = 0 at the start and fine-tuning begins exactly at the pretrained model.

What is the error of the best rank-r approximation in Frobenius norm?

The square root of the sum of the squared discarded singular values, √(Σᵢ>ᵣ σᵢ²).
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

The SVD splits any matrix into ranked directions; keeping the top r is the best low-rank approximation, and LoRA trains updates in that form for a tiny fraction of the parameters.
Say it in 30 seconds

Any matrix can be written as a rotation, a stretch along sorted directions, and another rotation: the singular value decomposition. Its pieces are rank-one matrices weighted by singular values, and keeping the largest r gives the closest possible rank-r matrix, with squared error equal to the discarded singular values squared. Storing it costs r times m plus n numbers instead of m times n. LoRA uses this for fine-tuning: it freezes the pretrained weight and learns a low-rank update BA, with B starting at zero, cutting trainable parameters by orders of magnitude.

Problem: full matrices and updates are huge.
Core idea: W = UΣVᵀ; top-r truncation is optimal.
Use: LoRA: ΔW = BA with r(d + k) parameters.
