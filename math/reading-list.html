Math for LLMs: key papers

Every paper cited on the decks' Key papers slides, in the order the guide introduces them. Each entry links to the paper and to the decks that use it.

Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space (word2vec)
Mikolov et al. · ICLR workshop 2013 · cited in Vectors and dot products

Learns word vectors whose dot products and differences capture meaning (king − man + woman ≈ queen) with cheap log-linear models.

Remember: under a day to learn vectors from 1.6B words
Attention Is All You Need
Vaswani et al. · NeurIPS 2017 · cited in Vectors and dot products, Matrices and linear layers

The Transformer: attention-only encoder-decoder with post-norm residual blocks, sinusoidal positions and a ReLU (rectified linear unit) FFN (feed-forward network). The baseline every later change is measured against.

Remember: 28.4 BLEU (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy, an n-gram overlap score for translation) on WMT (Workshop on Machine Translation) 2014 English-German
Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3)
Brown et al. · NeurIPS 2020 · cited in Matrices and linear layers

Besides in-context learning, a systematic contamination study: 13-gram overlap flags and clean vs dirty scores.

Remember: 13-gram overlap; a filtering bug left some overlap in
Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models
Kaplan et al. · arXiv 2020 · cited in Matrices and linear layers

Loss falls as a power law in parameters, data and compute; also gives the ≈ 6·N·D training-compute rule of thumb.

Remember: trends spanning more than seven orders of magnitude
LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models
Hu et al. · ICLR 2022 · cited in Eigenvalues, SVD and low rank

Freezes the pretrained weights and trains a low-rank update BA (B initialized to zero) that can be merged after training.

Remember: 10,000× fewer trainable parameters, 3× less GPU (graphics processing unit) memory (GPT-3 175B)
Intrinsic Dimensionality Explains the Effectiveness of Language Model Fine-Tuning
Aghajanyan et al. · ACL 2021 · cited in Eigenvalues, SVD and low rank

Fine-tuning works in a tiny subspace: a low-dimensional random reparameterization is nearly as effective as the full parameter space, and larger models have lower intrinsic dimension.

Remember: 200 parameters reach 90% of full fine-tuning (RoBERTa, MRPC (Microsoft Research Paraphrase Corpus))
Learning representations by back-propagating errors
Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams · Nature 1986 · cited in Derivatives and the chain rule

Backpropagation: apply the chain rule backward through a network to get every weight's gradient, so hidden units learn useful internal features.

Automatic differentiation in machine learning: a survey
Baydin et al. · JMLR 2018 · cited in Derivatives and the chain rule

Forward and reverse-mode automatic differentiation explained; backpropagation is reverse mode, which gets all gradients of a scalar loss in about one extra pass.

A Neural Probabilistic Language Model
Bengio et al. · JMLR 2003 · cited in Probability and sampling, Maximum likelihood and cross-entropy

The first neural language model: learned word embeddings plus a network that outputs a softmax over the next word, trained by maximum likelihood.

The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration
Holtzman et al. · ICLR 2020 · cited in Probability and sampling

Maximizing likelihood when decoding gives bland, repetitive text; nucleus (top-p) sampling samples from the smallest set of tokens holding probability p.

Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners (GPT-2)
Radford et al. · OpenAI 2019 · cited in Probability and sampling, Maximum likelihood and cross-entropy

Decoder-only language model that moved layer normalization to the input of each sub-block (pre-norm) and added a final norm.

Remember: context 1,024 tokens, vocabulary 50,257
A Mathematical Theory of Communication
Shannon · Bell System Technical Journal 1948 · cited in Maximum likelihood and cross-entropy, Information theory for LLMs

Defines entropy as average information and shows it is the limit of lossless compression; the root of cross-entropy loss and bits-per-token.

On Information and Sufficiency
Kullback & Leibler · Annals of Mathematical Statistics 1951 · cited in Information theory for LLMs

Introduces the divergence now called KL: the expected extra information needed when using one distribution in place of another.

Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network
Hinton, Vinyals & Dean · NeurIPS workshop 2015 · cited in Information theory for LLMs

Train a small model on a large model's softened output distribution (softmax with temperature T), minimizing cross-entropy to those soft targets.

Online normalizer calculation for softmax
Milakov & Gimelshein · arXiv 2018 · cited in Softmax and log-sum-exp

Computes the softmax normalizer in a single pass with a running max and a rescaled running sum, the trick FlashAttention builds on.

Remember: up to 1.3× faster softmax
FlashAttention: Fast and Memory-Efficient Exact Attention with IO-Awareness (IO = input/output, memory reads and writes)
Dao et al. · NeurIPS 2022 · cited in Softmax and log-sum-exp

Tiles attention so scores never leave on-chip SRAM (static random-access memory, fast on-chip memory), using an online softmax and recomputation in the backward pass. Exact, with memory linear in sequence length.

Remember: GPT-2 medium: 40.3 → 4.4 GB of memory traffic, 41.7 → 7.3 ms
Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization
Kingma & Ba · ICLR 2015 · cited in Optimization basics

Per-parameter step sizes from bias-corrected running averages of the gradient and its square; invariant to diagonal rescaling of the gradients.

Remember: defaults β₁ 0.9, β₂ 0.999, ε 10⁻⁸
Language Modeling with Gated Convolutional Networks
Dauphin et al. · ICML 2017 · cited in Optimization basics

Introduces the gated linear unit (GLU): one projection gates another through a sigmoid.

Why Momentum Really Works
Goh · Distill 2017 · cited in Optimization basics

Interactive analysis of momentum on quadratics: it damps oscillation across steep directions and gives a square-root speed-up in the condition number.

What Every Computer Scientist Should Know About Floating-Point Arithmetic
Goldberg · ACM Computing Surveys 1991 · cited in Floating-point numbers

The classic guide to IEEE floating point: rounding, ulps, relative error, cancellation and why (a + b) + c ≠ a + (b + c).

A Study of BFLOAT16 for Deep Learning Training
Kalamkar et al. · arXiv 2019 · cited in Floating-point numbers

BF16 keeps FP32's 8-bit exponent (same range) with a 7-bit mantissa, and trains image, speech, language and recommendation models without hyper-parameter changes.

Mixed Precision Training
Micikevicius et al. · ICLR 2018 · cited in Floating-point numbers

Half-precision weights, activations and gradients with an FP32 master copy of the weights and loss scaling to avoid gradient underflow.

Remember: nearly 2× less memory
