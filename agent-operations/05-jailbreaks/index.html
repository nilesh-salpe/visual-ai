Jailbreaks and adversarial robustness

How users get a model to do what its safety training forbids: why jailbreaks work, the main automated attack families (optimised suffixes, attacker LLMs (large language models), many-shot, best-of-N), and the layered defences used in production: safety training, input and output classifiers, monitoring and red teaming.

What is the problem?Safety training teaches a model to refuse harmful requests, but attackers rephrase, encode, role-play, pack hundreds of fake dialogues into the context, or simply retry random variations until one gets through. Every deployed model faces this, and agents with tools raise the stakes.
Core ideaTreat robustness as a property of the whole system, not of the model alone. Jailbreaks exploit competing objectives (being helpful vs being safe) and mismatched generalization (safety training did not cover a format the model still understands). Defend in layers: a safety-trained model, input and output classifiers trained from a written policy, monitoring across requests and accounts, and continuous red teaming. Measure attack success at a stated attacker budget, together with the false-refusal rate on real traffic.

1 · Problem

Refusals can be talked around

Learned, not enforced

Wei et al.: attacks designed from two failure modes succeeded on every prompt in a curated set of unsafe requests against GPT-4 and Claude v1.3, and on over 96% of all prompts evaluated (2023).

Automated attacks

GCG (Greedy Coordinate Gradient) finds an adversarial suffix by gradient-guided search on open models that transferred to GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 (up to 84%) and PaLM-2 (66%), much less to Claude (2.1%). PAIR (Prompt Automatic Iterative Refinement) uses an attacker LLM and often needs fewer than 20 queries.

Retries add up

Best-of-N: resample random variations of a prompt (shuffled, re-capitalised) until one works: 89% on GPT-4o and 78% on Claude 3.5 Sonnet with 10,000 samples, with success following a power law in N.

2 · Core idea

Why jailbreaks work, and what each family exploits

Attack family Published example What it exploits Main defence
Role-play and persuasion PAIR (attacker LLM refines a scenario) competing objectives: helpfulness vs safety safety training; output classifier
Encoding and obfuscation Base64 inputs; random capitalisation (Best-of-N) mismatched generalization: the model understands a format safety training missed classifiers that judge meaning, not surface form
Optimised suffixes GCG brittle refusal behaviour under adversarial tokens output classifier; adversarial training
Many-shot hundreds of faux dialogues in one prompt (up to 256 tested) in-context learning, which grows with context length classifying and modifying the prompt (61% → 2% success in one case)
Multi-turn decomposition splitting a task into individually harmless steps checks that look at one request at a time conversation- and account-level monitoring

A jailbreak is the user attacking the model's policy. Prompt injection is third-party content (a web page, a tool result) hijacking an agent; it is covered in Safety and reliability. OWASP (Open Worldwide Application Security Project) lists both under prompt injection (LLM01).

5 · Trade-offs

Trade-offs

Defence Strength Cost
Safety training (refusals) no extra latency; handles most plain requests can be argued around; too much causes over-refusal
Input and output classifiers Current practice robust to many styles; retrain quickly; output check catches harmless-looking prompts compute overhead (23.7% in the paper); false positives (+0.38% refusals)
Adversarial training on known attacks closes specific holes attack-specific; may not generalise to new attacks
Circuit breakers (representation rerouting) Research targets the internal representations of harmful output, attack-agnostic research-stage; Best-of-N still circumvented them
Account monitoring and rate limits catches retries and multi-turn attacks needs identity; privacy and false-alarm handling
Restricting risky features removes an attack surface (for example very long faux-dialogue prompts) can block legitimate uses
6 · Failure modes

Failure modes

Failure Symptom Mitigation
Universal jailbreak shared publicly one template works across many harmful requests bug bounties, threat intelligence, fast classifier updates
Single-attempt evaluation reported robustness collapses under retries report attack success at a stated budget N; use HarmBench or JailbreakBench plus your own policy
Over-refusal legitimate users blocked; complaints rise measure false refusals on real traffic alongside attack success
Obfuscated inputs encodings and odd formatting slip past filters classify meaning; check outputs, not only inputs
Harm split across turns each turn looks harmless conversation-level classifiers; account monitoring
Jailbroken agent with tools harmful actions, not just text least-privilege tools, approvals for risky actions (Safety and reliability deck)
7 · Challenges in production

In production

Defence in depth

No single layer is enough: Anthropic's AI (artificial intelligence) Safety Level 3 (ASL-3) deployment combines real-time constitutional classifiers, a bug bounty, offline monitoring and threat intelligence.

Write the policy first

Classifiers, red-team tasks and evals all derive from a written statement of what is allowed and what is not.

Measure both errors

Attack success at a budget, and false refusals on real traffic; a defence that blocks users is not a win.

Iterate fast

Classifiers can be retrained in days from new red-team findings; model retraining takes much longer.

Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Jailbroken: How Does LLM Safety Training Fail?
Wei, Haghtalab, Steinhardt · arXiv 2023

Two failure modes of safety training, competing objectives and mismatched generalization, explain jailbreaks and guide new ones (e.g. prefix injection, Base64).

Remember: new attacks succeeded on 100% of a curated set and over 96% of evaluated prompts (GPT-4, Claude v1.3)
Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models (GCG)
Zou, Wang, Carlini, Nasr, Kolter, Fredrikson · arXiv 2023

Greedy Coordinate Gradient search for an adversarial suffix that maximises the probability of an affirmative answer; suffixes transfer to black-box models.

Remember: up to 84% on GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, 66% PaLM-2, 2.1% Claude
Jailbreaking Black Box Large Language Models in Twenty Queries (PAIR)
Chao et al. · arXiv 2023

An attacker LLM iteratively refines a semantic jailbreak against a target using only black-box access.

Remember: often fewer than 20 queries
Many-shot jailbreaking
Anil et al. (Anthropic) · Anthropic research 2024

Hundreds of faux dialogues in a long prompt jailbreak models; effectiveness follows the same power law as benign in-context learning.

Remember: up to 256 shots tested; a prompt-classification mitigation cut success from 61% to 2% in one case
Best-of-N Jailbreaking
Hughes, Price, Lynch et al. · arXiv 2024

Repeatedly sample augmented variations of a prompt (shuffling, capitalisation; image and audio augmentations) until one succeeds; success follows power-law-like scaling in N.

Remember: 89% GPT-4o, 78% Claude 3.5 Sonnet with 10,000 samples
Constitutional Classifiers: Defending against Universal Jailbreaks across Thousands of Hours of Red Teaming
Sharma et al. (Anthropic) · arXiv 2025

Input and streaming output classifiers trained on synthetic data generated from a constitution of permitted and restricted content.

Remember: 3,000+ hours of red teaming, no universal jailbreak; automated success 86% → <5%; +0.38% refusals; 23.7% overhead
Activating AI Safety Level 3 protections
Anthropic · Anthropic news 2025

Deployment with Claude Opus 4: real-time Constitutional Classifiers on inputs and outputs, a bug bounty, offline monitoring and threat intelligence, iterated over time.

Remember: activated May 22, 2025
Llama Guard: LLM-based Input-Output Safeguard for Human-AI Conversations
Inan et al. (Meta) · arXiv 2023

An instruction-tuned Llama 2 7B that classifies both prompts and responses against a safety risk taxonomy.

Remember: prompt and response classification with one model
Improving Alignment and Robustness with Circuit Breakers
Zou et al. · arXiv 2024

Representation rerouting interrupts the internal representations responsible for harmful outputs, as an alternative to refusal and adversarial training.

Remember: attack-agnostic; also applied to agents
HarmBench: A Standardized Evaluation Framework for Automated Red Teaming and Robust Refusal
Mazeika et al. · arXiv 2024

Standardised evaluation of automated red-teaming methods and defences.

Remember: 18 red-teaming methods compared
JailbreakBench: An Open Robustness Benchmark for Jailbreaking Large Language Models
Chao et al. · NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks 2024

Open benchmark with a dataset of misuse behaviours, a leaderboard and a repository of jailbreak artifacts for reproducible evaluation.

Remember: standard threat model and scoring
OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025
OWASP GenAI Security Project · genai.owasp.org 2025

LLM06:2025 Excessive Agency: root causes are excessive functionality, excessive permissions and excessive autonomy; mitigations include minimising extensions and their permissions, executing in the user's context, user approval and complete mediation.
