Experimentation for LLM and agent products

Shipping a new model, prompt or tool safely: from offline evals to shadow traffic to online controlled experiments, with guardrail metrics, honest statistics (no peeking), sample-ratio checks and variance reduction.

What is the problem?A change that wins offline can still lower real task success, raise cost or latency, or annoy users: offline sets miss real traffic. And most ideas do not work: at Bing, "less than a third move the metrics they were designed to improve" when tested in controlled experiments.
Core ideaMove every change up a ladder: offline evals, then shadow traffic (run on real requests, show nothing), then an online controlled experiment. Randomise users into arms, fix one primary metric (e.g. task success) and guardrails (cost per task, latency, safety, escalations), decide the sample size in advance or use always-valid sequential tests, check for sample ratio mismatch, reduce variance with pre-experiment data (CUPED (Controlled-experiment Using Pre-Experiment Data)), then ramp up. These are proven web-experimentation methods applied to LLM (large language model) products.

1 · Problem

Offline wins are not online wins

Offline ≠ online

Eval sets are fixed and curated; real traffic has new intents, longer contexts and adversarial users.

Most ideas fail

At Bing, less than a third of ideas moved the metrics they were designed to improve; teams that skip experiments ship the other two thirds.

Small effects, noisy outputs

Real gains are often a point or two, and model outputs and costs vary from request to request.

2 · Core idea

Climb a ladder of evidence

Offline evals

Graded task suites and pass^k (Evaluating agents deck): cheap, repeatable, catch regressions before users see them.

Shadow traffic

Run the new version on copies of real requests and compare outputs, cost and latency without showing them to users.

Online controlled experiment

Randomly assign users to control or treatment; compare a pre-chosen primary metric and guardrails.

Ramp and hold out

Increase exposure stepwise (e.g. 1% → 10% → 50%); keep a small long-term holdout to measure lasting effects.

5 · Trade-offs

Trade-offs

Choice Benefit Cost
Randomise by user consistent experience; independent units fewer units than requests; slower to reach significance
Randomise by request many units fast users see both versions; carry-over effects in conversations
Fixed-horizon test simple, standard statistics no early stopping without inflating errors
Always-valid sequential test monitor continuously and stop early safely slightly less power at a fixed size
Shadow traffic first catches cost and latency surprises with zero user risk cannot measure user behaviour or satisfaction
LLM-judge metrics online scores every conversation judge bias; validate against human labels
6 · Failure modes

Failure modes

Failure Symptom Mitigation
Peeking many "wins" that vanish on re-test fixed sample size or always-valid inference
Sample ratio mismatch arms differ in size; results look too good or bad SRM check on every analysis; find the root cause before reading metrics
Proxy metric moves, goal does not thumbs-up rises while task completion falls a primary metric tied to the real outcome; guardrails
Model version drift a provider updates the model mid-test pin model versions and prompts in the experiment config
Novelty effects early lift fades over weeks run long enough; long-term holdouts
Interference between arms shared caches, rate limits or memory leak effects across arms separate caches and quotas per arm
7 · Challenges in production

In production

Config, not code

Model, prompt, tools and routing live in versioned config that the assignment service selects per arm.

Many experiments at once

Bing ran over 200 concurrent experiments on any given day; that needs a platform for assignment, logging and analysis.

Cost is a metric

Track tokens and cost per completed task in every experiment, not only quality.

Review samples

Read real transcripts from both arms: numbers say what changed, transcripts say why.

Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Online Controlled Experiments at Large Scale
Kohavi et al. · KDD 2013

Lessons from running online controlled experiments at Bing scale; many ideas move key metrics by about 1%, so experiments must be sensitive and trustworthy.

Remember: over 200 concurrent experiments on any given day, each with millions of users
Large Scale Experimentation at Bing
Bing Search Quality Insights · Bing Blogs 2013

Harry Shum (Bing R&D): when ideas are evaluated in controlled experiments, "less than a third move the metrics they were designed to improve".

Improving the Sensitivity of Online Controlled Experiments by Utilizing Pre-Experiment Data
Deng, Xu, Kohavi & Walker · WSDM 2013

CUPED: use each unit's pre-experiment metric as a covariate to remove explainable variance (a factor 1 − ρ² for correlation ρ).

Remember: about 50% variance reduction at Bing, i.e. the same power with half the users or half the duration
Always Valid Inference: Bringing Sequential Analysis to A/B Testing
Johari, Pekelis & Walsh · Operations Research 2022

Fixed-horizon p-values are unreliable when users continuously monitor tests and stop when they like; always-valid p-values and confidence intervals stay valid whenever the decision is made. Deployed on a large commercial A/B testing platform.

Diagnosing Sample Ratio Mismatch in Online Controlled Experiments: A Taxonomy and Rules of Thumb for Practitioners
Fabijan et al. · KDD 2019

An SRM is when the observed sample ratio differs from the expected one; like a fever it is a symptom of many data-quality issues. Taxonomy from four companies and over 25 products.

Large-Scale Validation and Analysis of Interleaved Search Evaluation
Chapelle, Joachims, Radlinski & Yue · ACM TOIS 2012

Interleaving merges two rankings into one list shown to the user and credits clicks to each, a paired online comparison; validated with Yahoo!, Bing and arXiv search data against judgments and other online metrics.

Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations
Miller · arXiv 2024

Standard errors from the central limit theorem, clustered standard errors, paired comparisons and power analysis for evals.

Remember: clustered SEs can be over 3× naive ones
