RLAIF and Constitutional AI

Using a model, guided by a written set of principles, to produce the feedback that humans used to provide: critique-and-revise for SFT, and AI preference labels for RL.

What is the problem?Human preference labels are slow and expensive, and for harmful requests they require people to read and judge harmful content. The rules the model is meant to follow are also implicit in thousands of individual labels.
Core ideaWrite the rules down as a constitution. In a supervised stage, the model critiques and revises its own answers against principles; in an RL stage, an AI model compares pairs of answers against principles, and RL uses a preference model trained on those AI labels (RLAIF).
1 · Problem

Feedback is the bottleneck

Scale

Every RLHF round needs new comparisons on the latest model's outputs. Human labelling limits how often and how widely that can happen.

Exposure

Labelling harmlessness means people reading and ranking answers to harmful requests.

Opacity

The intended behaviour lives implicitly in many individual judgements. Nobody can read off "the rules" the model was trained on.

2 · Limitation

The harmless-but-evasive trap

An easy way to be "harmless" is to refuse anything borderline. That is unhelpful and teaches users nothing. The Constitutional AI paper's goal was a "harmless but non-evasive" assistant that "engages with harmful queries by explaining its objections to them".

Why plain preference labels push towards evasion

If labelers judging harmlessness in isolation tend to prefer a refusal over any risky-looking answer, the reward model learns that refusing scores well, and RL amplifies it. Writing explicit principles that ask for explanations instead of bare refusals is one way to steer away from that.

4 · Core idea

Two stages

Supervised stage (SL-CAI)

Sample responses to harmful (red-team) prompts from a helpful-only RLHF model; ask it to critique its own response against a randomly chosen principle, then revise it; repeat. Fine-tune the original model on the revised responses.

RL stage (RL-CAI, i.e. RLAIF)

Sample pairs of responses from the SL-CAI model; ask a model which one better satisfies a principle; train a preference model on these AI labels; run RL against it, exactly as in RLHF. The paper wrote 16 principles and sampled one at random for each comparison label.

Chain of thought helps the judge

The paper reports that "both the SL and RL methods can leverage chain-of-thought style reasoning to improve the human-judged performance and transparency of AI decision making": asking the feedback model to reason before choosing makes its labels better and easier to inspect.

6 · Why it works

Why it works, and what to watch

Judging is easier than generating

A capable model can often tell which of two answers better follows a stated principle even if its own first answer didn't.

Scalable and inspectable

AI labels are cheap to regenerate for every new model, and the principles are written down, so the target behaviour can be read, debated and changed.

The feedback model's limits carry over

If the judging model misreads a principle or has blind spots, those errors become training signal, so AI feedback still needs human evaluation.

Widely reused idea

Model-generated judgements now appear throughout post-training: rejection sampling with reward models, synthetic SFT data, and LLM-as-judge evaluation (Evals topic).

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"RLAIF removes humans from alignment."

Humans write and choose the principles, and the paper still used human feedback for helpfulness. What changes is who produces each individual label.

"Constitutional AI is just a system prompt with rules."

The principles are used to generate training data: revised responses for SFT and AI preference labels for a preference model and RL. The behaviour is trained into the weights.

"Being harmless means refusing more."

The paper aimed for "harmless but non-evasive": engaging with the request and explaining objections rather than refusing blindly.

Describe the two stages of Constitutional AI.

SL-CAI: the model critiques and revises its own responses against sampled principles, and is fine-tuned on the revisions. RL-CAI: a model labels which of two responses better follows a principle; a preference model is trained on those AI labels and used for RL (RLAIF).

Which part of the Constitutional AI pipeline still used human preference labels?

Helpfulness. AI feedback replaced human labels for harmlessness.

Why might AI feedback make the target behaviour more transparent?

The behaviour is specified by a short written list of principles that anyone can read, and chain-of-thought judgements show why each label was chosen, instead of being implicit in thousands of individual human labels.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Constitutional AI replaces human harmlessness labels with AI feedback guided by written principles: self-critique and revision for SFT, AI preference labels for RL.
