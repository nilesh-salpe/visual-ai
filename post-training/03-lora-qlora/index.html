Post-training · 03

LoRA and QLoRA

Fine-tune by training a tiny low-rank update instead of every weight, and keep the frozen base model in 4-bit NormalFloat to fit large models on one GPU (graphics processing unit).

What is the problem?Full fine-tuning stores gradients and optimizer state for every parameter and produces a full model copy per task: for an 8B model that is roughly 100 GB of training memory and 16 GB per checkpoint.
Core ideaLoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation): freeze W₀ and learn ΔW = BA of rank r (B starts at 0), then merge after training. QLoRA (Quantized Low-Rank Adaptation): store the frozen base in 4-bit NF4 (4-bit NormalFloat), dequantize on the fly, train LoRA adapters in 16-bit.
1 · Problem

Where the memory goes in full fine-tuning

Rough training memory: BF16 (bfloat16, a 16-bit floating-point format with the exponent range of 32-bit floats) weights (2 bytes per parameter), FP32 (32-bit floating point) gradients (4), and Adam's two FP32 moments (8). Activations are extra. LoRA keeps the last three only for its adapters; QLoRA also shrinks the frozen weights to about 4 bits.

Estimates from parameter counts, ignoring activations. QLoRA reports 65B fine-tuning going from more than 780 GB to under 48 GB of GPU memory.

2 · Limitation

The cost of updating everything

Optimizer state for every weight

Adam keeps two extra numbers per parameter, plus a gradient. That state is several times the size of the model itself.

One full copy per task

Ten fine-tuned variants of an 8B model are ten 16 GB checkpoints to store and to serve separately.

Adapters add latency

Earlier parameter-efficient methods inserted extra layers ("adapters") that stay in the forward pass at inference time.

4 · Core idea

Low-rank updates

Freeze, then add BA

h = W₀x + (α/r)·BAx. W₀ is frozen. A gets a random Gaussian init and B starts at zero, "so ΔW = BA is zero at the beginning of training": you start exactly at the pretrained model.

Choose where and how big

The paper mostly adapts only W_q and W_v, and finds very small ranks (even r = 1–4) work well there. Many practitioners adapt all linear layers.

Merge for free inference

After training, W = W₀ + (α/r)BA is one ordinary matrix: "no additional inference latency". You can also keep W₀ shared and swap small adapters per task.

Reported results

On GPT-3 175B, LoRA "can reduce the number of trainable parameters by 10,000 times and the GPU memory requirement by 3 times", and performs on par or better than full fine-tuning in model quality on RoBERTa, DeBERTa, GPT-2 and GPT-3, with higher training throughput. The α/r scale means changing r doesn't require retuning the learning rate much; the paper simply sets α to the first r tried.

At a glance

No extra layer at inference once merged; or keep one base and swap small adapters.

5 · How it works

QLoRA: a 4-bit frozen base

The 16 NF4 levels (from the QLoRA paper) vs 16 evenly spaced INT4 (4-bit integer) levels, drawn over a normal distribution like the one pretrained weights follow. NF4 puts more levels where most weights are.

NF4 levelsINT4 levels (uniform)normal density

The three QLoRA ingredients

  • NF4: blockwise (64 weights per block) absmax-scaled 4-bit codes at normal quantiles. Notebook 03 measures lower error than uniform INT4 on Gaussian weights.
  • Double quantization: quantize the per-block scales too, cutting their overhead from 0.5 to 0.127 bits per parameter, about 3 GB for a 65B model.
  • Paged optimizers: page optimizer state between GPU and CPU (central processing unit) memory to survive memory spikes.

Weights are dequantized to BF16 for each matmul; gradients flow through them to the 16-bit LoRA adapters. The paper: 4-bit QLoRA with NF4 "matches 16-bit full finetuning and 16-bit LoRA finetuning performance on academic benchmarks".

5 · How it works

Worked example: LoRA on Llama 3 8B

Rank r = 8 on W_q (4,096 × 4,096) and W_v (4,096 × 1,024) in all 32 layers.

Worked example

  1. Per layer: 8 × (4,096 + 4,096) + 8 × (4,096 + 1,024) = 106,496 parameters.
  2. Total trainable: 3,407,872 = 0.042% of 8.03B.
  3. Adapter file in BF16: 6.5 MiB vs a 16 GB full checkpoint.
  4. QLoRA: base in NF4 ≈ 8.03B × 0.5 bytes = 4.0 GB + double-quantized scales (0.13 GB).
Train a few million parameters, store megabytes per task, and fit the frozen base in a quarter of its BF16 size.
6 · Why it works

Why a low rank is enough

Updates have low intrinsic rank

The LoRA paper's hypothesis, supported by its experiments: the change needed to adapt a pretrained model lives in a small subspace, even though the weights themselves are full rank.

Notebook evidence

Fitting a rank-4 update: ranks 1–3 plateau at clear error, rank ≥ 4 fits essentially perfectly. A rank above the true rank adds nothing.

The trade-off

Low rank limits what can change. For large shifts (new languages, lots of new knowledge), higher ranks, more target matrices, or full fine-tuning can do better.

Serving many variants

Because adapters are tiny, one base model can serve many fine-tunes by swapping or batching adapters, instead of loading many full copies.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"LoRA makes inference slower."

Merge W₀ + (α/r)BA after training and the model is exactly as fast as before. That is a main advantage over adapter layers.

"QLoRA trains in 4-bit."

Only the frozen base weights are stored in 4-bit. They are dequantized to BF16 for compute, and the LoRA adapters and their gradients are 16-bit.

"A bigger rank is always better."

Past the rank the task needs, extra rank only adds parameters; the paper found very small ranks sufficient for W_q and W_v.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Params per matrix = r·(d_in + d_out). Tiny compared with d_in·d_out.
  • Scale α/r. Set α to the first r you try; changing r then needs little retuning.
  • QLoRA overhead. 4 bits + 0.127 bits per parameter with double quantization.

⚠ Traps

  • Forgetting to merge. Unmerged adapters add a small extra matmul per layer at inference.
  • "QLoRA trains in 4-bit." Only the frozen base is 4-bit; compute is BF16 and adapters are 16-bit.
  • Rank as a free win. Beyond the needed rank, extra rank only adds parameters.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

LoRA trains a rank-r update BA beside a frozen W₀ and merges it afterwards; QLoRA keeps the frozen base in 4-bit NF4 so large models fine-tune on a single GPU.
Say it in 30 seconds

Full fine-tuning needs gradients and Adam state for every parameter and a full model copy per task. LoRA freezes the pretrained weights and learns a low-rank update, B times A, starting from B equal to zero so training begins at the pretrained model. Because adaptation updates have low intrinsic rank, a small r works, with 10,000 times fewer trainable parameters on GPT-3, and after training the update merges into the weight with no inference cost. QLoRA additionally stores the frozen base in 4-bit NormalFloat, quantizes the scales again, and pages optimizer state, which brings 65 billion parameter fine-tuning from over 780 gigabytes to under 48.

Problem: full fine-tuning memory ≈ 14 bytes/param plus a full copy per task.
LoRA: W₀ + (α/r)BA, B = 0 at init, merge after: tiny trainable set, no latency.
QLoRA: NF4 frozen base + double quantization + paged optimizers; 65B on one 48 GB GPU.
