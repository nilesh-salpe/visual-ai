RLHF with PPO

Optimizing a language model against a reward model: the KL-penalized objective, PPO's clipped update, the four models in memory, and the "alignment tax".

What is the problem?We have a reward model. We want the policy to produce higher-scoring answers, but naive policy gradients are unstable, and maximizing an imperfect reward model without limits produces garbage that scores well.
Core ideaMaximize r(x, y) − β·KL(π ‖ π_SFT) with PPO: sample answers, score them, and take several small, clipped policy-gradient steps per batch so no update moves the policy too far.
1 · Problem

Four models at once

A PPO step for an LLM involves four networks. Two are trained, and two are frozen but must still run a forward pass on every sample. For a 7B policy that is a lot of memory before activations.

2 · Limitation

What goes wrong with plain policy gradients

High variance

One scalar reward for a whole answer of hundreds of tokens gives a noisy gradient. A baseline (value function) is needed to tell "better than expected" from "worse".

Steps that are too big

A single large update can collapse the policy (repeating one phrase, say), and on-policy data from the old policy no longer describes the new one.

Unlimited optimization

The reward model is wrong somewhere. Without a constraint, the policy finds those spots: odd phrasing, excessive length, flattery.

3 · Your turn

What does the KL term do?

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

RLHF maximizes r(x,y) − β·log(π_RL(y|x)/π_SFT(y|x)). What happens as β grows large?

B. The penalty term, averaged over samples, is β·KL(π‖π_SFT). A large β makes drifting from the SFT model expensive, trading reward for staying where the reward model is trustworthy. The exact optimum is π* ∝ π_SFT·exp(r/β).
4 · Core idea

The objective and the update

What is maximized

InstructGPT eq. 2: E[ rθ(x,y) − β·log(πRL(y|x)/πSFT(y|x)) ] + γ·Epretrain[log πRL(x)]. The KL penalty is applied per token; the γ term (PPO-ptx) mixes in pretraining gradients.

How it is maximized (PPO)

LCLIP = E[ min(ρ·Â, clip(ρ, 1−ε, 1+ε)·Â) ], with ρ the new/old probability ratio and ε = 0.2 in the PPO paper. Several epochs of minibatch updates per batch of samples.

Advantages for text

The reward model scores the full answer once. The per-token KL penalty gives every token a small reward too. A value model (InstructGPT: initialized from the RM) predicts the expected future reward at each token, and the advantage Â is how much better the actual outcome was than predicted (usually with generalized advantage estimation). GRPO (deck 08) replaces this value model with the average reward of a group of samples.

5 · How it works

PPO's clip, visualized

The contribution of one sample to LCLIP as the policy changes its probability (ratio ρ = π_new/π_old). For a good sample (Â > 0) the gain stops growing once ρ > 1+ε; for a bad one (Â < 0) the gain from pushing it down stops once ρ < 1−ε. Flat regions have zero gradient.

6 · Why it works

Why RLHF with PPO worked

Learns from its own samples

Online RL scores what the current policy actually writes, so it fixes the model's own failure modes, not just copies demonstrations.

Stable enough at scale

Clipping plus the KL leash keep updates small. InstructGPT, Llama 2-Chat (PPO after rejection-sampling rounds) and many others used it.

The alignment tax, and PPO-ptx

InstructGPT saw regressions on some public NLP datasets after RLHF. Mixing pretraining gradients into PPO (PPO-ptx) greatly reduced them "without compromising labeler preference scores".

The costs

Four models, generation inside the training loop, many sensitive hyperparameters (β, ε, learning rates, GAE settings). Hence the search for simpler methods (decks 07–08).

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"PPO simply maximizes the reward model's score."

It maximizes reward minus β·KL to the SFT model. Without that term the policy would exploit the reward model.

"The value model and the reward model are the same thing."

The RM scores complete answers. The value model predicts expected future reward at each token to compute advantages. InstructGPT initializes the value model from the RM, but trains it separately.

"Clipping limits the size of the gradient."

It removes the incentive to move a sample's probability ratio beyond 1 ± ε in the helpful direction. Gradients from samples in the flat region are zero; others are unchanged.
8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Answer from memory. If you can't, revisit the slide named in the answer.

Which four models does a typical PPO-based RLHF step use?

Policy (trained), reference/SFT (frozen, for KL), reward model (frozen), value model (trained).

What is the closed-form optimum of E[r] − β·KL(π‖π_ref)?

π*(y) ∝ π_ref(y)·exp(r(y)/β). Higher reward answers are up-weighted exponentially, more strongly the smaller β is. DPO is built on this formula.

What problem does PPO-ptx address?

The alignment tax: RLHF degraded some benchmark performance. Mixing pretraining log-likelihood gradients into the update (γ > 0) reduced the regressions while keeping preference scores.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

RLHF maximizes reward-model score minus β·KL to the SFT model, using PPO's clipped updates to stay stable, at the cost of four models and much tuning.
Say it in 30 seconds

Given a reward model, RLHF samples answers from the current policy, scores them, and updates the policy to raise the score while paying a per-token KL penalty for drifting from the SFT model, because the reward model is only trustworthy near its training data. PPO makes the update stable by clipping the probability ratio between new and old policy, so several gradient steps per batch can't move any answer's probability too far. A value model supplies per-token advantages. The exact optimum of reward minus beta KL is the reference policy times exp of reward over beta. InstructGPT also mixed in pretraining gradients to reduce the alignment tax.
