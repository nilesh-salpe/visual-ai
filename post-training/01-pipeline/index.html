Post-training · 01

The post-training pipeline

How a model that only predicts internet text becomes an assistant: demonstrations (SFT), preferences (reward models, RLHF, DPO) and verifiable rewards (RLVR), and how real labs combine them.

What is the problem?Pretraining optimizes "predict the next token of internet text". That makes a model knowledgeable, not helpful: it continues prompts rather than following them, and it has no notion of which answer people prefer.
Core ideaFine-tune the pretrained model in stages. SFT imitates good demonstrations. Preference optimization (RLHF with a reward model, or DPO) pushes towards answers people prefer. RL with verifiable rewards sharpens tasks whose answers can be checked.
1 · Problem

Same knowledge, different behaviour

One prompt, three stages of the same model. The outputs are illustrative (written for this deck), but the pattern is the one InstructGPT documented: a base model continues text, an SFT model answers, and preference tuning makes the answer what people actually want.

2 · Limitation

Why pretraining alone isn't enough

Wrong objective

The pretraining loss rewards predicting what text comes next on the web, which includes unhelpful, wrong and toxic text. InstructGPT calls this objective "misaligned" with following instructions helpfully and safely.

Demonstrations are expensive

Writing an ideal answer for every kind of request takes expert time. InstructGPT's SFT set had about 13k prompts; Llama 2 stopped at 27,540 high-quality annotations.

Imitation has a ceiling

SFT can only copy its demonstrations. To prefer answers better than any single demonstrator would write, you need a signal that compares answers, which is much cheaper than writing them.

4 · Core idea

The three InstructGPT steps

1 · SFT

Labelers write demonstrations of the desired output for prompts; fine-tune the pretrained model on them with ordinary next-token loss. (~13k training prompts.)

2 · Reward model

Sample several outputs per prompt, labelers rank them (K = 4 to 9), and train a model to output a scalar score that agrees with the rankings. (~33k prompts.)

3 · RL (PPO)

Optimize the SFT model to maximize the reward model's score with PPO, with a KL penalty that keeps it close to the SFT model. (~31k prompts.)

Where the idea came from

Christiano et al. (2017) showed RL from human comparisons could teach Atari and robotics behaviours with feedback on less than 1% of the agent's interactions, and about an hour of human time for some novel behaviours. Stiennon et al. (2020) applied it to summarization; InstructGPT applied it to general instruction following.

5 · How it works

How real recipes combine the pieces

ModelPost-training recipe (as reported)
InstructGPT (2022)SFT → reward model → PPO, with a pretraining-loss mix ("PPO-ptx") to reduce regressions
Llama 2-Chat (2023)SFT on 27,540 annotations → two reward models (helpfulness, safety) on over 1M comparisons → five RLHF rounds: rejection-sampling fine-tuning only up to V4, then PPO on top
Llama 3 (2024)Several rounds (six, following Llama 2) of reward modelling → SFT on rejection-sampled data → DPO
Tülu 3 (2024)SFT → DPO → RL with verifiable rewards (the paper that named RLVR)
DeepSeek-R1 (2025)R1-Zero: RL with rule-based rewards directly on the base model, no SFT. R1: a "multi-stage learning framework that integrates rejection sampling, reinforcement learning, and supervised finetuning", starting from cold-start long chain-of-thought data

The common pattern

Every recipe starts with SFT to teach the format, then uses comparisons or checks on the model's own outputs to improve beyond the demonstrations, and repeats in rounds so new preference data comes from the current model. The choice of optimizer (PPO, rejection sampling, DPO, GRPO) is mostly about cost, stability and what signal is available.

6 · Why it works

Why preferences work so well

Judging is easier than writing

A labeler can reliably pick the better of two answers even when they couldn't write either. Comparisons are cheaper and more consistent than absolute scores.

Feedback on the model's own outputs

SFT learns from someone else's text. Preference and RL methods learn from what this model actually produces, including its own mistakes.

Knowledge comes from pretraining

Post-training is small compared with pretraining (thousands to millions of examples vs trillions of tokens). It mostly selects and shapes behaviour the base model can already produce.

The cost: an "alignment tax"

InstructGPT saw RLHF reduce performance on some public NLP benchmarks, and mixing pretraining gradients into PPO (PPO-ptx) largely fixed it without hurting preference scores.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Post-training is where the model learns its knowledge."

Knowledge comes overwhelmingly from pretraining on trillions of tokens. Post-training uses far less data and mainly changes behaviour: format, helpfulness, refusals, reasoning style.

"RLHF means humans give a reward to every answer during training."

Humans label a fixed set of comparisons once per round. A reward model trained on them scores the millions of samples seen during RL.

"There is one standard recipe."

InstructGPT used PPO, Llama 3 used rejection sampling + DPO, DeepSeek-R1-Zero used RL with rule-based rewards and no SFT. The building blocks are shared; the mix varies.
8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Answer from memory. If you can't, revisit the slide named in the answer.

In InstructGPT, what do labelers provide for the reward model?

Rankings of K = 4–9 outputs, converted into pairwise comparisons.

Why is a KL penalty to the SFT model added during RL?

The reward model is an imperfect proxy. Without a leash, the policy drifts into outputs where the reward model is wrong (reward hacking). The KL term keeps it near the SFT model, where the reward model was trained.

Name the three InstructGPT steps and what each trains.

SFT trains the policy on demonstrations; reward modelling trains a scalar scorer on rankings; PPO trains the policy to maximize that score minus a KL penalty.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Post-training turns a next-token predictor into an assistant: imitate demonstrations (SFT), then optimize towards what people prefer or what can be verified, repeating in rounds.
Say it in 30 seconds

A pretrained model knows a lot but just continues text. Post-training fixes behaviour in stages. Supervised fine-tuning imitates written demonstrations and teaches the chat format. Then preference optimization uses comparisons, which are much cheaper than demonstrations: either train a reward model and optimize against it with RL and a KL penalty, as InstructGPT did, or optimize the same objective directly with DPO. For maths and code, a verifier can replace the reward model. InstructGPT showed a 1.3 billion parameter model after this process was preferred to the 175 billion parameter base GPT-3.
