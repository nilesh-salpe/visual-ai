Post-training LLMs: key papers

Every paper cited on the decks' Key papers slides, in the order the guide introduces them. Each entry links to the paper and to the decks that use it.

Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback (InstructGPT)
Ouyang et al. · NeurIPS 2022 · cited in The post-training pipeline, Supervised fine-tuning, Reward models, RLHF with PPO, Reward hacking and overoptimization, RLAIF and Constitutional AI

SFT on demonstrations, a reward model from rankings, then PPO with a per-token KL penalty and a pretraining mix (PPO-ptx).

Remember: 1.3B InstructGPT preferred over 175B GPT-3
Deep reinforcement learning from human preferences
Christiano et al. · NeurIPS 2017 · cited in The post-training pipeline, Reward models

Learns a reward model from human comparisons of behaviour clips and trains RL against it.

Remember: feedback on <1% of interactions; ~1 hour of human time
Learning to summarize from human feedback
Stiennon et al. · NeurIPS 2020 · cited in The post-training pipeline, RLHF with PPO, Reward hacking and overoptimization

RLHF for summarization: reward model from human comparisons, PPO with a KL penalty; also gives the analytic KL of best-of-n.

Remember: KL_bon = log n − (n−1)/n
The Llama 3 Herd of Models
Llama Team, AI (artificial intelligence) @ Meta · arXiv 2024 · cited in The post-training pipeline

Dense Transformer with GQA (8 KV (key-value) heads), RoPE (rotary position embedding) base 500,000, a 128K vocabulary and a document mask; staged long-context pretraining to 128K; post-training with SFT, rejection sampling and DPO in six rounds.

Remember: 128K context after six stages over ~800B tokens
Llama 2: Open Foundation and Fine-Tuned Chat Models
Touvron et al. · arXiv 2023 · cited in Supervised fine-tuning, Reward models, DPO, KTO and rejection sampling

Adds grouped-query attention for the 34B and 70B models and documents the chat post-training: SFT, two reward models and iterative RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) with rejection sampling and PPO.

Remember: 27,540 SFT annotations; over 1M human comparisons
LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models
Hu et al. · ICLR 2022 · cited in LoRA and QLoRA

Freezes the pretrained weights and trains a low-rank update BA (B initialized to zero) that can be merged after training.

Remember: 10,000× fewer trainable parameters, 3× less GPU memory (GPT-3 175B)
QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs
Dettmers et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · cited in LoRA and QLoRA

Fine-tunes LoRA adapters through a frozen 4-bit NormalFloat base, with double quantization and paged optimizers.

Remember: 65B on one 48 GB GPU; Guanaco 99.3% of ChatGPT on Vicuna
Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms
Schulman et al. · arXiv 2017 · cited in RLHF with PPO

A clipped surrogate objective that allows several gradient steps per batch while keeping the new policy close to the old one.

Remember: ε = 0.2
Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization
Gao, Schulman & Hilton · ICML 2023 · cited in Reward hacking and overoptimization

Uses a "gold" reward model as ground truth to measure how true reward changes as a policy optimizes a proxy RM.

Remember: best-of-n: d(α − βd); RL: d(α − β log d), d = √KL
Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model
Rafailov et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · cited in DPO, KTO and rejection sampling

Shows the KL-regularized RLHF objective can be optimized with a classification loss on preference pairs, with no reward model or RL loop.

Remember: implicit reward β·log(π/π_ref)
KTO: Model Alignment as Prospect Theoretic Optimization
Ethayarajh et al. · ICML 2024 · cited in DPO, KTO and rejection sampling

Alignment from a binary desirable/undesirable signal per output, using a prospect-theory utility instead of pairwise preferences.

Remember: matches or exceeds DPO from 1B to 30B
DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models
Shao et al. · arXiv 2024 · cited in RLVR and GRPO

Introduces GRPO: PPO without a critic, using the mean and std of rewards within a group of samples as the baseline.

Remember: MATH 46.8% → 51.7%, GSM8K (Grade School Math 8K) 82.9% → 88.2%
DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs (large language models) via Reinforcement Learning
DeepSeek-AI · arXiv 2025 · cited in RLVR and GRPO, Knowledge distillation

R1-Zero: RL with rule-based accuracy and format rewards directly on a base model; R1 adds cold-start data and a multi-stage pipeline.

Remember: AIME 2024 pass@1 15.6% → 77.9% (R1-Zero)
Tülu 3: Pushing Frontiers in Open Language Model Post-Training
Lambert et al. · arXiv 2024 · cited in RLVR and GRPO

An open post-training recipe (SFT → DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) → RL) that names Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback
Bai et al. · arXiv 2022 · cited in RLAIF and Constitutional AI

Self-critique and revision against written principles for SFT, then RL from AI preference labels (RLAIF) for harmlessness.

Remember: 16 principles, one sampled per comparison label
Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network
Hinton, Vinyals & Dean · NeurIPS workshop 2015 · cited in Knowledge distillation

Train a small model on a large model's softened output distribution (softmax with temperature T), minimizing cross-entropy to those soft targets.

DistilBERT, a distilled version of BERT: smaller, faster, cheaper and lighter
Sanh, Debut, Chaumond, Wolf · NeurIPS EMC² workshop 2019 · cited in Knowledge distillation

Distillation during pretraining with a triple loss (soft targets, masked language modelling, cosine of hidden states).

Remember: 40% smaller, 60% faster, 97% of BERT's language understanding
Sequence-Level Knowledge Distillation
Kim, Rush · EMNLP 2016 · cited in Knowledge distillation

Train the student on the teacher's beam-search outputs instead of matching per-token distributions; removes the need for beam search at test time.

Remember: student 10× faster than its teacher; 13× fewer parameters with pruning, −0.4 BLEU
MiniLLM: On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models
Gu, Dong, Wei, Huang · ICLR 2024 · cited in Knowledge distillation

Replaces forward KL with reverse KL, so the student does not overestimate the teacher's low-probability regions, optimised with an on-policy policy-gradient method.

Remember: students from 120M to 13B parameters
On-Policy Distillation of Language Models: Learning from Self-Generated Mistakes (GKD)
Agarwal et al. · ICLR 2024 · cited in Knowledge distillation

Generalized Knowledge Distillation: train the student on its own sampled sequences with teacher feedback, with a choice of divergence; addresses the train-inference distribution mismatch.

Remember: summarisation, translation and GSM8K (Grade School Math 8K) with T5 students
Gemma 2: Improving Open Language Models at a Practical Size
Gemma Team, Google DeepMind · arXiv 2024 · cited in Knowledge distillation

The 2B and 9B models are trained with knowledge distillation from a larger teacher instead of next-token prediction.

Remember: 2B on 500B tokens: 67.7 distilled vs 60.3 from scratch (3-benchmark average)
Llama 3.2: Revolutionizing edge AI (artificial intelligence) and vision with open, customizable models
Meta AI · Meta AI blog 2024 · cited in Knowledge distillation

The 1B and 3B models were pruned from Llama 3.1 8B and trained with logits from Llama 3.1 8B and 70B as token-level targets during pretraining.

Remember: teachers: Llama 3.1 8B and 70B
Compact Language Models via Pruning and Knowledge Distillation (Minitron)
Muralidharan et al. (NVIDIA) · arXiv (NVIDIA) 2024 · cited in Knowledge distillation

Prune depth, width, attention and MLP (multilayer perceptron) of Nemotron-4 15B, then retrain with distillation on under 3% of the original data.

Remember: up to 40× fewer training tokens per model; up to 16% better MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding) than from scratch
Qwen3 Technical Report
Qwen Team · arXiv 2025 · cited in Knowledge distillation

Small models are post-trained with strong-to-weak distillation: off-policy distillation from teacher outputs, then on-policy logit distillation to Qwen3-32B or Qwen3-235B-A22B.

Remember: about 1/10 of the GPU hours of the four-stage pipeline
On-Policy Distillation
Lu and Thinking Machines Lab · Thinking Machines (Connectionism) 2025 · cited in Knowledge distillation

The student samples, the teacher scores each token, and the student minimises per-token reverse KL; replicates Qwen3's results for a fraction of the cost of RL.

Remember: Qwen3-8B-Base: 70% AIME 2024; 9–30× cheaper than extending off-policy SFT
Commercial Terms of Service
Anthropic · anthropic.com 2026 · cited in Knowledge distillation

Use restrictions (D.4): customers may not access the services to build a competing product or service, including to train competing AI models.

Remember: section D.4
