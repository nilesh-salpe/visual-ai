Pretraining Data · 08

PII and safety filtering

Removing personal information and harmful content from training data: regex masking of emails, IP (Internet Protocol) addresses and phone numbers, domain blocklists, toxicity classifiers, and their trade-offs.

What is the problem?Web text contains people's emails, phone numbers and addresses, plus adult, violent and hateful content. Models can memorise and reproduce such text.
Core ideaUse cheap, high-precision tools at scale: URL/domain blocklists for adult and unsafe sites, regular expressions to mask PII (personally identifiable information) such as emails, IP addresses and phone numbers, and classifiers for toxicity, removing documents where problems are dense.
1 · Problem

Mask the PII

Edit the text. Regexes for emails, IPv4 (Internet Protocol version 4) addresses and phone numbers mark spans; with 5 or fewer spans they are masked (Dolma's rule), otherwise the whole document is dropped.

Illustrative patterns, simpler than production ones. All example values are fictional.

2 · Limitation

Why this is hard at scale

Volume

Model-based PII detectors are too slow for trillions of tokens (Dolma).

Ambiguity

A number may be a phone, a product code or a date; a name may be public or private.

Definitions

There is no single definition of toxicity or quality; annotators and tools disagree.

4 · Core idea

Layers of filtering

Domain level

Blocklists of adult, gambling and malicious domains; drop domains dense in PII or unsafe content (RefinedWeb, Llama 3).

Span level

Regex-detect high-precision PII (emails, IPs, phones) and replace with typed placeholder tokens.

Document level

Classifiers for toxicity and adult content; remove documents above a threshold or with dense PII.

At a glance

Precision matters: false positives delete good data, false negatives leak PII.

5 · How it works

Worked example: Dolma's PII policy

Dolma (Soldaini et al., 2024) found model-based PII detectors too slow for 3T tokens and used regular expressions for three high-precision types: email addresses, IP addresses and phone numbers.

Worked example

  1. Documents with 5 or fewer PII spans: each span is replaced with a special token such as |||EMAIL_ADDRESS|||. This affected 0.02% of documents.
  2. Documents with more spans (dense PII, e.g. directories) are removed entirely: 0.001% of documents.
  3. In ablations, masking versus removal made no measurable difference to model performance.
  4. Llama 3 additionally removed whole domains with high volumes of PII or unsafe content, and domains known to contain adult content.
Simple, precise rules applied at scale remove most obvious PII at almost no cost in data.
6 · Why it works

Why layered, simple filters work

Precision first

Regex for well-structured PII rarely deletes good text.

Cheap to run

Domain lists and regexes cost almost nothing per document.

Small data loss

Dolma's PII policy touched only 0.02% (masked) and 0.001% (removed) of documents.

Not sufficient alone

Dedup reduces memorisation; post-training and output filters add further safeguards.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Removing PII from training data makes models private."

It reduces exposure, but unstructured PII (names, addresses in prose) is hard to detect; memorisation evaluation is still needed.

"Toxicity filters only remove bad content."

They also remove some benign text, disproportionately from some groups; thresholds involve trade-offs.

"Masking PII hurts model quality."

Dolma found no measurable effect of masking versus removal on performance.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Mask, don't just delete. Keeps the surrounding useful text.
  • Filter domains first. Cheap and catches whole problem sites (RefinedWeb: 4.6M-domain blocklist).
  • Measure removal rates. Report what fraction each filter removes.

⚠ Traps

  • Assuming regex finds all PII. Names and addresses are hard; regex targets high-precision types only.
  • Toxicity filters are biased. They can disproportionately remove dialects and minority voices (Dolma, citing prior work).
  • Filtering only at training time. Memorisation of rare strings can still occur; dedup and evaluation for leakage also matter.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Remove unsafe and personal data in layers: domain blocklists, regex masking of high-precision PII (emails, IPs, phones), dropping PII-dense documents, and toxicity classifiers, at tiny data cost.
Say it in 30 seconds

Web text contains personal information and harmful content that models can memorise. Pipelines filter in layers. Whole domains are removed with blocklists of adult or malicious sites and, for Llama 3, domains dense in personal data. Regular expressions find high-precision personal data such as email addresses, IP addresses and phone numbers; Dolma masks them with placeholder tokens when a document has five or fewer and removes the document otherwise, touching only a few hundredths of a percent of documents. Toxicity classifiers remove the most harmful text, though they can be biased against some groups.

Problem: PII and harmful content in web text.
Core idea: domains → regex masking → document drop → classifiers.

Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Dolma: an Open Corpus of Three Trillion Tokens for Language Model Pretraining Research
Soldaini et al. · ACL 2024

Open 3T-token corpus and toolkit: Gopher rules plus C4's punctuation rule, toxicity classifiers, regex-based PII masking (emails, IP addresses, phone numbers) and dedup.

Remember: ~200 TB raw → 11 TB
The Llama 3 Herd of Models
Llama Team, AI (artificial intelligence) @ Meta · arXiv 2024

Dense Transformer with GQA (8 KV (key-value) heads), RoPE (rotary position embedding) base 500,000, a 128K vocabulary and a document mask; staged long-context pretraining to 128K; post-training with SFT (supervised fine-tuning), rejection sampling and DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) in six rounds.

Remember: 128K context after six stages over ~800B tokens
The RefinedWeb Dataset for Falcon LLM (large language model): Outperforming Curated Corpora with Web Data, and Web Data Only
Penedo et al. · NeurIPS 2023

Shows filtered, heavily deduplicated web data alone can beat curated corpora; trafilatura extraction, Gopher-style rules, strict MinHash plus exact-substring dedup.

Remember: 5T tokens; ~90% of documents removed; 600B released
Deduplicating Training Data Makes Language Models Better
Lee et al. · ACL 2022

Exact-substring (suffix array) and MinHash near-duplicate removal; deduplicated models memorize less, train in fewer steps and have less train-test overlap.

Remember: one 61-word sentence repeated 60,000+ times in C4; 10× less memorized output
