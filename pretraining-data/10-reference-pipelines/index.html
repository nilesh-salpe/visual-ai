Reference pipelines

Seven published pipelines side by side, from C4 to Llama 3: what each one does at every stage, and the lessons the field converged on.

What is the problem?Each paper describes its own pipeline with different names and orderings. To design or evaluate one, you need a common map of the choices.
Core ideaNearly every pipeline follows the same skeleton: extract → language ID (identifier) → heuristic filters → dedup → (model-based filter) → safety/PII (personally identifiable information) → mix. They differ in extraction (WET (plain text extracted from Web ARChive records) vs WARC (Web ARChive format)), how aggressive dedup is, and whether a learned quality filter is used. The trend: learned filters and careful dedup, validated by small-model ablations.

2 · Limitation

Why comparing pipelines is hard

Different names

The same step is called "content filtering", "URL filtering" or "safety filtering".

Different orders

Some dedup before filtering, others after; some dedup per snapshot, others globally.

Few ablations

Until recently, most choices were not tested individually.

4 · Core idea

One skeleton, many choices

Extraction

WET (C4, CCNet) → WARC + trafilatura (RefinedWeb, FineWeb, DCLM) → custom parsers (Llama 3).

Filtering

Rules (C4, Gopher) → + perplexity (CCNet) → + learned classifiers (DCLM, FineWeb-Edu, Llama 3).

Deduplication

Spans (C4) → MinHash (Gopher, RefinedWeb, FineWeb) + URL and line levels (Llama 3).

Validation

Small-model ablations per decision (FineWeb, DCLM) instead of intuition.

At a glance

Choices are validated by training small models and comparing benchmarks.

5 · How it works

Lessons the field converged on

Extract from WARC

Modern extractors beat Common Crawl's WET text (FineWeb ablation).

Deduplicate carefully

Strict near-dup removal helps; global cross-snapshot dedup can hurt (FineWeb).

Learn what "good" means

A cheap classifier trained on carefully chosen positives gives the largest gains (DCLM, FineWeb-Edu).

Mix deliberately

Domain weights and late annealing on high-quality data matter (Llama 3).

Scrub and respect consent

PII masking, safety filters and robots.txt compliance are part of the pipeline.

Measure everything

Report kept fractions and ablate each stage with small models.

5 · How it works

Worked example: what changed from C4 to FineWeb-Edu

The same source (Common Crawl), four years of pipeline evolution.

Worked example

  1. C4 (2019 crawl): WET text, line and page rules, three-sentence-span dedup, English ≥ 0.99: about 750 GB of text.
  2. RefinedWeb (2023): WARC + trafilatura, Gopher-style rules, strict MinHash and exact-substring dedup: 5T tokens, beats curated corpora.
  3. FineWeb (2024): 96 snapshots, per-snapshot MinHash, ablated C4 filters: 15T tokens; FineWeb-Edu adds an LLM-labelled (LLM = large language model) classifier: 1.3T tokens.
  4. DCLM (2024): a fastText quality classifier as the key ingredient: a 7B model reaches 64% MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding) on 2.6T tokens.
Scale grew 20× while selection became more aggressive: the best datasets are both bigger and more selective.
6 · Why it works

Why open pipelines matter

Reproducible science

Open datasets (RefinedWeb, FineWeb, DCLM, Dolma) let others test data choices directly.

Data beats tricks

DCLM-Baseline matched Mistral-7B-v0.3 and Llama 3 8B on MMLU (63% and 66%) with 6.6× less compute than Llama 3 8B.

Transfer to new domains

The same skeleton is reused for code, math and other languages with domain-specific rules.

Accountability

Documented filters make biases and removals inspectable.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Frontier labs use secret magic data."

Published pipelines show the same stages; details and scale differ, and open datasets now rival closed ones on many benchmarks.

"The best pipeline keeps the most data."

FineWeb-Edu kept under 10% of FineWeb and scored much higher on knowledge benchmarks.

"Pipelines are finished once."

Each new crawl, language or domain needs re-tuned filters and fresh ablations.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Same skeleton everywhere. Learn the stages, then compare choices.
  • Ablate with small models. FineWeb and DCLM decide each filter by training ~1B models.
  • Report removal rates. Each stage's kept fraction makes pipelines comparable.

⚠ Traps

  • Copying a pipeline without its ablations. Thresholds tuned for English web may fail for code or other languages.
  • Benchmark contamination. Classifiers or filters that favour test-like text inflate scores.
  • Ignoring consent trends. Re-crawls may now be restricted by robots.txt (deck 04).

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Published pipelines share one skeleton (extract, filter, dedup, scrub, mix); the field converged on WARC extraction, careful MinHash dedup and learned quality classifiers, all validated by small-model ablations.
Say it in 30 seconds

C4, Gopher, RefinedWeb, FineWeb, DCLM, Dolma and Llama 3 all follow the same skeleton: extract text, identify the language, apply heuristic filters, deduplicate, often apply a learned quality filter, scrub personal and unsafe content, and mix. They differ in the details: newer pipelines extract from raw WARC files with tools such as trafilatura, deduplicate with MinHash at several levels while avoiding over-aggressive global deduplication, and select data with cheap classifiers trained on carefully chosen examples. Crucially, FineWeb and DCLM validated each choice by training small models, which is how data work became a measurable science.

Core idea: one skeleton; compare choices per stage.
Lessons: WARC extraction, careful dedup, learned filters, ablations.

Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer (T5)
Raffel et al. · JMLR 2020

Systematic study of encoder-decoder pretraining with a span-corruption objective, framing every task as text-to-text.

Remember: corrupts 15% of tokens
Scaling Language Models: Methods, Analysis & Insights from Training Gopher
Rae et al. · arXiv 2021

MassiveText pipeline: content filtering, text extraction, simple quality rules (word count, word length, symbols, stop words), repetition removal, MinHash deduplication and test-set filtering.

Remember: near-duplicate if 13-gram Jaccard > 0.8
The RefinedWeb Dataset for Falcon LLM: Outperforming Curated Corpora with Web Data, and Web Data Only
Penedo et al. · NeurIPS 2023

Shows filtered, heavily deduplicated web data alone can beat curated corpora; trafilatura extraction, Gopher-style rules, strict MinHash plus exact-substring dedup.

Remember: 5T tokens; ~90% of documents removed; 600B released
The FineWeb Datasets: Decanting the Web for the Finest Text Data at Scale
Penedo et al. · NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks 2024

15T tokens from 96 Common Crawl snapshots with fully ablated choices: WARC + trafilatura over WET, per-snapshot (not global) MinHash dedup, selected C4 filters; FineWeb-Edu uses an LLM-annotated educational-quality classifier.

Remember: 15T tokens; FineWeb-Edu 1.3T
DataComp-LM: In search of the next generation of training sets for language models
Li et al. · NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks 2024

A 240T-token Common Crawl pool and controlled benchmark for data curation; model-based filtering with a fastText classifier (instruction data + ELI5 (Explain Like I'm Five) as positives) works best.

Remember: 7B model, 64% MMLU with 2.6T tokens
Dolma: an Open Corpus of Three Trillion Tokens for Language Model Pretraining Research
Soldaini et al. · ACL 2024

Open 3T-token corpus and toolkit: Gopher rules plus C4's punctuation rule, toxicity classifiers, regex-based PII masking (emails, IP (Internet Protocol) addresses, phone numbers) and dedup.

Remember: ~200 TB raw → 11 TB
The Llama 3 Herd of Models
Llama Team, AI (artificial intelligence) @ Meta · arXiv 2024

Dense Transformer with GQA (8 KV (key-value) heads), RoPE (rotary position embedding) base 500,000, a 128K vocabulary and a document mask; staged long-context pretraining to 128K; post-training with SFT (supervised fine-tuning), rejection sampling and DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) in six rounds.

Remember: 128K context after six stages over ~800B tokens
