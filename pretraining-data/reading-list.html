Web Crawling and Pretraining Data: key papers

Every paper cited on the decks' Key papers slides, in the order the guide introduces them. Each entry links to the paper and to the decks that use it.

Common Crawl: Overview
Common Crawl Foundation · commoncrawl.org 2026 · cited in Where pretraining data comes from, Crawling at scale, robots.txt and consent

Free, open web crawl released as WARC (raw responses), WAT (metadata) and WET (extracted text) files; the starting point of most open pretraining corpora.

Remember: over 300 billion pages; 3–5 billion new pages a month
The RefinedWeb Dataset for Falcon LLM: Outperforming Curated Corpora with Web Data, and Web Data Only
Penedo et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · cited in Where pretraining data comes from, Deduplication, PII and safety filtering, Reference pipelines

Shows filtered, heavily deduplicated web data alone can beat curated corpora; trafilatura extraction, Gopher-style rules, strict MinHash plus exact-substring dedup.

Remember: 5T tokens; ~90% of documents removed; 600B released
The Llama 3 Herd of Models
Llama Team, AI @ Meta · arXiv 2024 · cited in Where pretraining data comes from, Text extraction, PII and safety filtering, Data mixtures and repetition, Reference pipelines

Dense Transformer with GQA (8 KV (key-value) heads), RoPE (rotary position embedding) base 500,000, a 128K vocabulary and a document mask; staged long-context pretraining to 128K; post-training with SFT (supervised fine-tuning), rejection sampling and DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) in six rounds.

Remember: 128K context after six stages over ~800B tokens
Dolma: an Open Corpus of Three Trillion Tokens for Language Model Pretraining Research
Soldaini et al. · ACL 2024 · cited in Where pretraining data comes from, PII and safety filtering, Reference pipelines

Open 3T-token corpus and toolkit: Gopher rules plus C4's punctuation rule, toxicity classifiers, regex-based PII masking (emails, IP (Internet Protocol) addresses, phone numbers) and dedup.

Remember: ~200 TB raw → 11 TB
High-Performance Web Crawling (Mercator)
Najork & Heydon · Compaq SRC (Systems Research Center) Research Report 173 2001 · cited in How a web crawler works

Mercator's URL frontier: front-end priority queues, back-end per-host FIFO queues and a heap of next-contact times for politeness; content-seen and URL-seen tests with fingerprints.

Remember: wait 10× the last download time before re-contacting a host
The Anatomy of a Large-Scale Hypertextual Web Search Engine
Brin & Page · WWW (World Wide Web (conference)) 1998 · cited in How a web crawler works

The original Google: a URL server feeding distributed crawlers, a store server and an indexer; DNS caching was a major crawler bottleneck.

Remember: 4 crawlers × ~300 connections, 100+ pages/s
RFC 9309: Robots Exclusion Protocol
Koster, Illyes, Zeller & Sassman · IETF (Internet Engineering Task Force) 2022 · cited in How a web crawler works, robots.txt and consent

Standardizes robots.txt: user-agent groups, allow/disallow with * and $, longest match wins (allow on ties), 4xx means crawl allowed, 5xx means assume complete disallow.

Remember: cache ≤ 24 h; parse ≥ 500 KiB
IRLbot: Scaling to 6 Billion Pages and Beyond
Lee, Leonard, Wang & Loguinov · WWW (World Wide Web (conference)) 2008 · cited in Crawling at scale

Single-server crawler design for billions of pages: disk-based URL uniqueness checks, budget-based crawling to resist spam farms, and per-host/per-server rate limits.

Remember: 6.3B pages in 41 days, 1,789 pages/s
Detecting Near-Duplicates for Web Crawling
Manku, Jain & Das Sarma · WWW 2007 · cited in Crawling at scale

Uses 64-bit simhash fingerprints, where near-duplicates differ in few bits, and fast Hamming-distance search to deduplicate a web-scale crawl.

Remember: 8B pages, 64-bit fingerprints, k = 3 bits
Consent in Crisis: The Rapid Decline of the AI Data Commons
Longpre et al. · NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks 2024 · cited in robots.txt and consent

Audits robots.txt and terms of service of the sources behind C4, RefinedWeb and Dolma; restrictions on AI crawlers grew sharply in one year.

Remember: ~5%+ of all C4 tokens and ~25%+ of critical sources newly restricted (2023–24)
Trafilatura: A Web Scraping Library and Command-Line Tool for Text Discovery and Extraction
Barbaresi · ACL (demo) 2021 · cited in Text extraction

Open-source main-text extraction from HTML (boilerplate removal, metadata), used by RefinedWeb and FineWeb.

The FineWeb Datasets: Decanting the Web for the Finest Text Data at Scale
Penedo et al. · NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks 2024 · cited in Text extraction, Quality filtering, Deduplication, Reference pipelines

15T tokens from 96 Common Crawl snapshots with fully ablated choices: WARC + trafilatura over WET, per-snapshot (not global) MinHash dedup, selected C4 filters; FineWeb-Edu uses an LLM-annotated (LLM = large language model) educational-quality classifier.

Remember: 15T tokens; FineWeb-Edu 1.3T
fastText language identification models
Facebook AI (artificial intelligence) Research · fasttext.cc 2017 · cited in Text extraction

Pretrained fastText language-ID models (lid.176.bin, 126 MB; lid.176.ftz, 917 kB) trained on Wikipedia, Tatoeba and SETimes; widely used to split web crawls by language.

Remember: 176 languages
Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer (T5)
Raffel et al. · JMLR 2020 · cited in Quality filtering, Reference pipelines

Systematic study of encoder-decoder pretraining with a span-corruption objective, framing every task as text-to-text.

Remember: corrupts 15% of tokens
Scaling Language Models: Methods, Analysis & Insights from Training Gopher
Rae et al. · arXiv 2021 · cited in Quality filtering, Deduplication, Reference pipelines

MassiveText pipeline: content filtering, text extraction, simple quality rules (word count, word length, symbols, stop words), repetition removal, MinHash deduplication and test-set filtering.

Remember: near-duplicate if 13-gram Jaccard > 0.8
CCNet: Extracting High Quality Monolingual Datasets from Web Crawl Data
Wenzek et al. · LREC 2020 · cited in Quality filtering

Common Crawl pipeline: paragraph-level deduplication, fastText language identification, and quality scoring by the perplexity of a language model trained on Wikipedia.

DataComp-LM: In search of the next generation of training sets for language models
Li et al. · NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks 2024 · cited in Quality filtering, Reference pipelines

A 240T-token Common Crawl pool and controlled benchmark for data curation; model-based filtering with a fastText classifier (instruction data + ELI5 as positives) works best.

Remember: 7B model, 64% MMLU with 2.6T tokens
On the resemblance and containment of documents
Broder · Compression and Complexity of Sequences 1997 · cited in Deduplication

Defines resemblance (Jaccard similarity of shingle sets) and estimates it by random min-wise sampling: the origin of MinHash.

Deduplicating Training Data Makes Language Models Better
Lee et al. · ACL 2022 · cited in Deduplication, PII and safety filtering

Exact-substring (suffix array) and MinHash near-duplicate removal; deduplicated models memorize less, train in fewer steps and have less train-test overlap.

Remember: one 61-word sentence repeated 60,000+ times in C4; 10× less memorized output
Scaling Data-Constrained Language Models
Muennighoff et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · cited in Data mixtures and repetition

When unique data runs out, repeating it is nearly as good as new data for a few epochs, then returns diminish rapidly; proposes a scaling law for repeated tokens.

Remember: up to 4 epochs ≈ unique data
DoReMi: Optimizing Data Mixtures Speeds Up Language Model Pretraining
Xie et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · cited in Data mixtures and repetition

Trains a small proxy model with group distributionally robust optimization to choose domain weights, then trains the large model on that mixture.

Remember: 280M proxy → 8B model: +6.5 points, 2.6× fewer steps
