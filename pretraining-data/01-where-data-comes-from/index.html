Pretraining Data · 01

Where pretraining data comes from

The sources behind trillion-token training sets, why the open web dominates, and how much of a raw crawl survives the cleaning pipeline.

What is the problem?A frontier model trains on about 15 trillion tokens. No curated collection is that large, so most of it must come from the web, which is mostly noise.
Core ideaStart from a web crawl (usually Common Crawl), then run a pipeline: extract text, identify language, filter for quality, deduplicate, remove unsafe and personal data, and mix with curated sources (code, books, papers, math). Typically only about 10% of crawled documents survive.

2 · Limitation

Why not just use curated text?

Too small

Wikipedia, books and papers together are far short of 15T tokens.

Too narrow

Models need conversational, technical, multilingual and code text in many styles.

Too static

Curated sets update slowly; a monthly crawl brings 3–5 billion new pages.

4 · Core idea

Crawl, clean, mix

Crawl

Common Crawl: a free, open crawl of over 300 billion pages, released as WARC (Web ARChive format), WAT (metadata extracted from Web ARChive records) and WET (plain text extracted from Web ARChive records) files.

Clean

Extract main text, keep target languages, apply quality rules and classifiers.

Deduplicate

Remove exact and near-duplicate documents and repeated lines.

Mix

Combine cleaned web text with code, math, books and multilingual data in chosen proportions.

5 · How it works

Worked example: how much of a crawl survives?

RefinedWeb (Penedo et al., 2023) reports what fraction of Common Crawl documents remain after each stage of its pipeline.

Worked example

  1. URL filtering, text extraction and language identification (English only): 48% of documents remain, most lost to language ID (identifier).
  2. Repetition removal and document/line quality filters: 23% remain.
  3. Fuzzy (MinHash) and exact deduplication: about 10% remain: "nearly 90%" of documents are removed in total.
  4. Even so, RefinedWeb extracted about 5 trillion tokens; FineWeb's 96 snapshots gave 15 trillion.
Web data is abundant but mostly unusable as-is: the pipeline, not the crawl, is where data quality is made.
6 · Why it works

Why the web works despite the noise

Scale

Even a 10% survival rate leaves trillions of tokens.

Diversity

The web covers nearly every topic, register and language.

Filtering is cheap

Rules and small classifiers process terabytes on CPUs (Dolma: ~122 CPU-hours per TB for C4-style filtering).

Evidence

RefinedWeb showed properly filtered and deduplicated web data alone can match or beat curated mixtures.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"LLMs (large language models) are trained on "the whole internet"."

They are trained on a heavily filtered, deduplicated slice: typically about a tenth of crawled documents, plus curated sources.

"Common Crawl is clean text."

It is raw crawl output; its WET text keeps navigation, ads and boilerplate.

"More data is always better."

Removing low-quality and duplicate data improves models at the same compute (RefinedWeb, FineWeb, DCLM (DataComp for Language Models)).
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Common Crawl formats. WARC = raw responses, WAT = metadata, WET = extracted text.
  • Expect ~90% loss. Language, quality and duplicates each remove large shares.
  • Mix matters. Llama 3: ~50% general, 25% math/reasoning, 17% code, 8% multilingual.

⚠ Traps

  • Assuming "more web" = better. Quality and deduplication beat raw volume.
  • Treating WET text as clean. It keeps menus and boilerplate.
  • Forgetting licensing and consent. robots.txt and terms of service increasingly restrict AI (artificial intelligence) crawlers (deck 04).

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Pretraining data is mostly filtered web crawl: extract, identify language, filter, deduplicate, scrub and mix, and only about a tenth of crawled documents survive.
Say it in 30 seconds

Trillion-token training sets can only come from the web, usually Common Crawl, which publishes raw responses as WARC files plus metadata and extracted text. A pipeline turns that into training data: extract the main text from HTML, keep the target languages, apply quality rules and classifiers, remove exact and near duplicates, mask or remove personal and unsafe content, and finally mix the cleaned web text with code, math, books and multilingual data. In RefinedWeb only about ten percent of documents survived, yet that still gave five trillion tokens. Llama 3 trained on about fifteen trillion.

Problem: need ~15T tokens; the web is the only source that large.
Core idea: crawl → clean → dedup → scrub → mix.
Numbers: ~10% of documents survive; WARC/WAT/WET.

Common Crawl: Overview
Common Crawl Foundation · commoncrawl.org 2026

Free, open web crawl released as WARC (raw responses), WAT (metadata) and WET (extracted text) files; the starting point of most open pretraining corpora.

Remember: over 300 billion pages; 3–5 billion new pages a month
The RefinedWeb Dataset for Falcon LLM: Outperforming Curated Corpora with Web Data, and Web Data Only
Penedo et al. · NeurIPS 2023

Shows filtered, heavily deduplicated web data alone can beat curated corpora; trafilatura extraction, Gopher-style rules, strict MinHash plus exact-substring dedup.

Remember: 5T tokens; ~90% of documents removed; 600B released
The Llama 3 Herd of Models
Llama Team, AI @ Meta · arXiv 2024

Dense Transformer with GQA (8 KV (key-value) heads), RoPE (rotary position embedding) base 500,000, a 128K vocabulary and a document mask; staged long-context pretraining to 128K; post-training with SFT (supervised fine-tuning), rejection sampling and DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) in six rounds.

Remember: 128K context after six stages over ~800B tokens
Dolma: an Open Corpus of Three Trillion Tokens for Language Model Pretraining Research
Soldaini et al. · ACL 2024

Open 3T-token corpus and toolkit: Gopher rules plus C4's punctuation rule, toxicity classifiers, regex-based PII masking (emails, IP (Internet Protocol) addresses, phone numbers) and dedup.

Remember: ~200 TB raw → 11 TB
