Data mixtures and repetition

How much of each source to train on: domain weights, temperature sampling for small domains and languages, learned mixtures (DoReMi), and how many times data can be repeated before it stops helping.

What is the problem?Cleaned web text dwarfs code, math, books and low-resource languages. Sampling by size starves the small domains; up-weighting them means repeating them many times.

2 · Limitation

Why the mix is hard

Imbalance

Web text is orders of magnitude larger than code, math or most languages.

Repetition

Up-weighting a small domain means repeating it; too much leads to memorisation and wasted compute.

Expensive to tune

Each candidate mix needs a training run to evaluate.

4 · Core idea

Three ways to set weights

Natural

wᵢ ∝ nᵢ: proportional to size. Simple, but small domains vanish.

Temperature

wᵢ ∝ (nᵢ/Σn)^(1/T): T > 1 flattens toward uniform; widely used for multilingual data.

Learned

DoReMi: a small proxy model trained with group DRO (distributionally robust optimization) outputs domain weights for the big run.

At a glance

Late in training, small high-quality sets are often up-weighted ("annealing").

Mixes and schedules in practice

Llama 3 mix

About 50% general knowledge, 25% math and reasoning, 17% code, 8% multilingual tokens, chosen with small-model scaling experiments.

Annealing

At the end of pretraining, Llama 3 trained on small amounts of high-quality code and math with the learning rate decaying, which boosted key benchmarks.

DoReMi

280M proxy → 8B model (30× larger): +6.5 points few-shot accuracy, baseline reached with 2.6× fewer steps on The Pile.

5 · How it works

Worked example: a 15T-token budget

Notebook 06: web 12T, code 800B, math 150B, books 60B, Wikipedia 20B, low-resource languages 5B unique tokens; budget 15T tokens.

Worked example

  1. Natural sampling (T = 1): web gets 92.1%; every domain is seen 1.2 times; low-resource languages get almost nothing.
  2. Temperature T = 3: web 50%, code 20%, math 11.6%; but math is repeated 11.6×, Wikipedia 44×, low-resource languages 112×.
  3. Capping at 4 epochs and giving the rest to web: web 72%, code 21%, math 4%; nothing exceeds 4 epochs.
  4. Reported mixes: Llama 3 ≈ 50% general, 25% math and reasoning, 17% code, 8% multilingual; DoReMi's learned weights gave +6.5 points and 2.6× fewer steps on The Pile.
The mix is a trade-off between emphasis and repetition; caps keep small domains from being memorised.
6 · Why it works

Why mixtures matter

Capabilities follow data

Code and math share drives coding and reasoning ability.

Repetition has a budget

A few epochs are nearly free; many are wasted compute.

Small models predict big ones

Mixtures found with proxies transfer to larger runs (DoReMi, Llama 3 experiments).

Timing matters

Up-weighting high-quality data late in training (annealing) helps more than early.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"The mix just reflects what data exists."

It is a deliberate choice; natural proportions would be over 90% web.

"Repeating data is always bad."

Up to about 4 epochs costs almost nothing compared with unique data.

"One mix fits all model sizes."

Optimal weights depend on scale and goals; teams re-tune with scaling experiments.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Epochs = weight × budget ÷ unique tokens. Compute it per domain.
  • ~4 epochs is nearly free. Beyond that, value decays quickly; ~40 epochs is about worthless.
  • Proxy models for mixtures. DoReMi: 280M proxy chose weights for an 8B model.

⚠ Traps

  • Natural sampling. Starves code, math and languages.
  • Unlimited up-weighting. Hundreds of epochs on a small set means memorisation.
  • Tuning the mix on the final benchmarks only. Risks overfitting the evaluation; use held-out validation domains.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

A data mix is a set of domain weights; temperature or learned weights up-weight small domains, and capping repetition near 4 epochs keeps that emphasis from turning into memorisation.

Scaling Data-Constrained Language Models
Muennighoff et al. · NeurIPS 2023

When unique data runs out, repeating it is nearly as good as new data for a few epochs, then returns diminish rapidly; proposes a scaling law for repeated tokens.
