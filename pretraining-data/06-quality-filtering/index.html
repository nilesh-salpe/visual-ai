Quality filtering

Deciding which documents are worth training on: transparent heuristic rules (C4, Gopher), perplexity scoring (CCNet), and model-based classifiers (DCLM (DataComp for Language Models), FineWeb-Edu, Llama 3).

What is the problem?After extraction, much of the web is still spam, keyword lists, machine-generated pages, fragments and code dumps. Training on it wastes compute and teaches bad habits.
Core ideaApply cheap heuristic rules first (word counts, word length, symbol ratios, punctuation, stop words, repetition), then model-based scoring: a classifier (often fastText) trained to separate high-quality reference text from random web text, keeping the top fraction.

4 · Core idea

Three generations of filters

Heuristic rules

C4 (2020) and Gopher (2021): length, characters, punctuation, stop words, repetition. Transparent and cheap.

Reference-model perplexity

CCNet (2020): score each document by the perplexity of a language model trained on Wikipedia.

Learned classifiers

DCLM, FineWeb-Edu, Llama 3: classifiers trained on curated or LLM-labelled (LLM = large language model) examples pick the top fraction.

At a glance

Rules remove obvious junk; classifiers decide what counts as good.

5 · How it works

The rules, verbatim

SourceRule
C4keep lines ending in terminal punctuation with ≥ 5 words; drop pages with < 3 sentences, bad words, "lorem ipsum" or "{"; drop lines with "javascript" or cookie/privacy notices; English with probability ≥ 0.99
Gopher50–100,000 words; mean word length 3–10; symbol-to-word ratio ≤ 0.1 for # and ellipsis; ≤ 90% bullet lines; ≤ 30% lines ending in ellipsis; ≥ 80% words with a letter; ≥ 2 of 8 stop words
Llama 3duplicated n-gram coverage, "dirty word" counts, token-distribution KL (Kullback-Leibler divergence) divergence; then fastText and DistilRoberta quality classifiers trained on Llama 2 labels

From Raffel et al. 2020, Rae et al. 2021 and the Llama 3 report.

5 · How it works

Worked example: model-based filtering pays off

DCLM (Li et al., 2024) compared filters on a 240T-token Common Crawl pool; FineWeb-Edu (Penedo et al., 2024) annotated pages with an LLM.

Worked example

  1. DCLM: a fastText classifier trained with instruction-style data (OpenHermes 2.5) and top r/ExplainLikeImFive posts as positives gave a 3.5-point lift on its core benchmark over more conventional choices.
  2. The resulting DCLM-Baseline trained a 7B model to 64% 5-shot MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding) with 2.6T tokens.
  3. FineWeb-Edu: Llama-3-70B-Instruct scored 460,000 pages for educational value (0–5); a classifier trained on those labels (threshold 3, F1 82%) selected 1.3T tokens from FineWeb's 15T.
  4. Running that classifier over all 15T tokens took 6,000 H100 GPU-hours (GPU = graphics processing unit).
Heuristics are necessary but not sufficient; a cheap learned notion of "good text" gives the biggest gains.
6 · Why it works

Why filtering improves models

Better tokens per FLOP

Removing junk means each training step learns from more informative text.

Fewer bad habits

Spam, keyword lists and broken text teach repetition and incoherence.

Cheap at scale

Rules and fastText run on CPUs (central processing units) over trillions of tokens.

Ablation-driven

FineWeb and DCLM choose thresholds by training small models and measuring benchmarks.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Quality filtering means keeping text like Wikipedia."

That is one choice (CCNet, GPT-3-style classifiers); DCLM found instruction-like and ELI5 (Explain Like I'm Five) positives worked better.

"Heuristic filters are obsolete."

Every modern pipeline still runs them first; classifiers come after.

"Filtering is neutral."

Definitions of "quality" and "toxicity" encode choices and can remove dialects and minority voices (Dolma discusses this).
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Gopher rules. 50–100,000 words; mean word length 3–10; ≥ 2 stop words; symbol ratio ≤ 0.1.
  • C4 rules. Lines end in punctuation, ≥ 5 words; ≥ 3 sentences; no "{" or lorem ipsum.
  • fastText classifiers. Fast enough for trillions of tokens on CPUs.

⚠ Traps

  • Over-filtering. C4's curly-bracket rule removes all code; strict rules can remove dialects and minority topics.
  • Filtering on your benchmark. Classifiers trained toward test-like text inflate scores.
  • Perplexity alone. Prefers text similar to the reference corpus (e.g. Wikipedia), not necessarily useful text.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Quality filtering stacks cheap heuristic rules (C4, Gopher) with learned classifiers (fastText, LLM-labelled) that keep the most useful fraction; model-based filtering gives the biggest gains.
Say it in 30 seconds

After extraction, much web text is still spam, keyword lists, fragments or code dumps. Pipelines first apply transparent rules: C4 keeps lines ending in punctuation and drops pages with placeholder text or curly brackets; Gopher requires between 50 and 100,000 words, normal word lengths, few symbols and at least two common English stop words, and removes highly repetitive documents. Then a learned filter scores what remains. DCLM's fastText classifier trained on instruction-style examples and FineWeb-Edu's classifier trained on Llama 3 ratings both beat heuristics alone, which is why model-based filtering is now standard.

Wenzek et al. · LREC 2020

A 240T-token Common Crawl pool and controlled benchmark for data curation; model-based filtering with a fastText classifier (instruction data + ELI5 as positives) works best.

Penedo et al. · NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks 2024

15T tokens from 96 Common Crawl snapshots with fully ablated choices: WARC (Web ARChive format) + trafilatura over WET (plain text extracted from Web ARChive records), per-snapshot (not global) MinHash dedup, selected C4 filters; FineWeb-Edu uses an LLM-annotated educational-quality classifier.
