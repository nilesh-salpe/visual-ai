Crawling at scale

What changes when a crawl reaches billions of pages: throughput budgets, URL uniqueness on disk, spam farms and crawler traps, near-duplicate detection, and keeping pages fresh.

What is the problem?At billions of pages, the URL-seen set no longer fits in memory, infinite link spaces and spam sites can swallow the crawl, and pages change after you fetch them.
Core ideaBudget the crawl: pages/s × time sets the size. Store the seen-set on disk with batched checks; give each site a crawl budget so spam farms and traps cannot dominate; fingerprint content to drop near-duplicates; and revisit pages by how often they change.

2 · Limitation

What breaks past a billion pages

Memory

A seen-set of tens of billions of URLs does not fit in RAM (random-access memory); random disk lookups are too slow.

Adversarial web

Spam farms, auto-generated pages and infinite calendars create unlimited URLs.

Change

Pages are edited and deleted; a crawl is a snapshot that decays.

4 · Core idea

Four scaling techniques

Disk-backed URL checks

Batch new URLs and check them against a sorted on-disk set sequentially (IRLbot), instead of random lookups.

Budgets

Pages per site in proportion to reputation; caps on URL depth and parameters to escape traps.

Content fingerprints

Simhash or MinHash detects near-identical pages under different URLs.

Revisit scheduling

Re-crawl frequently-changing pages more often; Common Crawl publishes new snapshots regularly.

5 · How it works

Worked example: sizing a monthly crawl

Common Crawl adds about 3–5 billion new pages a month. IRLbot sustained 1,789 pages/s on a single server.

Worked example

  1. 3 billion pages in 30 days = 3 × 10⁹ ÷ (30 × 86,400 s) ≈ 1,157 pages/s on average.
  2. That is within a single IRLbot-class server (1,789 pages/s), or a modest cluster with headroom.
  3. With 0.5 s downloads and a 10× politeness delay (0.18 pages/s per host), 1,157 pages/s needs at least 6,366 hosts active at once.
  4. IRLbot: 6.3 billion valid HTML (HyperText Markup Language) pages (7.6 billion connection requests) in 41 days at an average 319 Mb/s.
Bandwidth is rarely the limit; scheduling across millions of hosts, URL bookkeeping and spam resistance are.
6 · Why it works

Why these techniques work

Sequential beats random

Batched, sorted disk access turns billions of random lookups into fast scans.

Reputation resists spam

Spammers can create pages cheaply but not links from many independent reputable sites.

Fingerprints are tiny

64 bits per page make near-duplicate checks over billions of pages feasible.

Not all pages change

Spending revisits where change is likely keeps the snapshot fresh at the same cost.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Crawling the web is mainly a bandwidth problem."

IRLbot crawled 6.3 billion pages from one server; the hard parts were URL uniqueness, spam and politeness.

"A crawl captures "the web"."

It captures a budgeted, biased sample at one time; much of the web is behind logins or generated on demand.

"Duplicate URLs are the only duplicates."

The same content appears under many URLs and sites; content fingerprints are needed too.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • pages/s = pages ÷ seconds. 1B pages a month ≈ 386 pages/s.
  • Per-site budgets. Limit pages per domain by its reputation (IRLbot's budgets).
  • Simhash for near-duplicates. Google: 64-bit fingerprints, ≤ 3 differing bits, 8B pages.

⚠ Traps

  • Infinite URL spaces. Calendars, session IDs (identifiers) and faceted search generate endless URLs.
  • Spam farms. Highly-branching link spam can absorb most of a breadth-first crawl.
  • Stale pages. Without revisits, the crawl ages; with naive revisits, it wastes budget on static pages.

1 billion pages in 10 days needs roughly how many pages/s?

10⁹ ÷ 864,000 s ≈ 1,157.

What is a crawler trap?

A site structure that generates unlimited URLs (calendars, session IDs, faceted filters), which can absorb the crawl without new content.

Why use simhash or MinHash during crawling?

To detect pages whose content is almost the same as one already stored, even under a different URL.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

At billions of pages the bottlenecks are bookkeeping and adversaries: disk-backed URL checks, per-site budgets, content fingerprints and revisit policies make crawls scale.
Say it in 30 seconds

Scaling a crawler is less about bandwidth than bookkeeping. One server running IRLbot crawled 6.3 billion pages in 41 days at about 1,800 pages per second. To get there the seen-set lives on disk and is checked in sorted batches, each site gets a crawl budget tied to its reputation so spam farms and infinite calendars cannot take over, and content fingerprints such as Google's 64-bit simhash catch near-identical pages under different URLs. Since pages change, revisits are scheduled by how often they update. Common Crawl applies this at web scale, adding three to five billion pages a month.

IRLbot: Scaling to 6 Billion Pages and Beyond
Lee, Leonard, Wang & Loguinov · WWW (World Wide Web (conference)) 2008

Single-server crawler design for billions of pages: disk-based URL uniqueness checks, budget-based crawling to resist spam farms, and per-host/per-server rate limits.

Manku, Jain & Das Sarma · WWW 2007

Uses 64-bit simhash fingerprints, where near-duplicates differ in few bits, and fast Hamming-distance search to deduplicate a web-scale crawl.

Remember: 8B pages, 64-bit fingerprints, k = 3 bits

Free, open web crawl released as WARC (raw responses), WAT (metadata) and WET (extracted text) files; the starting point of most open pretraining corpora.
