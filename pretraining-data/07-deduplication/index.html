Pretraining Data · 07

Deduplication

Why the web is full of copies, and how pipelines remove exact duplicates, near-duplicates (MinHash with locality-sensitive hashing) and repeated lines and substrings.

What is the problem?The same text appears thousands of times: mirrored pages, templates, licences, quotes, spam. Training on it wastes compute, encourages memorisation and leaks test data.
Core ideaRemove exact duplicates with hashes (URL, document, line). Find near-duplicates by comparing documents' sets of word n-grams: MinHash compresses each set into a signature whose agreement estimates Jaccard similarity, and LSH (locality-sensitive hashing) banding finds similar pairs without comparing all pairs.
1 · Problem

Tune the LSH S-curve

Probability that a pair with Jaccard similarity s becomes a candidate, for b bands of r rows. Presets reproduce RefinedWeb and FineWeb.

2 · Limitation

Why simple hashing isn't enough

Near-duplicates

A changed date, ad or footer gives a different hash for essentially the same page.

All-pairs is impossible

Billions of documents mean ~10¹⁸ pairs.

Repeated pieces

Boilerplate lines and licences repeat inside otherwise unique documents.

3 · Your turn

What does MinHash estimate?

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

For two documents' shingle sets, the fraction of positions where their MinHash signatures agree estimates…

B. Broder (1997): the probability that two sets have the same minimum under a random hash equals their Jaccard similarity.
4 · Core idea

Exact, near, and sub-document

Exact

Hash URLs, documents or lines; keep one per hash. Cheap and lossless.

Near-duplicate

Shingle → MinHash → LSH bands → candidate pairs → cluster → keep one (Broder; Lee et al.).

Sub-document

Remove repeated lines (Llama 3, CCNet) or long repeated substrings (suffix arrays, Lee et al.).

At a glance

P(candidate) = 1 − (1 − sʳ)ᵇ: an S-curve around s ≈ (1/b)^(1/r).

FineWeb's surprise

Deduplicating all snapshots against each other removed up to 90% of the oldest snapshots, and the data that survived was worse than what was removed (more ads and keyword lists). Deduplicating each snapshot independently gave 20T tokens that matched RefinedWeb.

5 · How it works

Worked example: choosing b and r

Two pipelines' MinHash settings, evaluated with P(s) = 1 − (1 − sʳ)ᵇ for Jaccard similarity s.

Worked example

  1. RefinedWeb: 9,000 hashes as 450 bands × 20 rows. P(0.75) = 76%, P(0.8) = 99.5%; threshold ≈ (1/450)^(1/20) = 0.74.
  2. FineWeb: 112 hashes as 14 bands × 8 rows, "targeting documents that are at least 75% similar": P(0.75) = 77%, P(0.8) = 92%, P(0.5) = 5.3%.
  3. Gopher: near-duplicates are documents whose 13-gram Jaccard similarity exceeds 0.8.
  4. Lee et al. (2022) found one 61-word sentence repeated over 60,000 times in C4; deduplicated models emitted memorised text 10× less often.
More bands lower the threshold; more rows per band sharpen it. Pipelines tune both to their notion of "duplicate".
6 · Why it works

Why deduplication helps

Less memorisation

Lee et al.: deduplicated models emit memorised text 10× less often.

Better use of compute

Same or better accuracy in fewer training steps.

Cleaner evaluation

Train-test overlap affected over 4% of some validation sets before dedup.

Scales

MinHash + LSH is linear in the number of documents.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Deduplication only removes identical files."

Most removed data are near-duplicates and repeated passages, found with MinHash, simhash or suffix arrays.

"More aggressive dedup is always better."

FineWeb found global cross-snapshot dedup hurt; settings must be validated by ablation.

"Duplicates are harmless extra epochs."

Heavily repeated text drives memorisation and verbatim regurgitation.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Threshold ≈ (1/b)^(1/r). Steepness grows with r.
  • Jaccard on word n-grams. 5-grams (RefinedWeb, FineWeb) or 13-grams (Gopher).
  • Line-level dedup. Llama 3 removed lines appearing more than 6 times per 30M-document bucket.

⚠ Traps

  • Global dedup across all snapshots. FineWeb found it kept worse data; per-snapshot dedup worked better.
  • Comparing all pairs. n² is impossible at billions of documents; use LSH.
  • Forgetting test sets. Remove documents overlapping benchmarks (Gopher's test-set filtering).

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Deduplicate at every level: hashes for exact copies, MinHash + LSH banding (P = 1 − (1 − sʳ)ᵇ) for near-duplicates, and line or substring removal for repeated pieces.
Say it in 30 seconds

The web is full of copies, from mirrored pages to licences and spam, and training on them wastes compute and causes memorisation. Exact duplicates are removed by hashing URLs, documents or lines. Near-duplicates are found by representing each document as a set of word n-grams and estimating Jaccard similarity with MinHash, which keeps the minimum of many random hashes. Locality-sensitive hashing splits signatures into bands so only documents sharing a band are compared; the probability of becoming a candidate follows an S-curve set by the number of bands and rows. Deduplicated models memorise far less.

Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

On the resemblance and containment of documents
Broder · Compression and Complexity of Sequences 1997

Defines resemblance (Jaccard similarity of shingle sets) and estimates it by random min-wise sampling: the origin of MinHash.

Deduplicating Training Data Makes Language Models Better
Lee et al. · ACL 2022

Exact-substring (suffix array) and MinHash near-duplicate removal; deduplicated models memorize less, train in fewer steps and have less train-test overlap.

Remember: one 61-word sentence repeated 60,000+ times in C4; 10× less memorized output
The FineWeb Datasets: Decanting the Web for the Finest Text Data at Scale
Penedo et al. · NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks 2024

15T tokens from 96 Common Crawl snapshots with fully ablated choices: WARC (Web ARChive format) + trafilatura over WET (plain text extracted from Web ARChive records), per-snapshot (not global) MinHash dedup, selected C4 filters; FineWeb-Edu uses an LLM-annotated (LLM = large language model) educational-quality classifier.

Remember: 15T tokens; FineWeb-Edu 1.3T
The RefinedWeb Dataset for Falcon LLM: Outperforming Curated Corpora with Web Data, and Web Data Only
Penedo et al. · NeurIPS 2023

Shows filtered, heavily deduplicated web data alone can beat curated corpora; trafilatura extraction, Gopher-style rules, strict MinHash plus exact-substring dedup.

Remember: 5T tokens; ~90% of documents removed; 600B released
Scaling Language Models: Methods, Analysis & Insights from Training Gopher
Rae et al. · arXiv 2021

MassiveText pipeline: content filtering, text extraction, simple quality rules (word count, word length, symbols, stop words), repetition removal, MinHash deduplication and test-set filtering.

Remember: near-duplicate if 13-gram Jaccard > 0.8
