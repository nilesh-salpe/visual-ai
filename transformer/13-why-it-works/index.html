Attention Is All You Need · 13

Why the Transformer works

How four ingredients together address the limits of recurrent sequence models: direct token-to-token connections, multiple heads, positional information, and stacked layers.

What is the problem?Recurrent models are slow to train (sequential), struggle with long-range dependencies (long paths), and capture all relationships through one hidden state.
Core ideaEach ingredient fixes one limit. The paper's ablations (Table 3) measure how much each design choice matters.
① What is the problem?

Three criteria that recurrent layers score poorly on

§4 compares layer types on three criteria. For translation-length sequences (n < d), recurrence loses on all three.

Parallelism

Sequential operations per layer: RNN O(n) vs self-attention O(1). Training time grows with sequence length.

Long-range paths

Maximum path length: RNN O(n) vs self-attention O(1). Distant dependencies are harder to learn.

Compute per layer

RNN O(n·d²) vs self-attention O(n²·d). Self-attention is cheaper when n < d, as in sentence-level translation with BPE.

Attention fixes parallelism and path length, but on its own it brings two new problems: it is order-blind, and averaging blurs distinct relationships. The other ingredients address those.

The paper's own conclusions

§6.2 (A): "While single-head attention is 0.9 BLEU worse than the best setting, quality also drops off with too many heads."
§6.2 (B): "Reducing the attention key size dk hurts model quality. This suggests that determining compatibility is not easy and that a more sophisticated compatibility function than dot product may be beneficial."
§6.2 (C, D, E): "Bigger models are better, and dropout is very helpful in avoiding over-fitting." Replacing sinusoids with learned positional embeddings gives "nearly identical results".
§7: the Transformer is "the first sequence transduction model based entirely on attention", and "can be trained significantly faster than architectures based on recurrent or convolutional layers."
Recap

Why it works, in two lines

Problem: recurrence is sequential, uses long paths between distant tokens, and squeezes all relationships into one state.
Core idea: direct attention (parallel, O(1) paths) + multiple heads (several relationships) + positional encoding (order) + stacked residual layers with FFNs (depth), each backed by the Table 3 ablations.
