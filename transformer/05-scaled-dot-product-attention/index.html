Attention Is All You Need · 05

Scaled dot-product attention

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKᵀ / √dk) V: how the scores are computed, scaled, normalized and used to take weighted sums.

What is the problem?Dot products of dk-dimensional vectors grow with dk. Large scores push the softmax into a near one-hot regime where its gradients are tiny.
Core ideaDivide the scores by √dk so their variance stays near 1 whatever the dimension, then apply softmax and take weighted sums of V, all as matrix multiplications.
① What is the problem?

Bigger dk → bigger dot products → saturated softmax

If the components of q and k are independent with mean 0 and variance 1, then q·k has variance dk. With dk = 64 the scores spread over roughly ±8, the softmax puts nearly all its weight on one key, and its gradients become very small. Drag dk.

② Core idea

Why dot products, and why the scaling?

Dot-product attention

Compatibility = q·k. It's fast and memory-efficient "since it can be implemented using highly optimized matrix multiplication code" (§3.2.1). The paper's version is identical to this except for the 1/√dk factor.

Additive attention

Compatibility is computed by a feed-forward network with one hidden layer. Its theoretical complexity is similar, but it is slower in practice.

Small dk

The two mechanisms perform similarly.

Large dk

Additive attention outperforms unscaled dot-product attention. The paper attributes this to large dot products saturating the softmax, so it scales by 1/√dk.

Scaling keeps the fast matrix-multiply form of dot-product attention and avoids its large-dk problem.
In the paper · §3.2.1, Eq. 1, footnote 4

The exact statement

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( QKᵀ / √dk ) V    (1)
Inputs: queries and keys of dimension dk, values of dimension dv. Compute the dot products of the query with all keys, divide each by √dk, and apply a softmax to obtain the weights on the values.
Footnote 4: if the components of q and k are independent random variables with mean 0 and variance 1, then q·k = Σi qiki has mean 0 and variance dk.
Masking (used in the decoder): illegal connections are masked out by setting their softmax inputs to −∞ (§3.2.3; Figure 2's optional "Mask" step). See deck 09.
Recap

Scaled dot-product attention in two lines

Problem: raw dot products have variance dk, so the softmax saturates and gradients shrink as dk grows.
Core idea: scores = QKᵀ / √dk, then a row-wise softmax gives weights, then weights × V gives outputs. That is two matrix multiplications and a softmax.
