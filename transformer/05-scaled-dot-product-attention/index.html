Attention Is All You Need · 05

Scaled dot-product attention

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKᵀ / √dk) V: how the scores are computed, scaled, normalized and used to take weighted sums.

What is the problem?Dot products of dk-dimensional vectors grow with dk. Large scores push the softmax into a near one-hot regime where its gradients are tiny.
Core ideaDivide the scores by √dk so their variance stays near 1 whatever the dimension, then apply softmax and take weighted sums of V, all as matrix multiplications.
1 · Problem

Attention needs a compatibility score for every query-key pair, and it must be fast

With n tokens, a single head computes n² query-key scores. The base model runs 8 heads in each of its 18 attention sub-layers (6 encoder + 12 decoder), on every training step. Whatever function produces the scores has to be cheap and has to train well.

n² scores per head

A 50-token sentence means 2,500 scores per head per layer.

Two known options

Additive attention (a small feed-forward network per pair) and dot-product attention (q·k) (§3.2.1).

Must map to GPUs

The fastest operation on a GPU is a large matrix multiply.

2 · Limitation

Why dot products, and why the scaling?

Dot-product attention

Compatibility = q·k. It's fast and memory-efficient "since it can be implemented using highly optimized matrix multiplication code" (§3.2.1). The paper's version is identical to this except for the 1/√dk factor.

Additive attention

Compatibility is computed by a feed-forward network with one hidden layer. Its theoretical complexity is similar, but it is slower in practice.

Small dk

The two mechanisms perform similarly.

Large dk

Additive attention outperforms unscaled dot-product attention. The paper attributes this to large dot products saturating the softmax, so it scales by 1/√dk.

Scaling keeps the fast matrix-multiply form of dot-product attention and avoids its large-dk problem.
2 · Limitation

Bigger dk → bigger dot products → saturated softmax

If the components of q and k are independent with mean 0 and variance 1, then q·k has variance dk. With dk = 64 the scores spread over roughly ±8, the softmax puts nearly all its weight on one key, and its gradients become very small. Drag dk.

3 · Your turn

Fix the saturation, keep the speed

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

Dot products of unit-variance vectors have variance dk. What is the simplest fix that keeps the matrix-multiply speed?

C. Dividing by √dk brings the variance back to exactly 1, whatever dk is. Dividing by dk (B) shrinks the variance to 1/dk, so the softmax becomes nearly uniform. Additive attention (A) is slower in practice, and clipping (D) throws away information and breaks gradients.
4 · Core idea

Use dot products, and divide them by √dk

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( QKᵀ / √dk ) V

Dot product

Every query-key score in one matrix multiply, QKᵀ.

÷ √dk

Brings score variance back to about 1, so the softmax stays responsive.

softmax, then × V

Row-wise weights, then a weighted sum of values: a second matrix multiply.

5 · How it works from the paper · §3.2.1, Eq. 1, footnote 4

The exact statement

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( QKᵀ / √dk ) V    (1)
Inputs: queries and keys of dimension dk, values of dimension dv. Compute the dot products of the query with all keys, divide each by √dk, and apply a softmax to obtain the weights on the values.
Footnote 4: if the components of q and k are independent random variables with mean 0 and variance 1, then q·k = Σi qiki has mean 0 and variance dk.
Masking (used in the decoder): illegal connections are masked out by setting their softmax inputs to −∞ (§3.2.3; Figure 2's optional "Mask" step). See deck 09.
6 · Why it works

Why it works

Variance stays at 1

If q and k have independent components with mean 0 and variance 1, q·k has variance dk (footnote 4). Dividing by √dk brings it back to 1 for any dk.

Softmax stays in its sensitive range

Moderate scores give a soft distribution with useful gradients instead of a one-hot one with "extremely small gradients" (§3.2.1).

Hardware-friendly

Two matrix multiplies and a softmax: "much faster and more space-efficient in practice" than additive attention.

Room for masking

Forbidden pairs are handled by writing −∞ into the scores before the softmax (deck 09), with no change to the rest of the computation.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"The √dk factor is a learned temperature."

It is a fixed constant (√64 = 8 in the base model). Nothing about it is learned.

"Scaling changes which key gets the most attention."

Dividing every score by the same positive number keeps their order. Only the sharpness of the softmax changes.

"The softmax is taken over the whole score matrix."

It is applied row by row: each query gets its own distribution over the keys.
8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Answer from memory. If you can't, revisit the slide named in the answer.

In the base model (dk = 64), what are the scores divided by?

8 = √64.

With unit-variance components and dk = 64, what is the standard deviation of an unscaled dot product q·k?

√64 = 8. Variance is dk = 64 (footnote 4), so scores routinely reach ±8 to ±16, enough to saturate a softmax.

For n queries and m keys, what is the shape of softmax(QKᵀ/√dk)?

n × m: one row of weights over the m keys for each query. Multiplying by V (m × dv) gives an n × dv output.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Score with dot products, divide by √dk to keep the variance at 1, softmax each row, and take the weighted sum of V: two matrix multiplies and a softmax.
Say it in 30 seconds

Scaled dot-product attention computes softmax(QKᵀ/√dk)V. Dot products are fast because they are a matrix multiply, but with unit-variance components q·k has variance dk, so large dk pushes the softmax toward one-hot and its gradients vanish. Dividing by √dk restores unit variance. The paper notes that unscaled dot-product attention underperforms additive attention at large dk; scaling fixes this while keeping the speed.

Problem: raw dot products have variance dk, so the softmax saturates and gradients shrink as dk grows.
Core idea: scores = QKᵀ / √dk, then a row-wise softmax gives weights, then weights × V gives outputs. That is two matrix multiplications and a softmax.
