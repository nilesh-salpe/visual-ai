2 · Limitation

The best systems were ensembles, and they were expensive

Ensembles cost more

The best EN-DE result (ConvS2S ensemble, 26.36 BLEU) took 7.7·10¹⁹ training FLOPs; the best EN-FR ensembles took about 10²¹ (Table 2).

Small gains

On EN-DE, the best single model reached 26.03 (MoE (Mixture of Experts)) and the best ensemble only 26.36.

Slow to train

Recurrent models can't parallelize across positions within a sentence, so every experiment takes longer (deck 01).

5 · How it works

Worked example: reproducing the training-cost column

The paper estimates FLOPs = GPUs × sustained single-precision TFLOPS × training time, with 9.5 TFLOPS for a P100.

Worked example

  1. Base: 8 × 9.5 TFLOPS × 12 h = 3.3e+18 FLOPs (Table 2: 3.3 × 10¹⁸).
  2. Big: 8 × 9.5 TFLOPS × 3.5 days = 2.3e+19 FLOPs (Table 2: 2.3 × 10¹⁹).
  3. Big model BLEU: 28.4 on EN-DE and 41.8 on EN-FR (Table 2; the text says 41.0 for EN-FR).
  4. The strongest earlier EN-DE ensembles cost 7.7 × 10¹⁹ (ConvS2S) and 1.8 × 10²⁰ (GNMT + RL) FLOPs and scored 26.4 and 26.3 BLEU, below the big model's 28.4.
The headline is a cost-quality frontier shift: higher BLEU at much lower training FLOPs.
5 · How it works from the paper · Abstract, §6.1

The headline claims

Abstract: 28.4 BLEU on WMT 2014 English-to-German, "improving over the existing best results, including ensembles, by over 2 BLEU", and a new single-model state of the art of 41.8 BLEU on English-to-French "after training for 3.5 days on eight GPUs, a small fraction of the training costs of the best models from the literature."
Setup (§5): WMT 2014 EN-DE, ~4.5M sentence pairs with a shared BPE (byte-pair encoding) vocabulary of ~37K; EN-FR, 36M sentences with a 32K word-piece vocabulary. Adam (β₁ = 0.9, β₂ = 0.98, ε = 10⁻⁹) with 4,000 warmup steps, then inverse-square-root decay.
Decoding (§6.1): beam size 4, length penalty α = 0.6, checkpoint averaging (last 5 for base, last 20 for big).
6 · Why it works

Why the results came out this way

Parallel training

All positions are processed in each step, so a big model trains in 3.5 days on one 8-GPU machine (decks 01, 12).

Short paths

Direct connections make long-range dependencies easier to learn (Table 1), which matters for translation quality.

Cheap enough to beat ensembles

The base model needs 3.3·10¹⁸ FLOPs (12 hours) and still beats ensembles costing up to 10²¹.

It generalizes

Without task-specific tuning it also did well on English constituency parsing (next slide).

6 · Why it works

Does it generalize? English constituency parsing (Table 4)

A 4-layer Transformer (dmodel = 1024) trained on the WSJ (Wall Street Journal) portion of the Penn Treebank (~40K sentences), and in a semi-supervised setting (~17M sentences), with little task-specific tuning. Metric: F1 on WSJ section 23.

It beat all previously reported models except the Recurrent Neural Network Grammar, and unlike RNN seq2seq models it outperformed the BerkeleyParser even with only the 40K WSJ sentences (§6.3).
7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"28.4 BLEU means 28.4% of the translation is correct."

BLEU scores n-gram overlap with reference translations on a 0–100 scale. It is a corpus-level similarity score, not a percentage of correct sentences.

"The training FLOPs in Table 2 were measured."

They are estimates: training time × number of GPUs × an assumed sustained TFLOPS per GPU (9.5 for a P100, footnote 5).

"The Transformer won EN-FR by a large margin."

On EN-FR the gain is small: 41.8 vs 41.29 for the ConvS2S ensemble. The big win there is cost: under 1/4 of the previous state of the art's training cost (§6.1).
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • FLOPs = GPUs × TFLOPS × time. The paper's own estimate method.
  • 28.4 / 41.8. Big model, EN-DE / EN-FR.
  • Checkpoint averaging. Last 5 (base) or 20 (big) checkpoints averaged.

⚠ Traps

  • 41.0 vs 41.8. EN-FR differs between the text and Table 2; cite the table.
  • Comparing ensembles with single models. Table 2 mixes both; read the labels.
  • Ignoring parsing. The paper also shows English constituency parsing generalization (Table 4).

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Better and cheaper: 28.4 BLEU EN-DE (+2.0 over all prior ensembles) and 41.8 EN-FR, for 2.3·10¹⁹ FLOPs on 8 P100 GPUs in 3.5 days.
Say it in 30 seconds

On WMT 2014, the big Transformer reached 28.4 BLEU English-German, more than 2 BLEU above the best previous results including ensembles, and 41.8 English-French, a new single-model state of the art, after 3.5 days of training on 8 P100 GPUs. The paper estimates its training cost at 2.3·10¹⁹ FLOPs, a fraction of what earlier systems used. The base model reached 27.3 in 12 hours. It also transferred to constituency parsing, with 92.7 F1 in the semi-supervised setting.

Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Attention Is All You Need
Vaswani et al. · NeurIPS 2017

The Transformer: attention-only encoder-decoder with post-norm residual blocks, sinusoidal positions and a ReLU (rectified linear unit) FFN (feed-forward network). The baseline every later change is measured against.

Remember: 28.4 BLEU on WMT 2014 English-German
Neural Machine Translation in Linear Time (ByteNet)
Kalchbrenner et al. · arXiv 2016

A dilated convolutional encoder-decoder that runs in time linear in sequence length; a parallel alternative to RNNs.

Convolutional Sequence to Sequence Learning (ConvS2S)
Gehring et al. · ICML 2017

A fully convolutional sequence-to-sequence model with gated linear units and per-layer attention, fully parallel in training.

Remember: beats the deep LSTM (long short-term memory network) of Wu et al. at an order of magnitude faster speed
Google's Neural Machine Translation System (GNMT)
Wu et al. · arXiv 2016

The production deep-LSTM translation system with attention and wordpieces that the Transformer was compared against.

Remember: 60% fewer translation errors than phrase-based production in side-by-side evaluation
