Attention Is All You Need · 06

Multi-head attention

Eight attention functions run in parallel, each in its own 64-dimensional subspace, and their outputs are concatenated and projected.

What is the problem?One attention distribution per token has to average over every kind of relationship (who "it" refers to, what describes it, its neighbours), which blurs them together.
Core ideaProject Q, K and V h = 8 times into smaller subspaces (dk = 64), attend in each separately, then concatenate and apply WO. The total cost is about the same as one full-width head.
1 · Problem

A word relates to other words in several ways at once

To understand "it" in "The animal didn't cross the street because it was tired", a model needs several different relationships at the same time.

Reference

What does "it" refer to? → "animal"

Predicate

What is said about it? → "was tired"

Local syntax

Neighbouring words such as "because" and "was".

Long-distance structure

In the paper's Figure 3, "making" links to the distant "more difficult".

2 · Limitation

One head has to blend different relationships into one average illustrative weights

To understand "it", a model might want three things at once: what it refers to ("animal"), what is said about it ("tired") and its local syntax ("was"). A single softmax spreads one budget of 100% across all of them.

The paper puts it this way: "With a single attention head, averaging inhibits this" (§3.2.2). §2 also names the cost of attention's averaging: "reduced effective resolution".

4 · Core idea

h = 8 heads in parallel, then concatenate and project

Each head i has its own projections WiQ, WiK, WiV (512 → 64) and runs scaled dot-product attention on its own. The eight 64-dim outputs are concatenated back to 512 and mixed by WO. Click a head to see the kind of pattern a head can learn.

Head patterns are illustrative. The paper reports that individual heads "clearly learn to perform different tasks", with behaviour related to the syntactic and semantic structure of sentences (§4, Figures 3–5).

At a glance

Same parameter budget as one 512-dim head, split into 8 subspaces.

5 · How it works

Shapes through one multi-head attention block (base model)

StepOperationShape
1Input X (or Q, K, V sources)n × 512
2Per head i: Qi = XWiQ, Ki = XWiK, Vi = XWiVn × 64, for each of 8 heads
3Scores QiKiᵀ / √64, softmax by rown × n, for each of 8 heads
4headi = weights × Vin × 64, for each of 8 heads
5Concat(head1, …, head8)n × 512
6× WO (512 × 512)n × 512
Parameter check: Q, K, V projections = 3 × 8 × (512 × 64) = 3 × 512² ; WO = 512². Total 4 × 512², the same as a single head with full 512-dim projections. That is why the cost is "similar to that of single-head attention with full dimensionality" (§3.2.2).
5 · How it works

Worked example: the head-count experiment (Table 3)

Base model on newstest2013 (dev): perplexity 4.92, BLEU (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy, an n-gram overlap score for translation) 25.8, with h = 8 heads of 64 dims.

Worked example

  1. h = 1, d_k = 512: BLEU 24.9 (−0.9).
  2. h = 4, d_k = 128: BLEU 25.5.
  3. h = 16, d_k = 32: BLEU 25.8 (same as base).
  4. h = 32, d_k = 16: BLEU 25.4: too many tiny heads also hurts.
Several heads beat one big head at the same cost, but there is a sweet spot; later work found many trained heads can be pruned (Michel et al.).
5 · How it works from the paper · §3.2.2

The equations

MultiHead(Q, K, V) = Concat(head1, …, headh) WO
headi = Attention(Q WiQ, K WiK, V WiV)
Shapes: WiQ ∈ ℝdmodel×dk, WiK ∈ ℝdmodel×dk, WiV ∈ ℝdmodel×dv, WO ∈ ℝhdv×dmodel. With h = 8 and dk = dv = dmodel/h = 64.
Why: "Multi-head attention allows the model to jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces at different positions." Because each head is smaller, the total computational cost is similar to single-head attention with full dimensionality.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"8 heads cost 8× as much as one head."

Each head works in 64 dims rather than 512, so total computation is about the same as one full-width head (§3.2.2).

"Each head is assigned a specific job, such as coreference."

Nothing is assigned. Heads learn their behaviour from data. The paper observes that many end up doing structure-related tasks (Figures 4–5).

"The heads' outputs are averaged together."

They are concatenated and then mixed by a learned projection WO.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Same cost for any h. h × d_k = d_model, so parameters and FLOPs (floating-point operations) stay constant.
  • Heads specialize. Some track syntax, some coreference (paper Figures 3–5).
  • Concat then project. W_O mixes information across heads.

⚠ Traps

  • More heads always better. Table 3: 32 heads did worse than 8 or 16.
  • Each head sees a slice of the input. Each head sees the full input, projected to a smaller subspace.
  • Heads are independent forever. W_O and later layers combine them.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Split 512 dimensions into 8 heads of 64, let each head attend in its own subspace, then concatenate and mix with WO: several relationships for the price of one.
Say it in 30 seconds

One attention head gives a single weighted average per token, which blurs different relationships together. Multi-head attention projects Q, K and V h = 8 times into 64-dimensional subspaces, runs scaled dot-product attention in each in parallel, concatenates the results and applies WO. Because each head is smaller, the total cost is similar to one full-width head. In Table 3, one head was 0.9 BLEU worse than eight, and 32 heads also did worse.

Problem: one softmax per token averages different relationships together and loses resolution.
Core idea: 8 heads × 64 dims attend in parallel, in different subspaces, then Concat → WO. Same cost, and measurably better than 1 head (Table 3A).
