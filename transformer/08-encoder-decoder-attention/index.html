Attention Is All You Need · 08

Encoder-decoder attention

How every target position looks at every source position, connecting the sentence being generated to the sentence being translated.

What is the problem?While writing each target word, the decoder needs the relevant parts of the source. Compressing the whole source into one fixed-size vector creates a bottleneck.
Core ideaIn the decoder's second sub-layer, queries come from the decoder and keys and values come from the encoder output, so each target position attends over all source positions.
1 · Problem

Each output word depends on specific input words

Alignment

"Katze" comes from "cat", and "Matte" from "mat". Each target word draws on particular source words.

Changing focus

The relevant source words change at every step of the output.

Different word order

Languages order words differently, so the right source word isn't simply "the one at the same position".

While writing each target token, the decoder needs flexible access to the relevant parts of the source sentence.
2 · Limitation

The decoder has to see the source, and one summary vector is too narrow

A basic encoder-decoder squeezes the whole source sentence into one fixed-size vector c. The longer the sentence, the less room each word gets. Attention-based seq2seq models fixed this by letting the decoder look at all encoder states. The Transformer builds this in as one of its three uses of attention.

§3.2.3: this "mimics the typical encoder-decoder attention mechanisms in sequence-to-sequence models", citing Wu et al. 2016, Bahdanau et al. 2014 and Gehring et al. 2017.
3 · Your turn

Who asks, who answers?

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

In the attention layer that connects decoder to encoder, where should the queries come from?

B. The decoder is the one looking for information: "which source words do I need right now?" So queries come from the decoder, and the encoder output supplies the keys (what each source word can be matched on) and values (what it contributes).
4 · Core idea

Q from the decoder; K and V from the encoder output

The encoder runs once and produces a memory of n × 512 vectors. In every one of the 6 decoder layers, the second sub-layer projects its own states into queries and projects that same memory into keys and values.

encoder (source)decoder (target)attention

At a glance

Q from the decoder, K and V from the final encoder layer, in every decoder layer.

5 · How it works

5 · How it works

Worked example: cross-attention shapes and cost

Source of 10 tokens, target prefix of 9 tokens, 8 heads of 64.

Worked example

  1. Queries from the decoder: [9, 64] per head; keys and values from the encoder output: [10, 64].
  2. Weights per head: [9, 10]: each target position distributes attention over the 10 source tokens.
  3. All 6 decoder layers attend to the same encoder output, so the encoder runs once per sentence and its K and V can be computed once.
  4. Unlike a single summary vector (seq2seq), every source position stays available to every target step.
Cross-attention replaces the fixed-length bottleneck with a learned, per-word lookup into the source.

6 · Why it works

Why it works

No bottleneck

The source is never squeezed into one vector. Every target position can read all n source vectors.

Fresh focus at every position and layer

Each target position computes its own weights over the source, and each of the 6 decoder layers can look again with refined queries.

Several alignments at once

It is multi-head attention, so different heads can track different source relationships.

A proven idea, built in

It "mimics the typical encoder-decoder attention mechanisms" of earlier seq2seq models (§3.2.3), now as one of three uniform attention uses.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Encoder-decoder attention is masked like decoder self-attention."

It is not masked: every decoder position may attend to all source positions. The source is fully known.

"Each decoder layer attends to the matching encoder layer (layer 3 to layer 3)."

All decoder layers attend to the final encoder output (§3.2.3).

"The attention weights form a hard word-to-word alignment."

They are soft: a probability distribution over all source positions, and each head has its own.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Q from decoder, K/V from encoder. The only place the two stacks connect.
  • Compute encoder K/V once. Reused for every generated token.
  • No causal mask here. The whole source is known.

⚠ Traps

  • Masking the source. Only padding is masked in cross-attention.
  • Thinking alignment is hard-coded. It is learned, like Bahdanau attention.
  • Forgetting it's per layer. Every decoder layer has its own cross-attention.
8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Answer from memory. If you can't, revisit the slide named in the answer.

Which sub-layer of a decoder layer is encoder-decoder attention?

Second: after masked self-attention and before the FFN (feed-forward network).

For m target tokens and n source tokens, what is the shape of one head's attention-weight matrix?

m × n: one row per target query, one column per source key.

During generation, does the encoder run again for every new target token?

No. The source is fixed, so the encoder runs once and its output (the memory) is reused by every decoder pass (deck 12).
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Queries from the decoder, keys and values from the encoder output: every target position can look at the whole source, in every decoder layer.
Say it in 30 seconds

In encoder-decoder attention, the decoder's states provide the queries, and the final encoder output provides the keys and values. Each target position therefore computes a soft alignment over all source positions, in every one of the 6 decoder layers, with multiple heads. It replaces the single fixed context vector of early seq2seq models and is not masked, because the whole source is known.

Problem: the decoder needs the relevant source words at every step, and a single context vector is a bottleneck.
Core idea: Q = decoder states and K, V = encoder memory, in every decoder layer, so each target position attends over the whole source.
