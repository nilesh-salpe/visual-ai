Scheduling a GPU cluster

What a cluster scheduler decides and why GPU (graphics processing unit) training makes it hard: queues and priorities, quotas, all-at-once (gang) placement, topology, backfill and preemption.

What is the problem?Thousands of GPUs, many teams, and jobs from one GPU for an hour to thousands of GPUs for weeks. Starting jobs in arrival order leaves GPUs idle behind big jobs; starting them anywhere scatters a training job across slow links; partial starts deadlock.
Core ideaA scheduler orders the queue by priority (age, fair share, quality of service), admits jobs within quotas, places each job all at once on nodes that are close in the network, fills idle gaps with backfill (short jobs that cannot delay the reserved start of the job at the head), and preempts low-priority work when urgent work arrives.

1 · Problem

First come, first served wastes GPUs

Head-of-line blocking

A 1,024-GPU job at the front waits for GPUs to drain while small jobs behind it could have run.

Fragmentation

Free GPUs scattered over half-used nodes cannot host a job that needs whole nodes in one network block (see the GPU sharing deck).

Partial starts

Two distributed jobs that each get half their GPUs both wait forever.

The GPU decks cover hardware sharing (MIG (Multi-Instance GPU, hardware partitioning of one GPU), time-slicing) and failure rates; this topic is about the scheduler that decides who runs where and when.

2 · Core idea

Order, admit, place, fill, preempt

Priority

A score per job: in Slurm a weighted sum of age, fair-share, job size, partition, QOS (quality of service) and more, each normalised to [0, 1].

Quotas and fair share

Each team gets a share; usage decays over time (Slurm's default half-life is 7 days) so heavy recent users wait longer.

Gang and topology

All of a job's GPUs start together, on nodes under as few switches as possible.

Backfill and preemption

Short jobs run in gaps if they cannot delay the reserved job; urgent work can requeue or cancel lower-priority jobs.

Policy Starts a later job when Risk
Strict FIFO every job ahead has started idle GPUs behind a big job

5 · Trade-offs

Trade-offs

Choice Benefit Cost
Exclusive whole nodes no interference; simple topology idle GPUs when jobs need fewer than a node
Shared nodes / fractional GPUs higher utilisation for small jobs noisy neighbours; fragmentation for big jobs
Strict topology placement fast collectives longer waits for a fitting block
Aggressive preemption urgent work starts fast lost progress unless jobs checkpoint
Backfill with padded time limits safe reservations fewer gaps filled; users learn to under-request
6 · Failure modes

Failure modes

Failure Symptom Mitigation
Starvation a large job never starts reservations (backfill), age factor in priority
Partial-allocation deadlock two jobs each hold half their GPUs gang admission: all or nothing
Bad time limits backfill disabled in practice; reservations wrong enforce limits; default limits per partition
Quota gaming many small jobs to grab share fair share on usage, per-user limits
Scattered placement jobs run slowly across many switches topology-aware placement with a wait limit
7 · Challenges in production

In production

Measure the queue

Track wait time by job size, utilisation, and GPU-hours lost to fragmentation and preemption.

Separate pools

Large pretraining runs often get dedicated reservations; research and inference share the rest.

Make jobs restartable

Preemption and failures are routine; checkpointing turns them into delays, not lost weeks.

Explain decisions

Show users why a job is pending (priority, resources, reservation).

Scheduling Configuration Guide (Slurm 26.05)
SchedMD · slurm.schedmd.com 2026

The main scheduler tests jobs in strict priority order per partition (sched_interval 60 s); backfill starts lower-priority jobs that do not delay higher-priority ones and needs realistic time limits (bf_interval 30 s, bf_window 1,440 min, bf_max_job_test 100, bf_resolution 60 s).

Multifactor Priority Plugin
SchedMD · slurm.schedmd.com 2026

Priority = weighted sum of factors in [0, 1] (age, association, fair-share, job size, partition, QOS, site, TRES (trackable resources)) minus nice; every weight defaults to 0; PriorityDecayHalfLife defaults to 7 days; Fair Tree is the default fair-share algorithm since 19.05.

Kueue overview
Kubernetes SIG (special interest group) Scheduling · kueue.sigs.k8s.io 2026

Kubernetes-native job queueing: decides when a job waits, is admitted or is preempted; quotas and fair sharing, resource flavors, admission checks, all-or-nothing scheduling with a timeout, topology-aware scheduling; integrates with Jobs, JobSet, Ray and Kubeflow Trainer.

Setup All-or-nothing with ready Pods (waitForPodsReady)
Kubernetes SIG Scheduling · kueue.sigs.k8s.io 2026

Kueue watches an admitted workload until all its pods are ready; if not within the timeout (default 30 minutes) it is evicted and requeued with exponential backoff (60 s up to 3,600 s), which breaks partial-allocation deadlocks between distributed jobs.
