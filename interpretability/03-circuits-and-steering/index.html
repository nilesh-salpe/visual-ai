Interpretability · 03

Circuits, probes and steering

From features to mechanisms: causal interventions that locate where a model computes something, circuits like indirect-object identification, attribution graphs in Claude, linear probes, and steering vectors that change behaviour at inference.

What is the problem?Knowing which features exist does not tell you how the model gets from input to output, or whether a representation is actually used. To explain or edit behaviour you need to find the components that cause it.
Core ideaIntervene on activations. Activation patching copies activations from one input into a run on another and measures the effect, locating the components that matter; combined, they form a circuit. Attribution graphs trace feature-to-feature paths through a replacement model. Linear probes test whether information is present. Steering vectors add a direction to activations to change behaviour without training.

2 · Limitation

Limits of looking

Probes find too much

A classifier can decode a property from activations that the model never uses.

Attention patterns

Where a head attends is not the same as what it contributes.

Features alone

An SAE (sparse autoencoder) dictionary lists parts, not how they connect.

5 · How it works

Worked example: a steering vector

Activation Addition (ActAdd): steer sentiment using one pair of prompts.

Worked example

  1. Run the model on "Love" and on "Hate" and record the activations at a chosen layer.
  2. Steering vector = activation("Love") − activation("Hate").
  3. On a new prompt, add a multiple of this vector at the same layer during the forward pass.
  4. Outputs shift toward positive sentiment; ActAdd reported state-of-the-art negative-to-positive shift and detoxification on LLaMA-3 and OPT while preserving off-target performance.
A single contrastive pair can define a direction that changes behaviour, with no training at all.
6 · Why it works

Why interventions matter

Causality

Patching and ablation show necessity and sufficiency, which observation cannot.

Editing and control

Knowing where a computation happens lets you change it (ROME) or steer it (ActAdd).

Safety audits

Mechanisms can reveal hidden reasoning or shortcuts that outputs and chains of thought do not show.

Incomplete

Even the best tools explain only part of behaviour on a fraction of prompts; replacement models are approximations.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"If a probe can read it, the model uses it."

Probes test presence, not use; patching tests use.

"Each behaviour has one circuit."

Attribution graphs found shortcut paths alongside multi-step ones.

"Steering requires finetuning."

ActAdd needs no optimisation: one contrast pair defines the vector.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Patch, do not just observe. Causal interventions separate used information from mere correlation.
  • Contrast pairs. Steering and probing directions come from differences between matched inputs.
  • Judge circuits by faithfulness, completeness, minimality. The IOI (indirect object identification) study used all three criteria.

⚠ Traps

  • Probe accuracy ≠ use. A probe can decode information the model never uses.
  • One mechanism per behaviour. Models often have parallel paths and shortcuts (attribution graphs found both).
  • Over-steering. Large steering coefficients degrade fluency and off-target abilities.

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Interpretability in the Wild: a Circuit for Indirect Object Identification in GPT-2 small
Wang et al. · ICLR 2023

A circuit of 26 attention heads in 7 classes, found with causal interventions and evaluated for faithfulness, completeness and minimality.

Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT
Meng et al. · NeurIPS 2022

Causal tracing finds factual recall in mid-layer feed-forward modules at subject tokens; Rank-One Model Editing (ROME) updates a fact by modifying those weights.

On the Biology of a Large Language Model
Lindsey et al. (Anthropic) · Transformer Circuits Thread 2025

Attribution graphs on a replacement model of Claude 3.5 Haiku: multi-hop reasoning (Dallas → Texas → Austin) alongside shortcuts, planning rhymes ahead, shared multilingual circuits; satisfying insight for about a quarter of prompts tried.

Steering Language Models With Activation Engineering
Turner et al. · arXiv 2023

Activation Addition: the activation difference between contrasting prompts (e.g. "Love" − "Hate") added during the forward pass steers outputs; state-of-the-art sentiment shift and detoxification on LLaMA-3 and OPT with no optimisation.

Representation Engineering: A Top-Down Approach to AI (artificial intelligence) Transparency
Zou et al. · arXiv 2023

Reads and controls population-level representations (honesty, harmlessness, power-seeking) rather than neurons or circuits.

In-context Learning and Induction Heads
Olsson et al. (Anthropic) · Transformer Circuits Thread 2022

Induction heads complete [A][B] ... [A] → [B]; they form at the same point as a sharp rise in in-context learning (a bump in the training loss); causal evidence in small attention-only models, correlational in larger ones.
