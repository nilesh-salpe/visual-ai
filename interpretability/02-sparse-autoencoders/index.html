Interpretability · 02

Sparse autoencoders

Dictionary learning for language models: training a wide, sparse autoencoder on activations to recover interpretable features, from a one-layer transformer to Claude 3 Sonnet and GPT-4, and steering behaviour by clamping features.

What is the problem?If features are packed in superposition, neither neurons nor principal components give one-concept units. We need a method that finds an overcomplete set of sparse directions, at the scale of production models.
Core ideaTrain a sparse autoencoder (SAE) to reconstruct a layer's activations through a much wider hidden layer where only a few latents are active per token (L1 penalty, or keep the top k). Each decoder direction is a candidate feature. Inspect what activates it, then test causality by clamping it during the forward pass.
1 · Problem

Finding features in superposition

Superposition means a layer's activation vector is a sparse sum of many feature directions that overlap. Given only activations, can we recover those directions?

Overcomplete

There are more features than dimensions, so we need more directions than the layer has.

Sparse

Only a few features should be active on any token.

Production scale

Frontier models have millions of features across many layers.

2 · Limitation

Why simpler decompositions fail

Neurons

Polysemantic: each mixes several features.

PCA / ICA

At most d directions, chosen for variance or independence rather than sparsity of concepts.

Supervised probes

Find only the concepts you already thought to label.

4 · Core idea

Dictionary learning at scale

The model

f = ReLU(W_enc(x − b_dec) + b_enc), x̂ = W_dec f + b_dec; loss = reconstruction error + λ‖f‖₁ (or keep only the top k latents).

Towards Monosemanticity

A one-layer transformer's 512-neuron MLP (multilayer perceptron); SAEs from 512 to 131,072 features trained on 8 billion activations; features for Arabic script, DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid), base64, Hebrew.

Scaling Monosemanticity

Claude 3 Sonnet, middle-layer residual stream; ~1M, ~4M and ~34M features; fewer than 300 active per token; at least 65% of variance explained; features are multilingual and fire on images too.

Other labs

OpenAI: k-sparse SAEs with clean scaling laws, a 16M-latent SAE on GPT-4 trained on 40B tokens. DeepMind: Gemma Scope, open JumpReLU SAEs for every layer of Gemma 2 2B and 9B.

5 · How it works

Bigger dictionaries, more dead features paper numbers

Share of features never active on a sample of 10⁷ tokens at the end of training, for Anthropic's three Claude 3 Sonnet SAEs. Dead latents waste capacity; OpenAI's top-k changes reduced them even at the largest scales they tried.

In the notebook, a 16-latent SAE recovers 8 features hidden in 4 dimensions (mean cosine about 0.94) where PCA (principal component analysis) reaches about 0.82.

5 · How it works

Worked example: a feature in Claude 3 Sonnet

Anthropic trained SAEs with about 1M, 4M and 34M features on Claude 3 Sonnet's middle-layer residual stream.

Worked example

  1. Feature 34M/31164353 fires most strongly on references to the Golden Gate Bridge, more weakly on other bridges and tourist attractions.
  2. It also fires on images of the bridge, though the SAE was trained only on text activations.
  3. Clamping the feature high during the forward pass made the model bring up the bridge in most replies ("Golden Gate Claude", a 24-hour demo).
  4. The same method surfaced safety-relevant features: code backdoors, bias, deception, power-seeking, sycophancy, dangerous content.
Activation examples suggest what a feature means; clamping it tests whether the model actually uses it.
6 · Why it works

Why SAEs work, and what they miss

Matches superposition

An overcomplete sparse code is exactly the structure superposition predicts.

Unsupervised

Features emerge without labels, including ones nobody thought to look for.

Causal tests

Clamping or ablating features checks that the model really uses them (Golden Gate Claude).

Gaps

Reconstruction is partial, many latents can be dead, and features depend on dictionary size and training choices.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"SAE features are the model's true atoms."

They are one useful decomposition; different sizes split or merge features, and reconstruction is incomplete.

"Finding a deception feature means the model is deceptive."

Anthropic cautions that knowing about lies differs from lying; existence is not behaviour.

"Bigger SAEs are always better."

They find finer features but more dead latents and higher cost.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Two objectives. Judge an SAE on reconstruction and sparsity together, plus interpretability.
  • Top-k sparsity. k-sparse SAEs fix the number of active latents directly, simplifying tuning (OpenAI).
  • Test by intervention. Clamp or ablate a feature and watch the output.

⚠ Traps

  • Dead latents. In the 34M-feature SAE about 65% of features never fired on 10⁷ tokens.
  • Existence ≠ behaviour. A "deception" feature shows the model represents the concept, not that it lies.
  • Partial reconstruction. Anthropic's SAEs explained at least 65% of activation variance; the rest is unexplained.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

A sparse autoencoder turns a layer's tangled activations into many sparse, mostly interpretable features.
Say it in 30 seconds

Sparse autoencoders learn an overcomplete dictionary for a layer's activations: a wide hidden layer with few active latents per token. Towards Monosemanticity trained them on a one-layer transformer's 512-neuron MLP, with 512 to 131,072 features, and found features for Arabic script, DNA and base64. Scaling Monosemanticity applied them to Claude 3 Sonnet with up to about 34M features, fewer than 300 active per token, explaining at least 65% of variance; clamping the Golden Gate Bridge feature changed behaviour. OpenAI trained a 16M-latent top-k SAE on GPT-4; DeepMind released open SAEs on every layer of Gemma 2.

Scale: 1M-34M features on Claude 3 Sonnet; 16M on GPT-4

Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Towards Monosemanticity: Decomposing Language Models With Dictionary Learning
Bricken et al. (Anthropic) · Transformer Circuits Thread 2023

Sparse autoencoders on the 512-neuron MLP of a one-layer transformer, trained on 8 billion activations, with 512 to 131,072 features (analysis on 4,096); features for Arabic script, DNA, base64 and Hebrew; a single neuron mixes academic citations, English dialogue, HTTP (Hypertext Transfer Protocol) requests and Korean text.

Sparse Autoencoders Find Highly Interpretable Features in Language Models
Cunningham et al. · ICLR 2024

SAE features are more interpretable and monosemantic than alternative decompositions, and pinpoint the features causally responsible for indirect-object identification more finely.

Scaling Monosemanticity: Extracting Interpretable Features from Claude 3 Sonnet
Templeton et al. (Anthropic) · Transformer Circuits Thread 2024

SAEs on the middle-layer residual stream of Claude 3 Sonnet with ~1M, ~4M and ~34M features; fewer than 300 active per token, at least 65% of variance explained; multilingual and multimodal features; safety-relevant features (code backdoors, bias, deception, power-seeking, sycophancy); feature clamping steers behaviour.

Remember: dead features ≈2% (1M), 35% (4M), 65% (34M)
Golden Gate Claude
Anthropic · Anthropic News 2024

Amplifying the Golden Gate Bridge feature in Claude 3 Sonnet made most replies mention the bridge; available as a 24-hour research demo.

Scaling and evaluating sparse autoencoders
Gao et al. (OpenAI) · arXiv 2024

k-sparse (top-k) autoencoders control sparsity directly and, with other changes, leave few dead latents; clean scaling laws in size and sparsity; new feature-quality metrics.

Remember: a 16 million latent SAE on GPT-4 activations, trained on 40 billion tokens
Gemma Scope: Open Sparse Autoencoders Everywhere All At Once on Gemma 2
Lieberum et al. (Google DeepMind) · arXiv 2024

Open JumpReLU SAEs trained on all layers and sub-layers of Gemma 2 2B and 9B (and select layers of 27B).
