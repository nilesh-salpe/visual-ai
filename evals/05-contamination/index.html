Evaluating LLMs · 05

Benchmark contamination

When test questions leak into training data the score partly measures memorization. How to detect overlap, how to report it, and how fresh test sets reveal overfitting.

What is the problem?Pretraining corpora are scraped from the web, and benchmarks are published on the web. Test items (or close variants) can end up in training data, inflating scores in ways that don't reflect real capability.
Core ideaDetect overlap (GPT-3 flagged any 13-gram match with the training set), report clean-subset scores next to full ones, and validate on fresh or held-out test sets that can't have leaked.
1 · Problem

How leakage inflates a score simulation

A model with true skill 55% that answers leaked items correctly 95% of the time (memorized). Move the fraction of the test set that leaked into training.

2 · Limitation

Why it is hard to avoid

Web-scale data

Benchmarks, their solutions and discussions of them are posted online and get scraped along with everything else.

Filtering is imperfect

GPT-3 tried to remove overlaps with its benchmarks, but "a bug in the filtering caused us to ignore some overlaps, and due to the cost of training it was not feasible to retrain the model."

Paraphrases escape detection

Exact n-gram matching misses translated, reworded or reformatted copies of test items.

3 · Your turn

Reading a contamination report

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

A model scores 80% on a benchmark, 81% on the flagged ("dirty") items and 80% on the clean items. What does that suggest?

B. If clean-subset performance matches the full score, the overlapping items aren't giving the model an advantage. That is GPT-3's method: compare scores on the clean subset with the full benchmark. GPT-3 found contamination "has a minimal effect" on most datasets, while flagging some where it did matter.
4 · Core idea

Detect, report, validate

Detect

GPT-3 flagged test examples "that have a 13-gram overlap with anything in the pretraining set (or that overlap with the whole example when it is shorter than 13-grams)". Deliberately conservative.

Report

Score the full set, the clean subset and the dirty subset. A big gap between clean and full means the score is partly memorization.

Validate

Build new test sets the model can't have seen: GSM1k mirrors GSM8K (Grade School Math 8K)'s style with fresh problems. Models that overfit GSM8K drop on it.

What GSM1k found

On fresh, GSM8K-style problems, the authors "observe accuracy drops of up to 8%, with several families of models showing evidence of systematic overfitting across almost all model sizes", while frontier models (they name Gemini, GPT and Claude) "show little to no signs of overfitting". Contamination is not uniform: it depends on how each model's data was collected.

At a glance

Detect, report, validate.

5 · How it works

Worked example: how much does leakage inflate a score?

True skill 55%. Leaked items are answered correctly 95% of the time (memorized).

Worked example

  1. 10% leaked: 0.1 × 0.95 + 0.9 × 0.55 = 59.0% reported (+4 points).
  2. 20% leaked: 63.0% (+8 points).
  3. The clean subset still scores ~55%: the gap between full and clean scores is the inflation.
  4. A fresh, equivalent test set would also show ~55%.
Report clean-subset scores next to full ones, and confirm on fresh data when it matters.
6 · Why it works

Why it matters

Scores stop meaning capability

A memorized answer says nothing about solving a new problem of the same kind, which is what the benchmark is supposed to predict.

Comparisons become unfair

Models trained on different data are contaminated to different degrees, so rankings mix skill with exposure.

It compounds over time

The longer a benchmark is public, the more copies, solutions and discussions exist on the web.

Fresh tests restore trust

A gap between an old benchmark and a fresh equivalent (GSM8K vs GSM1k) is direct evidence of overfitting.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"If the benchmark was filtered from the training data, it is clean."

Filters miss paraphrases and can have bugs, as GPT-3's did. Report clean-subset results and use fresh sets.

"Contamination always inflates scores a lot."

GPT-3 found minimal effect on most benchmarks, and GSM1k found frontier models barely affected. It varies by model and dataset, so measure it.

"A shorter n-gram makes detection strictly better."

Short n-grams match by coincidence in real text, producing false positives. GPT-3 chose 13 to stay conservative.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Inflation = frac × (memorized − true). Quick estimate of the effect.
  • 13-grams. Conservative overlap flag, as in GPT-3.
  • Fresh sets reveal overfitting. GSM1k: drops of up to 8% for some families.

⚠ Traps

  • Filtered = clean. Filters miss paraphrases and can have bugs (GPT-3 had one).
  • Short n-grams. Coincidental matches create false positives in real text.
  • Assuming it always matters a lot. GPT-3 and GSM1k found it varies by model and dataset; measure it.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Contaminated test items turn benchmarks into memory tests: detect overlap (13-grams), report clean-subset scores, and confirm on fresh test sets.
Say it in 30 seconds

Benchmarks live on the web, and so does training data, so test items leak. A model that memorized leaked items looks better than it is, and the effect differs between models. GPT-3 flagged any test example sharing a 13-gram with its training data and compared scores on the clean and dirty subsets; it found little effect on most benchmarks, after a filtering bug had left some overlap in. n-gram matching misses paraphrases, so the strongest evidence comes from fresh test sets: on GSM1k, new GSM8K-style problems, some model families dropped by up to 8 percent while frontier models barely changed.
