Evaluating LLMs: key papers

Every paper cited on the decks' Key papers slides, in the order the guide introduces them. Each entry links to the paper and to the decks that use it.

Holistic Evaluation of Language Models (HELM)
Liang et al. · TMLR 2023 · cited in What an eval measures

Standardized evaluation of many models across many scenarios and seven metrics, not accuracy alone.

Remember: 7 metrics × 16 core scenarios; 30 models on 42 scenarios
Quantifying Language Models' Sensitivity to Spurious Features in Prompt Design
Sclar et al. · ICLR 2024 · cited in What an eval measures

Shows equivalent prompt formats (separators, casing, spacing) change accuracy dramatically, and proposes reporting the spread (FormatSpread).

Remember: up to 76 accuracy points (LLaMA-2-13B)
Measuring Massive Multitask Language Understanding (MMLU)
Hendrycks et al. · ICLR 2021 · cited in Benchmarks and their failure modes

A 57-task multiple-choice benchmark spanning STEM (science, technology, engineering and mathematics), humanities and professional subjects.

Remember: GPT-3 175B ≈ chance + 20 points at release
MMLU-Pro: A More Robust and Challenging Multi-Task Language Understanding Benchmark
Wang et al. · NeurIPS 2024 · cited in Benchmarks and their failure modes

A harder, reasoning-focused successor with ten options per question and noisy questions removed.

Remember: accuracy drops 16–33% vs MMLU
GPQA: A Graduate-Level Google-Proof Q&A Benchmark
Rein et al. · COLM 2024 · cited in Benchmarks and their failure modes

448 expert-written science questions that skilled non-experts can't answer even with web search.

Remember: experts 65%, non-experts 34%, GPT-4 39%
SWE-bench: Can Language Models Resolve Real-World GitHub Issues?
Jimenez et al. · ICLR 2024 · cited in Benchmarks and their failure modes, pass@k and sampling metrics

Real GitHub issues: the model edits the repository and the project's own tests decide success.

Remember: 2,294 issues; Claude 2 solved 1.96% at release
Evaluating Large Language Models Trained on Code (Codex, HumanEval)
Chen et al. · arXiv 2021 · cited in pass@k and sampling metrics

HumanEval's 164 hand-written problems scored by unit tests, and the unbiased pass@k estimator.

Remember: Codex-12B: 28.8% pass@1, 70.2% with 100 samples
Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations
Miller · arXiv 2024 · cited in Error bars and comparing models, Experiments in LLM training

Standard errors from the central limit theorem, clustered standard errors, paired comparisons and power analysis for evals.

Remember: clustered SEs can be over 3× naive ones
Chatbot Arena: An Open Platform for Evaluating LLMs (large language models) by Human Preference
Chiang et al. · ICML 2024 · cited in Error bars and comparing models, Arenas and ratings

Crowdsourced anonymous pairwise votes on real prompts, ranked with Bradley-Terry maximum likelihood and confidence intervals.

Remember: over 240K votes
Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3)
Brown et al. · NeurIPS 2020 · cited in Contamination

Besides in-context learning, a systematic contamination study: 13-gram overlap flags and clean vs dirty scores.

Remember: 13-gram overlap; a filtering bug left some overlap in
A Careful Examination of Large Language Model Performance on Grade School Arithmetic (GSM1k)
Zhang et al. · arXiv 2024 · cited in Contamination

A fresh set of GSM8K-style problems that exposes models overfit to the public benchmark.

Remember: accuracy drops of up to 8%
Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena
Zheng et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · cited in LLM-as-judge, Arenas and ratings

Measures how well strong LLMs judge open-ended answers, and documents position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases.

Remember: >80% agreement with humans; GPT-4 position consistency 65%
Length-Controlled AlpacaEval: A Simple Way to Debias Automatic Evaluators
Dubois et al. · arXiv 2024 · cited in LLM-as-judge

Removes the judge's length preference by predicting win rates as if both answers had the same length.

Remember: Spearman with Chatbot Arena 0.94 → 0.98
Quantifying Variance in Evaluation Benchmarks
Madaan et al. · arXiv 2024 · cited in Experiments in LLM training

Ten Llama-2-7B-architecture models trained for 210B tokens that differ only in seed: seed standard deviation ARC-C 0.80, HellaSwag 0.21, GSM8K (Grade School Math 8K) 0.41, MMLU 0.57, MMLU-Cloze 0.22; gains should exceed seed variance; use cloze formulations for pretraining ablations.

Signal and Noise: A Framework for Reducing Uncertainty in Language Model Evaluation
Heineman et al. · arXiv 2025 · cited in Experiments in LLM training

Signal (relative dispersion of scores across models of similar compute) over noise (relative std of one run's final checkpoints) predicts small-to-large decision accuracy; perplexity beats accuracy; filtering noisy subtasks and averaging checkpoints help. 30 benchmarks, 375 models from 60M to 32B.

Remember: SNR vs decision accuracy R = 0.791; checkpoint averaging 68.9% → 71.3% decision accuracy (30-task average)
DataDecide: How to Predict Best Pretraining Data with Small Experiments
Magnusson et al. · arXiv 2025 · cited in Experiments in LLM training

Controlled pretraining over 25 corpora, up to 100B tokens and 1B parameters, 3 seeds: rankings at a single small scale are a strong baseline; no scaling-law method among 8 baselines beats it for the same compute; continuous likelihood metrics make benchmarks highly predictable.

Remember: 150M rankings predict ~80% of 1B comparisons; >80% predictable with 0.01% of the compute
Random Search for Hyper-Parameter Optimization
Bergstra & Bengio · JMLR 2012 · cited in Experiments in LLM training

Randomly chosen trials are more efficient than a grid because only a few hyperparameters really matter, and which ones differ by dataset.

Hyperband: A Novel Bandit-Based Approach to Hyperparameter Optimization
Li et al. · JMLR 2018 · cited in Experiments in LLM training

Speeds up random search with adaptive resource allocation and early stopping, framed as a pure-exploration bandit problem.

Remember: over an order-of-magnitude speedup over the Bayesian-optimisation competitor set
Torch.manual_seed(3407) is all you need: On the influence of random seeds in deep learning architectures for computer vision
Picard · arXiv 2021 · cited in Experiments in LLM training

Scanning up to 10^4 seeds on CIFAR-10 (and fewer on ImageNet) shows it is surprisingly easy to find outlier seeds that perform much better or worse than average.

Why Language Models Hallucinate
Kalai, Nachum, Vempala, Zhang · arXiv (OpenAI) 2025 · cited in Hallucination and factuality

Hallucinations arise as ordinary classification errors (at least the singleton rate for arbitrary facts) and persist because binary-graded benchmarks reward guessing; proposes explicit confidence targets with penalty t/(1 − t).

Remember: answer only if confidence > t; errors cost t/(1 − t) points
A Survey on Hallucination in Large Language Models: Principles, Taxonomy, Challenges, and Open Questions
Huang et al. · ACM TOIS 2025 · cited in Hallucination and factuality

Taxonomy: factuality hallucination (factual inconsistency, fabrication) vs faithfulness hallucination (instruction, context and logical inconsistency); causes, detection and mitigation.

Remember: two primary types: factuality and faithfulness
TruthfulQA: Measuring How Models Mimic Human Falsehoods
Lin, Hilton, Evans · ACL 2022 · cited in Hallucination and factuality

Questions some people answer falsely because of misconceptions; larger models were generally less truthful.

Remember: 817 questions, 38 categories; best model 58% truthful vs 94% human
FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation
Min et al. · EMNLP 2023 · cited in Hallucination and factuality

Splits a generation into atomic facts and reports the share supported by a knowledge source; automated estimator for large-scale evaluation.

Remember: biographies: InstructGPT 42.5%, ChatGPT 58.3%, PerplexityAI 71.5%; ChatGPT 80% → 16% as entities get rarer
Measuring short-form factuality in large language models (SimpleQA)
Wei et al. · arXiv (OpenAI) 2024 · cited in Hallucination and factuality

Short fact-seeking questions with one indisputable answer, collected adversarially against GPT-4o; graded correct, incorrect or not attempted.

Remember: 4,326 questions; GPT-4o 38.2% correct / 60.8% incorrect; Claude 3.5 Sonnet 28.9% / 36.1% with 35.0% not attempted
SelfCheckGPT: Zero-Resource Black-Box Hallucination Detection for Generative Large Language Models
Manakul, Liusie, Gales · EMNLP 2023 · cited in Hallucination and factuality

Detects hallucinated sentences by checking whether stochastically sampled responses support them (BERTScore, QA, n-gram, NLI (natural language inference) or LLM-prompt variants); needs no output probabilities or external database.

Remember: black-box: sampled text only
Semantic Uncertainty: Linguistic Invariances for Uncertainty Estimation in Natural Language Generation
Kuhn, Gal, Farquhar · ICLR 2023 · cited in Hallucination and factuality

Clusters sampled answers by meaning with bidirectional entailment and computes entropy over meanings; more predictive of QA accuracy than token-level baselines.

Remember: unsupervised; one off-the-shelf model
Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know
Kadavath et al. · arXiv (Anthropic) 2022 · cited in Hallucination and factuality

Larger models are well calibrated on multiple-choice and true/false questions in the right format; models can estimate P(True) for their own answers and be trained to predict P(IK).

Remember: P(True) self-evaluation improves when the model sees several of its own samples
Chain-of-Verification Reduces Hallucination in Large Language Models
Dhuliawala et al. · arXiv (Meta AI (artificial intelligence)) 2023 · cited in Hallucination and factuality

Draft, plan verification questions, answer them independently, then revise; reduces hallucination on list questions, closed-book QA and long-form generation.

Remember: Wikidata list precision 0.17 → 0.36 (Llama 65B, two-step CoVe)
Does Fine-Tuning LLMs on New Knowledge Encourage Hallucinations?
Gekhman et al. · EMNLP 2024 · cited in Hallucination and factuality

Fine-tuning examples with knowledge the model lacks are learned much more slowly than known ones and, once learned, linearly increase hallucination; early stopping mitigates it.

Remember: models acquire new facts through pretraining, mostly not fine-tuning
Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP (natural language processing) Tasks
Lewis et al. · NeurIPS 2020 · cited in Hallucination and factuality

Combines a seq2seq generator with a dense retriever over Wikipedia (non-parametric memory), trained end to end.

Reduce hallucinations (Claude documentation)
Anthropic · Claude platform docs 2026 · cited in Hallucination and factuality

Allow "I don't know", extract word-for-word quotes first for documents over 20k tokens, require citations and retract unsupported claims, compare several runs.

Remember: quote-first grounding for long documents (>20k tokens)
