Evaluating LLMs · 01

What an eval measures

An eval score is the result of a task, a prompt, a sampling setup, a scoring rule and a sample of questions. Change any one and the number changes.

What is the problem?Teams ship, rank and choose models based on single eval numbers, yet those numbers depend on many choices that are rarely reported, and accuracy alone hides most of what matters.
Core ideaTreat every score as a measurement with a protocol: what capability, which questions, which prompt and decoding, which scoring rule, how much uncertainty. Measure more than accuracy, and report the setup with the number.

2 · Limitation

Why a single number misleads

Prompt sensitivity

Sclar et al. found accuracy varying by up to 76 points for LLaMA-2-13B across semantically equivalent prompt formats (separators, spacing, casing). A comparison that uses one format per model can be decided by formatting.

Accuracy isn't everything

A model can be accurate but badly calibrated, brittle to typos, biased or slow. HELM (Holistic Evaluation of Language Models) measures 7 metrics per scenario precisely so the trade-offs stay visible.

Sampling error

Benchmarks are finite samples of questions. With 1,000 questions at 70% accuracy, the 95% interval is about ±2.8 points, often wider than the gap being celebrated (deck 04).

3 · Your turn

Same model, different numbers

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

Two papers report different MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding) scores for the same model. Which is the LEAST likely explanation?

D. Same weights, but every other item is a common source of differences. Prompt format alone can swing results by tens of points (Sclar et al.), and how the answer is extracted and scored differs between evaluation harnesses.
4 · Core idea

A score is a measurement protocol

Validity

Does the task measure the capability you care about? Multiple-choice trivia measures recognition, not the ability to write a correct answer from scratch.

Reliability

Would you get the same number again with other prompts, seeds or a fresh sample of questions? Report standard errors and sensitivity, not just a point estimate.

Coverage

Measure several properties (accuracy, calibration, robustness, safety, cost) across several scenarios, so a gain in one isn't hiding a loss in another.

HELM's design

HELM measures 7 metrics (accuracy, calibration, robustness, fairness, bias, toxicity and efficiency) on 16 core scenarios "to the extent possible (87.5% of the time)", and evaluates 30 prominent models on 42 scenarios under standardized conditions. The point is to make the full trade-off visible rather than one leaderboard column.

5 · How it works

Worked example: is a 2-point lead real?

Model A scores 72% and model B 70% on a 1,000-question benchmark, each in one prompt format.

Worked example

  1. 95% CI (confidence interval) half-width at ~71%: 1.96 × √(0.71 × 0.29 / 1,000) = ±2.8 points.
  2. The 2-point gap is inside either model's interval; a paired test on the same questions is needed (deck 04).
  3. Prompt format alone can move scores by tens of points (up to 76 for LLaMA-2-13B in Sclar et al.).
  4. Evaluate both models over several equivalent formats and report the mean and spread.
Before calling a winner: same protocol, standard errors, a paired comparison, and the format spread.
6 · Why it works

Why the protocol view helps

Comparisons become fair

Same prompts, same scoring and same questions for every model, as in HELM's standardized setup, make differences interpretable.

Uncertainty becomes visible

With standard errors and format spreads you can tell a real improvement from noise.

Trade-offs stay honest

Seeing calibration, robustness and cost next to accuracy stops a model from winning on one axis by losing on another.

Results transfer

A score whose protocol you know can be reproduced and compared with next year's models.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"A benchmark score is a property of the model."

It is a property of the model plus the prompt, decoding, scoring and question sample. Change those and the score changes, sometimes by a lot.

"Higher accuracy means a better model, full stop."

Accuracy can trade off against calibration, robustness, safety and cost. HELM reports 7 metrics for exactly this reason.

"If two papers use the same benchmark, their numbers are comparable."

Only if the protocol matches: version, shots, template, extraction and scoring. Often it doesn't.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • CI ≈ ±1.96·√(p(1−p)/n). ±2.8 points at 70% on 1,000 questions.
  • Report the protocol. Version, shots, template, decoding, scoring, n.
  • Measure more than accuracy. HELM tracks calibration, robustness, fairness, bias, toxicity and efficiency too.

⚠ Traps

  • Comparing numbers across papers. Different harnesses and prompts make them incomparable.
  • One prompt per model. Formatting can decide the comparison.
  • Point estimates only. Gaps smaller than the error bars are noise.
8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Answer from memory. If you can't, revisit the slide named in the answer.

How many metrics does HELM measure for each core scenario?

7: accuracy, calibration, robustness, fairness, bias, toxicity, efficiency.

Name four protocol choices you would want reported with a benchmark score.

Benchmark version and number of questions; shots and prompt template; decoding (greedy or sampled, number of samples, k); answer extraction and scoring rule; standard error; contamination checks.

How would you make a prompt-format comparison robust?

Evaluate each model over a set of equivalent formats and report the mean and spread (or the full distribution), rather than one format chosen per model.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

An eval score is a measurement protocol (capability, questions, prompt, decoding, scoring, uncertainty), so report the protocol and more than one metric along with the number.
Say it in 30 seconds

A benchmark number depends on the capability the task actually measures, which questions were used, the prompt format, how answers were sampled, how they were scored, and sampling error. Prompt format alone moved LLaMA-2-13B by up to 76 accuracy points between equivalent formats, and 1,000 questions give about plus or minus 2.8 points of uncertainty. HELM's answer is to standardize the setup and measure seven properties, from accuracy and calibration to toxicity and efficiency, across many scenarios. In practice: report the protocol, standard errors and spreads, and look beyond accuracy.
