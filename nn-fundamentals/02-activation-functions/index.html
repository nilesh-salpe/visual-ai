Activation functions

The elementwise nonlinearities that make networks more than matrix products: sigmoid and tanh, ReLU (rectified linear unit), and the smooth GELU (Gaussian Error Linear Unit) and SiLU (Sigmoid Linear Unit) used in modern LLMs (large language models).

What is the problem?Without a nonlinearity a deep network is one linear map. But the wrong nonlinearity makes gradients vanish as they pass back through many layers.
Core ideaPick a φ whose derivative stays near 1 over the inputs it sees. Sigmoid and tanh saturate (σ' ≤ 1/4). ReLU passes gradient 1 for positive inputs. GELU = x·Φ(x) and SiLU = x·σ(x) are smooth versions used in BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers), GPT-2 and (as the SwiGLU (Swish-gated linear unit) gate) Llama.
1 · Problem

Shapes and slopes

Pick an activation. The solid line is φ(z), the dashed line its derivative φ′(z). The shaded band marks where |φ′| < 0.1: inputs there pass almost no gradient back.

2 · Limitation

What goes wrong with the classics

Saturation

For large |z|, sigmoid and tanh are flat: φ′ ≈ 0, so no gradient flows back.

Small maximum slope

σ′ ≤ 1/4: even at its best, each sigmoid layer cuts the gradient by 4×.

Non-zero mean

Sigmoid outputs are all positive, which biases the next layer's gradients in one direction (Glorot & Bengio).

4 · Core idea

The modern choices

ReLU

max(0, z). Cheap, non-saturating for z > 0, sparse activations (Nair & Hinton, 2010).

GELU

z·Φ(z), Φ the Gaussian CDF (cumulative distribution function). Smooth: weights inputs by value instead of gating by sign. BERT, GPT-2.

SiLU / Swish

z·σ(z), found by automated search (Ramachandran et al.). The gate inside SwiGLU.

Gated units (GLU family)

Multiply one projection by an activated other: (SiLU(xW) ⊙ xV). Shazeer (2020) found these improve transformers.

At a glance

If |φ′| is small, gradients shrink geometrically with depth.

Worked example

  1. Sigmoid: σ′(z) = σ(z)(1 − σ(z)) ≤ 0.25, so each layer can shrink the gradient 4×. Measured ratio after 30 layers: 7 × 10⁻¹⁹.
  2. tanh (derivative ≤ 1, gain 1): 0.19.
  3. ReLU with He scaling (gain 2): 4.7; GELU with gain 2: 0.61, both healthy.
  4. SiLU is sensitive to the scale: gain 2.0 gives 3 × 10⁻⁴, gain 2.25 keeps it near 1.
Non-saturating activations keep gradients usable through depth, together with the right initialisation (deck 06).
6 · Why it works

Why the ReLU family works

Slope ≈ 1 where active

Gradients pass through active units unchanged, so deep stacks stay trainable.

Cheap

max, or a multiply by a sigmoid: negligible next to the matmuls.

Smoothness helps optimisation

GELU and SiLU have no kink at 0 and let small negative values through, which tends to train slightly better at scale.

Gating adds expressiveness

Multiplicative interactions in GLU (gated linear unit) variants give each unit an input-dependent gain.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"ReLU networks are linear."

They are piecewise linear: every unit switching on or off creates a new region, so a deep ReLU net has a huge number of linear pieces.

"GELU is just a smoothed ReLU with no practical difference."

Its behaviour near zero matters at scale; GELU and SiLU variants consistently edge out ReLU in transformers.

"Vanishing gradients were solved by ReLU alone."

ReLU helps, but initialisation, normalisation and residual connections (decks 06–07) are just as important.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • ReLU′ = 1 for z > 0. Gradients pass straight through active units.
  • GELU(x) = x·Φ(x); SiLU(x) = x·σ(x). Smooth, slightly negative for small negative x.
  • SwiGLU = (SiLU(xW) ⊙ xV)W₂. The gated MLP (multilayer perceptron) in Llama-style models.

⚠ Traps

  • Sigmoid in hidden layers of deep nets. Saturation and non-zero mean kill gradients.
  • Dead ReLUs. Units stuck at z < 0 get zero gradient forever, often after too-large steps.
  • Assuming the activation choice is cosmetic. It interacts with initialisation and depth.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Activations make networks non-linear; saturating ones (sigmoid, tanh) kill gradients through depth, so modern LLMs use ReLU-like GELU and SiLU, usually inside a gated SwiGLU.
Say it in 30 seconds

An activation function is applied elementwise between linear layers so the network is not a single linear map. In the backward pass every gradient is multiplied by the activation's slope, so slopes much smaller than one compound through depth. Sigmoid's slope is at most a quarter and saturates for large inputs, which makes gradients vanish. ReLU passes slope one for positive inputs. GELU, z times the Gaussian CDF, and SiLU, z times the sigmoid, are smooth relatives; GPT-2 used GELU and Llama uses SiLU as the gate of its SwiGLU MLP.

Problem: need non-linearity without vanishing gradients.
Core idea: slope ≈ 1 where active: ReLU, GELU, SiLU.
In LLMs: GELU (GPT-2), SwiGLU (Llama).

Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Rectified Linear Units Improve Restricted Boltzmann Machines
Nair & Hinton · ICML 2010

Introduces rectified linear units, max(0, x), as hidden units; they do not saturate for positive inputs and became the default activation.

Gaussian Error Linear Units (GELUs)
Hendrycks & Gimpel · arXiv 2016

GELU(x) = x·Φ(x): weights inputs by their value instead of gating by sign; outperformed ReLU and ELU (exponential linear unit) on vision, language and speech tasks. Used in BERT and GPT-2.

Searching for Activation Functions (Swish)
Ramachandran, Zoph & Le · ICLR workshop 2018

Automated search finds x·sigmoid(βx) (Swish; SiLU at β = 1), which beats ReLU on deeper models. SiLU is the gate inside SwiGLU.

Remember: +0.9% ImageNet top-1 on Mobile NASNet-A
GLU Variants Improve Transformer
Shazeer · arXiv 2020

Replaces the Transformer FFN (feed-forward network) with gated linear units (ReGLU, GEGLU, SwiGLU…) at equal parameters by shrinking the hidden size to ⅔.

Remember: log-perplexity 1.677 (ReLU) → 1.636 (SwiGLU)
