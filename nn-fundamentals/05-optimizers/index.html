Optimizers

From SGD (stochastic gradient descent) to AdamW: how gradients become weight updates, why weight decay must be decoupled from Adam, and the warmup-plus-cosine schedule used to train LLMs (large language models).

What is the problem?Raw gradients are noisy, differently scaled across parameters, and unreliable early in training. Plain gradient steps are slow or unstable on such problems.
Core ideaSGD with momentum smooths noisy gradients. Adam also divides by a running RMS (root mean square) of each parameter's gradient. AdamW applies weight decay directly to the weights instead of through the gradient. A learning-rate schedule warms up, then decays.
1 · Problem

Shape a learning-rate schedule

Choose warmup length, decay shape and the final fraction of the peak. The curve is the learning rate over a 100,000-step run.

2 · Limitation

Why plain SGD is not enough for LLMs

Very different scales

Embedding rows, attention weights and norms receive gradients of very different sizes; one learning rate fits none.

Noise

Mini-batch gradients are noisy; following each one exactly wastes steps.

Fragile start

Early in training the curvature is high and statistics are unreliable; large steps diverge.

3 · Your turn

What does weight decay do in AdamW?

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

In AdamW, how is weight decay applied?

B. Decoupled: w ← w − η(m̂/(√v̂ + ε) + λw). Putting λw into the gradient instead would be divided by √v̂ and regularise heavily-updated weights less.
4 · Core idea

Three ingredients

Momentum

m ← β₁m + (1 − β₁)g. Averages noise, accelerates along consistent directions (Sutskever et al., 2013).

Adaptive scaling

v ← β₂v + (1 − β₂)g². Step ∝ m̂/√v̂: each parameter gets its own effective learning rate (Adam).

Decoupled decay

w ← w − ηλw separately from the gradient step (AdamW, Loshchilov & Hutter).

At a glance

AdamW + warmup + cosine decay is the default recipe for LLM pretraining.

5 · How it works

AdamW, one step

1 · moments

m = β₁m + (1 − β₁)g, v = β₂v + (1 − β₂)g².

2 · bias-correct

m̂ = m/(1 − β₁ᵗ), v̂ = v/(1 − β₂ᵗ): both start at 0.

3 · adaptive step

w −= η · m̂/(√v̂ + ε).

4 · decay

w −= η · λ · w (skip for norms and biases).

Memory cost

Adam keeps m and v for every parameter, usually in FP32 (32-bit floating point): 8 bytes per parameter on top of the weights and gradients, which is most of the "16 bytes per parameter" in Distributed deck 03.

5 · How it works

Worked example: the Llama recipe

LLaMA (Touvron et al., 2023): AdamW with β₁ = 0.9, β₂ = 0.95, weight decay 0.1, gradient clipping 1.0, 2,000 warmup steps, cosine decay to 10% of the peak.

Worked example

  1. Warmup: η grows linearly from ~0 to the peak over 2,000 steps, while Adam's v estimates settle.
  2. With peak 3 × 10⁻⁴ and 200,000 total steps: η = 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ at step 1,000, 3 × 10⁻⁴ at 2,000, ≈ 1.7 × 10⁻⁴ at 100,000 and 3 × 10⁻⁵ at the end (notebook 04).
  3. Decoupled decay shrinks every weight by η·λ per step, independently of its gradient history.
  4. Notebook 04: with λ = 0.1, putting the penalty inside Adam's gradient (L2) gives a final weight norm of 1.38, while AdamW gives 9.81: the two regularise very differently.
Adam's per-parameter scaling, decoupled weight decay and a warmup + decay schedule together make large runs stable.
6 · Why it works

Why this recipe works

Scale invariance

Adam is invariant to rescaling each gradient coordinate, so badly scaled parameters still learn.

Regularisation that behaves

Decoupled decay makes the decay strength independent of the learning rate and gradient history.

Warmup protects the start

Small early steps let Adam's statistics and the network settle before large updates.

Decay finishes the job

Shrinking η lets the weights settle into a low-loss region; cosine was popularised by SGDR (stochastic gradient descent with warm restarts).

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Adam needs no learning-rate tuning."

Its defaults are robust for small problems; LLMs still tune peak η, β₂, warmup and decay.

"Weight decay and L2 regularisation are the same."

Only for plain SGD. For Adam they differ, which is why AdamW exists.

"Schedules are a minor detail."

Warmup prevents divergence and the decay phase produces a large share of the final loss improvement.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • β₂ = 0.95 for LLMs. Faster-adapting variance than the 0.999 default.
  • Warm up, then decay. Linear warmup, cosine (or linear) decay.
  • Optimizer state = 2 extra values per parameter. m and v, in FP32: 8 bytes.

⚠ Traps

  • L2 penalty with Adam. Not weight decay; use AdamW.
  • No warmup at a high learning rate. Early instability or divergence.
  • Decaying weight decay on norms and biases. Usually excluded from decay.
8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Answer from memory. If you can't, revisit the slide named in the answer.

How many FP32 optimizer values does Adam store per parameter?

2: m and v (plus the step count, shared).

Why does Adam bias-correct m and v?

They start at 0, so early averages are biased toward 0; dividing by 1 − βᵗ removes that bias.

Why warm up the learning rate?

Early gradients and Adam's variance estimates are unreliable and curvature is high; large early steps can diverge.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

LLMs train with AdamW: momentum plus per-parameter RMS scaling, weight decay applied directly to weights, and a learning rate that warms up and then decays.
Say it in 30 seconds

Optimizers turn gradients into updates. Momentum keeps a running average of gradients to smooth noise. Adam also keeps a running average of squared gradients and divides by its square root, so each parameter gets its own effective step size, with bias corrections because both averages start at zero. AdamW applies weight decay directly to the weights rather than adding it to the gradient, where Adam's scaling would weaken it. The learning rate follows a schedule: linear warmup while statistics settle, then cosine or linear decay. Llama used beta-two of 0.95, weight decay 0.1 and 2,000 warmup steps.

Problem: noisy, badly scaled gradients; fragile start.
Core idea: momentum + RMS scaling + decoupled decay.
Schedule: warmup, then cosine to ~10%.

Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization
Kingma & Ba · ICLR 2015

Per-parameter step sizes from bias-corrected running averages of the gradient and its square; invariant to diagonal rescaling of the gradients.

Remember: defaults β₁ 0.9, β₂ 0.999, ε 10⁻⁸
Decoupled Weight Decay Regularization (AdamW)
Loshchilov & Hutter · ICLR 2019

L2 regularization and weight decay differ for adaptive optimizers; applying decay directly to the weights (AdamW) decouples it from the learning rate and improves generalization.

SGDR: Stochastic Gradient Descent with Warm Restarts
Loshchilov & Hutter · ICLR 2017

Introduces the cosine learning-rate schedule (with optional restarts), now the standard decay shape for LLM pretraining.

On the importance of initialization and momentum in deep learning
Sutskever et al. · ICML 2013

With a good initialization and a slowly increasing momentum schedule, plain SGD with (Nesterov) momentum trains deep and recurrent networks once thought to need second-order methods.

LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models
Touvron et al. · arXiv 2023

The recipe most open models followed: pre-normalization with RMSNorm (root-mean-square normalization), SwiGLU (Swish-gated linear unit) with a ⅔·4d hidden size, and rotary embeddings.

Remember: trained on 1.0–1.4T tokens
