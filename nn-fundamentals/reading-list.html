Neural Network Fundamentals: key papers

Every paper cited on the decks' Key papers slides, in the order the guide introduces them. Each entry links to the paper and to the decks that use it.

Learning representations by back-propagating errors
Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams · Nature 1986 · cited in From perceptron to MLP, Backpropagation

Backpropagation: apply the chain rule backward through a network to get every weight's gradient, so hidden units learn useful internal features.

Multilayer feedforward networks are universal approximators
Hornik, Stinchcombe & White · Neural Networks 1989 · cited in From perceptron to MLP

One hidden layer with enough units and a squashing nonlinearity can approximate any continuous function on a compact set to any accuracy.

Approximation by superpositions of a sigmoidal function
Cybenko · Mathematics of Control, Signals and Systems 1989 · cited in From perceptron to MLP

Proves universal approximation for single-hidden-layer networks with sigmoid units.

Rectified Linear Units Improve Restricted Boltzmann Machines
Nair & Hinton · ICML 2010 · cited in Activation functions

Introduces rectified linear units, max(0, x), as hidden units; they do not saturate for positive inputs and became the default activation.

Gaussian Error Linear Units (GELUs)
Hendrycks & Gimpel · arXiv 2016 · cited in Activation functions

GELU(x) = x·Φ(x): weights inputs by their value instead of gating by sign; outperformed ReLU and ELU (exponential linear unit) on vision, language and speech tasks. Used in BERT and GPT-2.

Searching for Activation Functions (Swish)
Ramachandran, Zoph & Le · ICLR workshop 2018 · cited in Activation functions

Automated search finds x·sigmoid(βx) (Swish; SiLU at β = 1), which beats ReLU on deeper models. SiLU is the gate inside SwiGLU.

Remember: +0.9% ImageNet top-1 on Mobile NASNet-A
GLU Variants Improve Transformer
Shazeer · arXiv 2020 · cited in Activation functions

Replaces the Transformer FFN (feed-forward network) with gated linear units (ReGLU, GEGLU, SwiGLU…) at equal parameters by shrinking the hidden size to ⅔.

Remember: log-perplexity 1.677 (ReLU) → 1.636 (SwiGLU)
Automatic differentiation in machine learning: a survey
Baydin et al. · JMLR 2018 · cited in Backpropagation

Forward and reverse-mode automatic differentiation explained; backpropagation is reverse mode, which gets all gradients of a scalar loss in about one extra pass.

Rethinking the Inception Architecture for Computer Vision
Szegedy et al. · CVPR 2016 · cited in Loss functions

Among other things, introduces label smoothing, the regularizer the Transformer uses with ε = 0.1.

A Neural Probabilistic Language Model
Bengio et al. · JMLR 2003 · cited in Loss functions, Embeddings, From MLP to language model

The first neural language model: learned word embeddings plus a network that outputs a softmax over the next word, trained by maximum likelihood.

Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization
Kingma & Ba · ICLR 2015 · cited in Optimizers

Per-parameter step sizes from bias-corrected running averages of the gradient and its square; invariant to diagonal rescaling of the gradients.

Remember: defaults β₁ 0.9, β₂ 0.999, ε 10⁻⁸
Decoupled Weight Decay Regularization (AdamW)
Loshchilov & Hutter · ICLR 2019 · cited in Optimizers

L2 regularization and weight decay differ for adaptive optimizers; applying decay directly to the weights (AdamW) decouples it from the learning rate and improves generalization.

SGDR: Stochastic Gradient Descent with Warm Restarts
Loshchilov & Hutter · ICLR 2017 · cited in Optimizers

Introduces the cosine learning-rate schedule (with optional restarts), now the standard decay shape for LLM pretraining.

On the importance of initialization and momentum in deep learning
Sutskever et al. · ICML 2013 · cited in Optimizers

With a good initialization and a slowly increasing momentum schedule, plain SGD with (Nesterov) momentum trains deep and recurrent networks once thought to need second-order methods.

LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models
Touvron et al. · arXiv 2023 · cited in Optimizers

The recipe most open models followed: pre-normalization with RMSNorm (root-mean-square normalization), SwiGLU (Swish-gated linear unit) with a ⅔·4d hidden size, and rotary embeddings.

Remember: trained on 1.0–1.4T tokens
Understanding the difficulty of training deep feedforward neural networks
Glorot & Bengio · AISTATS 2010 · cited in Initialization

Studies how activations and gradients shrink or grow across layers and proposes normalized (Xavier) initialization, Var(w) = 2/(fan_in + fan_out).

Delving Deep into Rectifiers: Surpassing Human-Level Performance on ImageNet Classification
He et al. · ICCV 2015 · cited in Initialization

Derives the initialization Var(w) = 2/fan_in for ReLU networks (He/Kaiming init), enabling very deep rectified models to train from scratch; also introduces PReLU (parametric ReLU).

Remember: 4.94% top-5 error on ImageNet
Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners (GPT-2)
Radford et al. · OpenAI 2019 · cited in Initialization, From MLP to language model

Decoder-only language model that moved layer normalization to the input of each sub-block (pre-norm) and added a final norm.

Remember: context 1,024 tokens, vocabulary 50,257
Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition (ResNet)
He et al. · CVPR 2016 · cited in Normalization and residual connections

Residual connections make very deep networks trainable by letting each block learn a correction to the identity.

Remember: 152 layers; 3.57% ImageNet test error (ensemble)
Batch Normalization: Accelerating Deep Network Training by Reducing Internal Covariate Shift
Ioffe & Szegedy · ICML 2015 · cited in Normalization and residual connections

Normalizes each feature over the mini-batch, allowing much higher learning rates and less careful initialization.

Remember: same accuracy with 14× fewer training steps
Layer Normalization
Ba et al. · arXiv 2016 · cited in Normalization and residual connections

Normalizes over all features of one training case instead of over the mini-batch, so it works for any batch size and for recurrent nets.

Root Mean Square Layer Normalization
Zhang & Sennrich · NeurIPS 2019 · cited in Normalization and residual connections

Drops LayerNorm's mean-centring and keeps only re-scaling by the root mean square, with comparable quality.

Remember: 7–64% less running time
Dropout: A Simple Way to Prevent Neural Networks from Overfitting
Srivastava et al. · JMLR 2014 · cited in Regularization and generalization

Randomly drops units during training, sampling an exponential number of thinned networks; at test time one unthinned network approximates their average.

Understanding deep learning requires rethinking generalization
Zhang et al. · ICLR 2017 · cited in Regularization and generalization

Standard networks easily fit completely random labels, so classical capacity measures cannot explain why they generalize on real data.

Reconciling modern machine learning practice and the bias-variance trade-off
Belkin et al. · PNAS 2019 · cited in Regularization and generalization

The "double descent" curve: test error rises as models approach the interpolation threshold, then falls again as they grow past it.

Deep Double Descent: Where Bigger Models and More Data Hurt
Nakkiran et al. · ICLR 2020 · cited in Regularization and generalization

Double descent appears in deep networks as a function of model size and of training epochs; near the threshold, more data can even hurt.

Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space (word2vec)
Mikolov et al. · ICLR workshop 2013 · cited in Embeddings

Learns word vectors whose dot products and differences capture meaning (king − man + woman ≈ queen) with cheap log-linear models.

Remember: under a day to learn vectors from 1.6B words
Distributed Representations of Words and Phrases and their Compositionality
Mikolov et al. · NeurIPS 2013 · cited in Embeddings

Skip-gram with negative sampling and subsampling of frequent words: faster training and better word vectors, plus vectors for phrases.

Attention Is All You Need
Vaswani et al. · NeurIPS 2017 · cited in From MLP to language model

The Transformer: attention-only encoder-decoder with post-norm residual blocks, sinusoidal positions and a ReLU (rectified linear unit) FFN (feed-forward network). The baseline every later change is measured against.

Remember: 28.4 BLEU (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy, an n-gram overlap score for translation) on WMT (Workshop on Machine Translation) 2014 English-German
