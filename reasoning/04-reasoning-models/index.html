Reasoning models and thinking budgets

Models trained to think at length before answering: reinforcement learning on verifiable rewards, budget forcing, thinking controls in APIs, and the limits (overthinking, unfaithful reasoning, cost).

What is the problem?Prompting and sampling get more out of a model that was never trained to reason at length. To use test-time compute well, the model itself must learn to explore, check and correct its work, and users need a way to control how much it thinks.
Core ideaTrain long chains of thought with reinforcement learning on verifiable rewards (math answers, tests): DeepSeek-R1-Zero went from 15.6% to 77.9% pass@1 on AIME (American Invitational Mathematics Examination) 2024, and reflection and self-verification emerged. Even small supervised datasets plus budget forcing (s1: 1,000 examples; "Wait" to extend thinking) give test-time scaling. APIs expose thinking as a controllable budget or effort level; thinking tokens are billed as output.
1 · Problem

Prompting can only go so far

Chain-of-thought prompting and sampling squeeze more out of an ordinary model. But the model was trained to imitate text, not to explore, check its work and backtrack over thousands of tokens.

Short habits

Pretrained models stop after a plausible-looking chain.

No self-correction

They rarely notice and fix their own mistakes unless trained to.

Uncontrolled cost

Users need to decide how much thinking a request deserves.

2 · Limitation

What sampling alone does not give you

Independent samples

Each sample starts over; none learns from another's mistakes.

Selection limits

Without a perfect verifier, accuracy plateaus below coverage (deck 02).

Human-labelled reasoning is scarce

Supervising long chains step by step with people is expensive.

5 · How it works

Worked example: what does thinking cost?

A request produces 300 answer tokens; with deep thinking it also produces 6,000 thinking tokens. The model streams 80 output tokens per second.

Worked example

  1. Without thinking: 300 output tokens, about 300 / 80 ≈ 4 s of generation.
  2. With thinking: 6,300 output tokens, about 79 s, and about 21× the output-token bill (thinking tokens are billed as output).
  3. If the question is easy, the extra 6,000 tokens buy nothing: that is overthinking.
  4. Use lower effort (or a smaller budget) for easy traffic and high effort only where accuracy pays for it.
Thinking is test-time compute you pay for in latency and tokens; route it to the requests that need it.
6 · Why it works

Why it works, and where it stops

Learned search

RL (reinforcement learning) rewards chains that end correctly, so the model learns to explore alternatives and check itself within one chain.

Compute on demand

Longer thinking on harder problems uses test-time compute adaptively.

Overthinking

o1-like models spend far more tokens than needed on simple problems such as "2 + 3", with minimal benefit; self-training can shorten chains without losing accuracy.

Unfaithful chains

Chains often omit what drove the answer; reward hacking rose under RL without being verbalised more. Chain-of-thought monitoring helps but cannot rule out bad behaviour.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"A reasoning model's chain of thought shows exactly why it answered."

Anthropic found chains revealed hints the model used only 25% (Claude 3.7 Sonnet) and 39% (DeepSeek R1) of the time.

"Reasoning models need huge human-written reasoning datasets."

R1-Zero used RL with rule-based rewards; s1 used only 1,000 curated examples.

"More thinking always helps."

Gains diminish, and easy questions gain nothing while latency and cost grow.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Verifiable rewards scale. Math and code answers can be checked automatically, so RL needs no human labels per step.
  • Budget forcing. Appending "Wait" when the model tries to stop made s1-32B check and fix its answers (AIME24 50% → 57%).
  • Read the usage fields. Claude reports thinking tokens in usage.output_tokens_details.thinking_tokens.

⚠ Traps

  • Trusting the chain as an audit log. Claude 3.7 Sonnet mentioned hints it used only 25% of the time, DeepSeek R1 39%.
  • Maximum effort everywhere. Overthinking spends compute on "2 + 3" with no gain.
  • Pinning old API parameters. On newer Claude models fixed budget_tokens is deprecated or rejected; use adaptive thinking with an effort level.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Reasoning models are trained to spend test-time compute well; your job is to give them the right amount and not to over-trust what they write.
