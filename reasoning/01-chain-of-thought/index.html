Reasoning and test-time compute · 01

Chain of thought

Why asking a model to write out intermediate steps makes it better at multi-step problems: few-shot and zero-shot chain-of-thought prompting, when it helps, and what the extra tokens buy.

What is the problem?A model asked for an answer directly must compute it in a single forward pass per output token. Multi-step problems (arithmetic word problems, logic, planning) need more sequential computation than that, so direct answers fail.
Core ideaLet the model write the intermediate steps before the answer. Each generated token is another forward pass that can build on the previous steps, so a chain of thought turns one hard step into many easy ones. Show a few worked examples (few-shot CoT), or simply add "Let's think step by step" (zero-shot CoT).
1 · Problem

Some answers need more than one step

"Roger has 5 tennis balls. He buys 2 more cans of 3 tennis balls each. How many tennis balls does he have now?" Asked for the number directly, large models in 2022 often got problems like this wrong.

Fixed compute per token

Each output token gets one forward pass through the network.

Hidden intermediate results

A direct answer must hold "2 × 3 = 6" and "5 + 6 = 11" implicitly inside one pass.

Benchmarks

GSM8K (Grade School Math 8K), 8.5K grade-school math word problems, was built because even the largest models then struggled with multi-step arithmetic.

2 · Limitation

Bigger models alone did not fix it

Flat scaling

The chain-of-thought paper notes scaling up model size alone had not been enough for arithmetic, commonsense and symbolic reasoning.

Finetuning is costly

Training models to produce rationales needs large labelled datasets of worked solutions.

Direct answers waste the model

The capability to do each step is there; the prompt never asks for the steps.

3 · Your turn

Why would writing steps help?

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

Why does generating intermediate reasoning steps improve accuracy on multi-step problems?

B. The model computes a little per token; writing steps spreads the computation over many tokens and keeps intermediate results available as text.
4 · Core idea

Ask for the reasoning, then the answer

Few-shot CoT

Include a few exemplars (question, worked steps, answer) in the prompt: the model imitates the format on the new question.

Zero-shot CoT

Append "Let's think step by step": MultiArith 17.7% → 78.7% and GSM8K 10.4% → 40.7% with text-davinci-002.

Scale matters

CoT was an emergent ability around 100B parameters in 2022; smaller models produced fluent but illogical chains.

Where it helps

Multi-step tasks, large models, flat scaling curves; little gain on one- or two-step problems already above 90%.

5 · How it works

The GSM8K result paper figure

GSM8K solve rate (Wei et al. 2022, Figure 2): eight chain-of-thought exemplars took PaLM 540B from 18% to 57%, above the previous best, a finetuned GPT-3 with a separately trained verifier.

Chain of thought is also the starting point for everything in this topic: sampling many chains and voting (deck 02), scoring chains with verifiers (deck 03), and training models to produce long chains with reinforcement learning (deck 04).

5 · How it works

Worked example: a word problem, direct vs step by step

"A cafeteria had 23 apples. They used 20 for lunch and bought 6 more. How many apples do they have?" (an exemplar style from the chain-of-thought paper).

Worked example

  1. Direct answer: the model must produce "9" in one step, combining both operations implicitly; in the paper's standard-prompting example the model answers 27.
  2. Chain of thought: "They had 23 apples. They used 20, so 23 − 20 = 3. They bought 6 more, so 3 + 6 = 9."
  3. Each line is a simple operation the model can do reliably, and each result is now text in the context for the next step.
  4. The final answer is read from the end of the chain: 9.
The chain externalises intermediate results, so no single step has to do the whole computation.
6 · Why it works

Why it works

Serial computation

Depth per token is fixed; generating more tokens adds sequential steps of computation.

Working memory

Intermediate results become context tokens the model can attend to.

Decomposition

Exemplars show how to break a problem into steps the model already handles well.

Inspectable (with limits)

You can read the steps to debug errors, though they may not be the model's true reasons (deck 04).

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Chain of thought makes any model reason."

In the original experiments the gains appeared only at large scale (about 100B parameters); small models wrote fluent nonsense.

"The written steps are exactly how the model got its answer."

Not necessarily: reasoning models often fail to mention hints they used (only 25% to 39% of the time in Anthropic's study).

"Longer chains are always better."

Extra steps on easy problems waste tokens and time (overthinking, deck 04).
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • CoT helps most on multi-step tasks. Little gain when a problem takes one or two steps and accuracy is already high.
  • Zero-shot CoT is one line. "Let's think step by step" lifted GSM8K from 10.4% to 40.7% on text-davinci-002.
  • Put the answer last. Reasoning first, answer at the end, so the answer can depend on the steps.

⚠ Traps

  • Small models. Below about 100B parameters (in 2022 models) chains were fluent but illogical.
  • Answer first, reasons after. A justification written after the answer cannot improve it.
  • Treating the chain as an explanation. The written steps are not guaranteed to be how the model actually decided (deck 04).

Check yourself

Answer from memory. If you can't, revisit the slide named in the answer.

Where in the output should the final answer go for chain-of-thought prompting?

B. The answer must be generated after, and conditioned on, the intermediate steps.

Name two conditions under which chain of thought helps most.

The task needs multiple steps, the model is large enough, and the standard-prompting scaling curve is flat (any two).

What is zero-shot chain of thought?

Adding a trigger phrase such as "Let's think step by step" without any worked exemplars.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

A chain of thought buys the model more sequential computation, one token at a time, before it commits to an answer.
Say it in 30 seconds

A transformer does a fixed amount of computation per generated token, so a direct answer to a multi-step problem has to happen in very few steps. Chain-of-thought prompting asks the model to write intermediate steps first; each step is cheap, and its result is in the context for the next one. With eight worked exemplars, PaLM 540B went from 18% to 57% on GSM8K, beating the previous best of 55%. Zero-shot, "Let's think step by step" took text-davinci-002 from 10.4% to 40.7% on GSM8K.

Mechanism: intermediate steps are extra sequential computation
Few-shot: worked exemplars in the prompt
Zero-shot: "Let's think step by step"
Fit: multi-step tasks, large models

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models
Wei et al. · NeurIPS 2022

A few exemplars with intermediate reasoning steps make large models much better at arithmetic, commonsense and symbolic reasoning; the ability emerges around 100B parameters and helps most on multi-step tasks with flat scaling curves.

Remember: GSM8K: PaLM 540B 18% → 57% with 8 exemplars (prior best 55%)
Large Language Models are Zero-Shot Reasoners
Kojima et al. · NeurIPS 2022

Adding "Let's think step by step" before the answer elicits chain-of-thought reasoning without any exemplars.

Remember: text-davinci-002: MultiArith 17.7% → 78.7%, GSM8K 10.4% → 40.7%
Training Verifiers to Solve Math Word Problems
Cobbe et al. · arXiv 2021

Introduces GSM8K (8.5K grade-school math word problems) and trained verifiers: generate many candidate solutions and pick the one the verifier ranks highest; verification scales better with data than a finetuning baseline.
