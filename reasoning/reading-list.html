Reasoning and test-time compute: key papers

Every paper cited on the decks' Key papers slides, in the order the guide introduces them. Each entry links to the paper and to the decks that use it.

Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models
Wei et al. · NeurIPS 2022 · cited in Chain of thought, Sampling, voting and coverage

A few exemplars with intermediate reasoning steps make large models much better at arithmetic, commonsense and symbolic reasoning; the ability emerges around 100B parameters and helps most on multi-step tasks with flat scaling curves.

Remember: GSM8K: PaLM 540B 18% → 57% with 8 exemplars (prior best 55%)
Large Language Models are Zero-Shot Reasoners
Kojima et al. · NeurIPS 2022 · cited in Chain of thought

Adding "Let's think step by step" before the answer elicits chain-of-thought reasoning without any exemplars.

Remember: text-davinci-002: MultiArith 17.7% → 78.7%, GSM8K 10.4% → 40.7%
Training Verifiers to Solve Math Word Problems
Cobbe et al. · arXiv 2021 · cited in Chain of thought, Verifiers and search

Introduces GSM8K (8.5K grade-school math word problems) and trained verifiers: generate many candidate solutions and pick the one the verifier ranks highest; verification scales better with data than a finetuning baseline.

Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models
Wang et al. · ICLR 2023 · cited in Sampling, voting and coverage

Sample diverse reasoning paths and return the most consistent final answer (marginalising over paths) instead of greedy decoding.

Remember: GSM8K +17.9, SVAMP +11.0, AQuA +12.2, StrategyQA +6.4, ARC-Challenge +3.9 points
Large Language Monkeys: Scaling Inference Compute with Repeated Sampling
Brown et al. · arXiv 2024 · cited in Sampling, voting and coverage, Verifiers and search

Coverage (problems solved by any sample) scales with the number of samples over four orders of magnitude, often log-linearly; with automatic verifiers it becomes accuracy, while majority voting and reward models plateau beyond several hundred samples.

Remember: SWE-bench Lite: 15.9% (1 sample) → 56% (250 samples) vs 43% single-sample state of the art
Evaluating Large Language Models Trained on Code (Codex, HumanEval)
Chen et al. · arXiv 2021 · cited in Sampling, voting and coverage

HumanEval's 164 hand-written problems scored by unit tests, and the unbiased pass@k estimator.

Remember: Codex-12B: 28.8% pass@1, 70.2% with 100 samples
Let's Verify Step by Step
Lightman et al. · ICLR 2024 · cited in Verifiers and search

Process supervision (feedback on each reasoning step) significantly outperforms outcome supervision for training reward models on MATH; active learning improves it further; releases PRM800K.

Remember: 78% of a representative MATH test subset; 800,000 step-level labels
Scaling LLM (large language model) Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters
Snell et al. · ICLR 2025 · cited in Verifiers and search

The best way to spend test-time compute (search against process verifiers, or revising responses) depends on prompt difficulty; allocating it adaptively per prompt is compute-optimal.

Remember: over 4× more efficient than best-of-N; FLOPs-matched, beats a 14× larger model where the small model has non-trivial success
Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with Large Language Models
Yao et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · cited in Verifiers and search

Search over intermediate "thoughts" with self-evaluation, lookahead and backtracking instead of a single left-to-right chain.

Remember: Game of 24: 74% vs 4% with chain-of-thought (GPT-4)
DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs (large language models) via Reinforcement Learning
DeepSeek-AI · arXiv 2025 · cited in Reasoning models and thinking budgets

R1-Zero: RL with rule-based accuracy and format rewards directly on a base model; R1 adds cold-start data and a multi-stage pipeline.

Remember: AIME 2024 pass@1 15.6% → 77.9% (R1-Zero)
s1: Simple test-time scaling
Muennighoff et al. · arXiv 2025 · cited in Reasoning models and thinking budgets

Supervised finetuning of Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on 1,000 curated reasoning traces (s1K) plus budget forcing: end thinking at a budget, or append "Wait" to extend it so the model double-checks its answer.

Remember: s1-32B beats o1-preview by up to 27% (MATH, AIME24); AIME24 50% → 57% with budget forcing
Do NOT Think That Much for 2+3=? On the Overthinking of o1-Like LLMs
Chen et al. · arXiv 2024 · cited in Reasoning models and thinking budgets

o1-like models allocate excessive compute to simple problems with minimal benefit; efficiency metrics and self-training strategies reduce overhead without hurting accuracy.

Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think
Chen et al. (Anthropic) · arXiv 2025 · cited in Reasoning models and thinking budgets

Reasoning models often use hints without mentioning them; outcome-based RL improves faithfulness then plateaus, and increased reward hacking is not verbalised more. CoT (chain of thought) monitoring helps but cannot rule out rare bad behaviour.

Remember: faithfulness: 25% Claude 3.7 Sonnet, 39% DeepSeek R1
Extended and adaptive thinking (Claude API)
Anthropic documentation · platform.claude.com 2026 · cited in Reasoning models and thinking budgets

Manual budgets (thinking.type enabled, budget_tokens ≥ 1,024) on older models; newer models use adaptive thinking with an effort level, and reject fixed budgets; thinking tokens are billed output (usage.output_tokens_details.thinking_tokens); batch processing above 32k thinking tokens.
