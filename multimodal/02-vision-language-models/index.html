Multimodal LLMs · 02

Connecting vision to an LLM

How vision-language models attach an image encoder to a language model: projection layers (LLaVA), resamplers and cross-attention (Flamingo), query transformers (BLIP-2), and early fusion of image tokens (Chameleon).

What is the problem?A good vision encoder and a strong LLM (large language model) exist separately, but the LLM cannot read the encoder's features, and training a multimodal model from scratch is very expensive. How do you connect them with little extra training?
Core ideaAdd a small connector that maps visual features into the LLM's input space. The simplest is a projection of patch features into word-embedding space (LLaVA); others compress the image to a few tokens with learned queries (Perceiver Resampler, Q-Former) or inject vision through cross-attention layers. Then instruction-tune on image conversations. Alternatively, early fusion turns images into discrete tokens and trains one model on mixed sequences.
1 · Problem

Two strong models that cannot talk

You have a CLIP vision encoder and a 7B chat model. You want to ask "What is unusual about this image?" and get an answer.

Different spaces

The encoder outputs patch features; the LLM expects word embeddings.

Training cost

Pretraining a multimodal model end to end is very expensive.

Instruction following

Even connected, the model needs examples of conversations about images.

2 · Limitation

Why not just caption the image?

Lossy

A caption drops most of the detail a question might need.

Fixed in advance

The captioner does not know the question.

Two models, two errors

Captioning mistakes cannot be corrected by the LLM.

4 · Core idea

Four ways to connect

Projection (LLaVA)

Patch features → LLM embeddings via a linear layer; instruction-tuned on GPT-4-generated image conversations; 85.1% relative to GPT-4 on a synthetic benchmark, 92.53% on ScienceQA with GPT-4. LLaVA-1.5: MLP, 336 px CLIP, 1.2M public examples, about 1 day on 8 A100s.

Resampler + cross-attention (Flamingo)

Frozen vision and language models; a Perceiver Resampler turns any image or video into 64 visual tokens; gated cross-attention layers inside the LM; few-shot from interleaved web data.

Query transformer (BLIP-2)

A lightweight Q-Former with 32 learned queries between frozen models, pretrained in two stages: beat Flamingo-80B by 8.7% on zero-shot VQAv2 with 54× fewer trainable parameters.

Early fusion (Chameleon)

Images become discrete tokens in the same sequence as text; one model trained from the start on mixed data understands and generates both.

5 · How it works

Visual tokens per image by design

Tokens an image contributes to the language model. LLaVA-1.5 and ViT (Vision Transformer) pass every patch; Flamingo's resampler and BLIP-2's Q-Former compress to a fixed small set of learned queries.

5 · How it works

Worked example: how many tokens does an image cost the LLM?

The same image through three connector designs.

Worked example

  1. LLaVA-1.5 (CLIP ViT-L/14 at 336 px, MLP projection): (336 / 14)² = 576 visual tokens enter the LLM's context.
  2. Flamingo's Perceiver Resampler: any number of image or video features → a fixed 64 visual outputs, read through cross-attention.
  3. BLIP-2's Q-Former: 32 learned queries summarise the frozen encoder's output for the frozen LLM.
  4. Fewer tokens are cheaper and leave more context; more tokens keep fine detail such as small text.
Connector design is a trade-off between visual detail and context cost.
6 · Why it works

Why connectors work

Reuse

Pretrained encoders already describe images well; pretrained LLMs already reason well. The connector only translates.

Cheap training

Freezing big models and training small connectors cuts cost (BLIP-2: 54× fewer trainable parameters than Flamingo-80B).

Synthetic instructions

A language-only model can write image conversations from captions and boxes, bootstrapping instruction data.

Early fusion's payoff

A single token space lets one model read and write mixed documents, at the cost of training from scratch.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"A multimodal model must be trained from scratch."

Most connect frozen or lightly tuned pretrained parts with a small trained connector.

"More visual tokens are always better."

They cost context and compute; compression works for many tasks, detail-heavy ones need more.

"Vision-language models can draw."

Connector-based understanding models output only text; generating images needs image tokens or a decoder (e.g. early fusion).
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Start simple. LLaVA-1.5 found a fully connected projector "surprisingly powerful and data-efficient".
  • Freeze to save cost. BLIP-2 keeps the image encoder and LLM frozen, training only the Q-Former.
  • Interleaved data enables few-shot. Flamingo trained on web pages mixing images and text.

⚠ Traps

  • Ignoring hallucination. Vision-language models often describe objects that are not in the image (deck 03).
  • Too few visual tokens for documents. Heavy compression loses small text and fine layout.
  • Assuming generation comes free. Understanding-only connectors cannot output images; early fusion can.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Most vision-language models are a pretrained vision encoder plus a pretrained LLM, joined by a small trained connector.
Say it in 30 seconds

LLaVA projects CLIP patch features into an LLM's embedding space and instruction-tunes on conversations generated by language-only GPT-4; it reached 85.1% of GPT-4's score on a synthetic benchmark. LLaVA-1.5 used an MLP projector, 336-pixel CLIP and 1.2M public examples, training in about a day on one 8-A100 node. Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Visual Instruction Tuning (LLaVA)
Liu et al. · NeurIPS 2023

Connects a CLIP vision encoder to an LLM through a projection and instruction-tunes on multimodal conversations generated by language-only GPT-4.

Remember: 85.1% relative score vs GPT-4 on a synthetic benchmark; 92.53% on ScienceQA with GPT-4
Improved Baselines with Visual Instruction Tuning (LLaVA-1.5)
Liu et al. · CVPR 2024

An MLP projector with CLIP-ViT-L-336px plus academic VQA (visual question answering) data with response-format prompts; state of the art on 11 benchmarks.

Remember: 1.2M public examples; about one day of training on a single 8-A100 node (13B)
Flamingo: a Visual Language Model for Few-Shot Learning
Alayrac et al. · NeurIPS 2022

Bridges frozen vision and language models with a Perceiver Resampler (a fixed 64 visual outputs from any image or video) and gated cross-attention layers; trained on interleaved image-text web data for in-context few-shot learning.

BLIP-2: Bootstrapping Language-Image Pre-training with Frozen Image Encoders and Large Language Models
Li et al. · ICML 2023

A lightweight Querying Transformer (32 learned queries) between a frozen image encoder and a frozen LLM, pretrained in two stages.

Remember: beats Flamingo-80B by 8.7% on zero-shot VQAv2 with 54× fewer trainable parameters
Chameleon: Mixed-Modal Early-Fusion Foundation Models
Chameleon Team (Meta) · arXiv 2024

Early-fusion, token-based model that understands and generates images and text in any order within one sequence.
