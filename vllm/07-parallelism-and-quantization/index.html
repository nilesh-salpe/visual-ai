Parallelism and quantization

Fitting and scaling models in vLLM: tensor, pipeline, data and expert parallelism flags, weight quantization formats, and FP8 (8-bit floating point) KV (key-value) caches, with their effect on memory and throughput.

What is the problem?A 70B model in BF16 (bfloat16, a 16-bit floating-point format with the exponent range of 32-bit floats) needs about 141 GB for weights alone, more than one 80 GB GPU (graphics processing unit). Even when a model fits, KV-cache space limits how many requests can run at once.
Core ideaTensor parallelism (-tp) splits each layer across GPUs in a node; pipeline parallelism (-pp) splits layers across nodes; data parallelism (-dp) replicates the engine; expert parallelism (--enable-expert-parallel) spreads MoE (Mixture of Experts) experts. Quantization (--quantization, e.g. fp8, awq, gptq) shrinks weights; --kv-cache-dtype fp8 halves KV memory.

2 · Limitation

Why one GPU isn't enough

Weights

70B × 2 bytes ≈ 141 GB in BF16.

KV cache

Every concurrent token needs KV in every layer; long contexts multiply it.

Throughput

One replica can only serve so many requests per second.

3 · Your turn

Which flag adds throughput but not memory?

Pick an answer before moving on. Committing to a guess, even a wrong one, makes the real answer stick.

Your model fits on one GPU but you need 4× the requests per second. Which option is most natural?

C. Data parallelism runs 4 independent engine replicas. TP/PP (pipeline parallelism) help when the model or its KV cache does not fit.
4 · Core idea

The knobs

Tensor (-tp)

Shard every layer across GPUs in one node; also shards KV heads, so KV per GPU shrinks.

Pipeline (-pp)

Split layers into stages, usually across nodes; activations pass stage to stage.

Data (-dp) and expert parallelism

Replicate engines; for MoE, --enable-expert-parallel places experts on different GPUs.

Quantization

--quantization (fp8, awq, gptq, compressed-tensors, mxfp4, …) for weights; --kv-cache-dtype fp8 for KV.

5 · How it works

Typical commands

# 70B dense model on one 8-GPU node
$ vllm serve meta-llama/Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct -tp 8

# large model over two nodes: TP inside, PP across
$ vllm serve  -tp 8 -pp 2

# MoE model: experts spread over GPUs
$ vllm serve  -tp 8 --enable-expert-parallel

# more throughput from a model that fits on 2 GPUs
$ vllm serve  -tp 2 -dp 4

# smaller weights and KV cache
$ vllm serve  --kv-cache-dtype fp8

Flags are generated from vllm/config (ParallelConfig, CacheConfig) and vllm/engine/arg_utils.py in v0.31.0.

5 · How it works

Worked example: Llama-3.1-70B on 80 GB GPUs

Notebook 04's planner: 70.6B parameters, 80 layers, 8 KV heads of dimension 128; gpu_memory_utilization 0.92; 4 GiB activation reserve per GPU.

Worked example

  1. KV bytes per token: 2 × 80 × 8 × 128 × 2 = 320 KiB in BF16.
  2. TP = 1: BF16 weights (≈ 131 GiB) do not fit. TP = 2: room for only ≈ 25K KV tokens (3 concurrent 8K requests).
  3. TP = 4: ≈ 481K KV tokens (58 × 8K requests): splitting weights frees far more room than the extra GPUs alone.
  4. An FP8 KV cache doubles that to ≈ 963K tokens (117 × 8K requests).
Parallelism first makes a model fit, then mostly buys KV space; quantization multiplies both.
6 · Why it works

Why these choices

Match communication to links

TP's per-layer all-reduces need NVLink; PP sends little between stages.

KV room is throughput

More KV tokens → more concurrent requests → higher throughput.

Bytes are time

Decoding is memory-bound; fewer bytes per weight and KV entry mean faster steps.

Replicas scale linearly

DP (data parallelism) adds capacity without changing per-request latency.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"More TP always lowers latency."

Beyond a point the all-reduce cost dominates; TP mostly buys memory and per-GPU work reduction.

"FP8 KV cache is free accuracy-wise."

It is usually close, but should be validated on your workload.

"Expert parallelism replaces tensor parallelism."

EP distributes experts; attention and dense layers still use TP or DP.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • TP within a node. Per-layer all-reduces need NVLink.
  • FP8 KV cache. --kv-cache-dtype fp8 halves KV memory on supporting GPUs.
  • MoE: --enable-expert-parallel. With TP or DP for the dense parts.

⚠ Traps

  • TP across nodes. Use PP between nodes instead.
  • Quantizing blindly. Check accuracy on your tasks; methods differ by hardware support.
  • Forgetting DP routing. Prefix-cache hits depend on which replica gets a request.
8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Answer from memory. If you can't, revisit the slide named in the answer.

KV bytes per token for Llama-3.1-8B in BF16 (32 layers, 8 KV heads, dim 128)?

2 × 32 × 8 × 128 × 2 = 128 KiB.

Why does going from TP = 2 to TP = 4 more than double KV capacity for a 70B model?

Each GPU's weight share halves too, freeing memory beyond the extra GPUs' own capacity.

Which flag halves KV-cache memory?

--kv-cache-dtype fp8 (on supporting hardware).
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

vLLM fits and scales models with -tp (in node), -pp (across nodes), -dp (replicas) and expert parallelism, and shrinks memory with weight quantization and an FP8 KV cache; KV room becomes concurrency.
Say it in 30 seconds

Large models need more than one GPU, and even small ones are limited by KV-cache space. vLLM's tensor parallelism splits every layer, and the KV heads, across GPUs in a node; pipeline parallelism splits layers across nodes; data parallelism runs independent engine replicas for throughput; expert parallelism spreads mixture-of-experts layers. Quantization flags shrink weights with formats like FP8, AWQ (Activation-aware Weight Quantization) or GPTQ, and an FP8 KV cache halves KV memory. In the notebook, a 70B model goes from not fitting, to three concurrent 8K requests at TP two, to fifty-eight at TP four.

Problem: weights and KV exceed one GPU.
Core idea: TP/PP to fit, DP/EP to scale, quantize to shrink.
Flags: -tp -pp -dp --enable-expert-parallel --kv-cache-dtype.

Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

Parallelism and Scaling
vLLM documentation · docs.vllm.ai 2026

Single-node tensor parallelism, multi-node pipeline parallelism, data parallelism and expert parallelism for MoE models, and how to choose sizes.

Quantization
vLLM documentation · docs.vllm.ai 2026

Supported weight/activation quantization (FP8, AWQ, GPTQ, compressed-tensors, MXFP4 (microscaling 4-bit floating point), NVFP4 (NVIDIA's block-scaled 4-bit floating point) and more) and quantized KV caches, with hardware support tables.

Megatron-LM: Training Multi-Billion Parameter Language Models Using Model Parallelism
Shoeybi et al. · arXiv 2019

Intra-layer (tensor) model parallelism that splits each attention and MLP (multilayer perceptron) block across GPUs with only a few all-reduce operations, implemented in plain PyTorch.

Remember: 2 all-reduces per transformer block in the forward pass; 76% scaling efficiency on 512 GPUs
AWQ: Activation-aware Weight Quantization for LLM (large language model) Compression and Acceleration
Lin et al. · MLSys 2024

Weight-only low-bit quantization that protects the most important weight channels, chosen by activation magnitude, by scaling them up before quantizing.

Remember: protecting 1% of salient weights greatly reduces error; >3× over Hugging Face FP16 (16-bit floating point) with its TinyChat kernels
GPTQ: Accurate Post-Training Quantization for Generative Pre-trained Transformers
Frantar et al. · ICLR 2023

One-shot weight quantization using approximate second-order information, quantizing one column at a time and compensating the error in the rest.

Remember: 175B model to 3–4 bits in about four GPU hours
FP8 Formats for Deep Learning
Micikevicius et al. · arXiv 2022

Defines the two FP8 encodings, E4M3 (more precision) and E5M2 (more range), and shows FP8 training and post-training quantization matching 16-bit results.

Remember: E4M3 max 448, E5M2 max 57,344
