Distributed Training: key papers

Every paper cited on the decks' Key papers slides, in the order the guide introduces them. Each entry links to the paper and to the decks that use it.

Efficient Large-Scale Language Model Training on GPU Clusters Using Megatron-LM
Narayanan et al. · SC 2021 · cited in Why training must be distributed, Data parallelism, Tensor and sequence parallelism, Pipeline parallelism, Putting it together: 4D layouts

Combines tensor, pipeline and data parallelism (PTD-P) with an interleaved pipeline schedule, placing tensor parallelism inside nodes.

Remember: 1T parameters at 502 PFLOP (quadrillion floating-point operations)/s on 3,072 GPUs, 52% of peak
ZeRO: Memory Optimizations Toward Training Trillion Parameter Models
Rajbhandari et al. · SC 2020 · cited in Why training must be distributed, ZeRO and FSDP

Partitions optimizer states, gradients and parameters across data-parallel workers instead of replicating them.

Remember: 100B+ parameters on 400 GPUs at 15 PFLOP/s; 8× larger models than prior state of the art
The Llama 3 Herd of Models
Llama Team, AI (artificial intelligence) @ Meta · arXiv 2024 · cited in Why training must be distributed, ZeRO and FSDP, Context parallelism, Mixed precision and FP8 training, Putting it together: 4D layouts, Reliability and stability

Dense Transformer with GQA (8 KV (key-value) heads), RoPE (rotary position embedding) base 500,000, a 128K vocabulary and a document mask; staged long-context pretraining to 128K; post-training with SFT (supervised fine-tuning), rejection sampling and DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) in six rounds.

Remember: 128K context after six stages over ~800B tokens
DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report
DeepSeek-AI · arXiv 2024 · cited in Why training must be distributed, Expert parallelism, Mixed precision and FP8 training

671B-parameter MoE trained with FP8 (8-bit floating point) mixed precision (fine-grained 1×128 and 128×128 scaling), DualPipe pipeline scheduling and 64-way expert parallelism with cross-node all-to-all kernels.

Remember: 2.788M H800 GPU-hours; no irrecoverable loss spikes or rollbacks
MegaScale: Scaling Large Language Model Training to More Than 10,000 GPUs
Jiang et al. · NSDI 2024 · cited in Why training must be distributed, Putting it together: 4D layouts, Reliability and stability

Production training system at ByteDance: algorithm-system co-design plus diagnosis tools for stragglers and failures at 10,000+ GPU scale.

Remember: 55.2% MFU on 12,288 GPUs (175B model), 1.34× Megatron-LM
PyTorch Distributed: Experiences on Accelerating Data Parallel Training
Li et al. · VLDB 2020 · cited in Data parallelism

PyTorch's DistributedDataParallel design: gradient bucketing, overlapping all-reduce with the backward pass, and skipping synchronization during gradient accumulation.

Remember: near-linear scaling on 256 GPUs
PyTorch FSDP: Experiences on Scaling Fully Sharded Data Parallel
Zhao et al. · VLDB 2023 · cited in ZeRO and FSDP

PyTorch's ZeRO-3-style fully sharded data parallelism, with flattened parameter units, prefetching and hybrid sharding.

Remember: comparable to DDP speed while supporting much larger models
Megatron-LM: Training Multi-Billion Parameter Language Models Using Model Parallelism
Shoeybi et al. · arXiv 2019 · cited in Tensor and sequence parallelism

Intra-layer (tensor) model parallelism that splits each attention and MLP block across GPUs with only a few all-reduce operations, implemented in plain PyTorch.

Remember: 2 all-reduces per transformer block in the forward pass; 76% scaling efficiency on 512 GPUs
Reducing Activation Recomputation in Large Transformer Models
Korthikanti et al. · MLSys 2023 · cited in Tensor and sequence parallelism, Activation memory and recomputation

Sequence parallelism (shard LayerNorm and dropout activations along the sequence) plus selective recomputation of the attention-score part.

Remember: 5× less activation memory; MFU 54.2% vs 42.1% on a 530B model
GPipe: Efficient Training of Giant Neural Networks using Pipeline Parallelism
Huang et al. · NeurIPS 2019 · cited in Pipeline parallelism

Splits a model into stages and the batch into micro-batches; the bubble becomes negligible with enough micro-batches.

Remember: bubble negligible when micro-batches ≥ 4 × stages
PipeDream: Fast and Efficient Pipeline Parallel DNN (deep neural network) Training
Narayanan et al. · SOSP 2019 · cited in Pipeline parallelism

Introduces the one-forward-one-backward (1F1B) schedule that interleaves forward and backward passes to bound in-flight activations.

Remember: up to 5× faster time-to-accuracy than data parallelism
Zero Bubble Pipeline Parallelism
Qi et al. · ICLR 2024 · cited in Pipeline parallelism

Splits the backward pass into input-gradient and weight-gradient parts and schedules them to fill pipeline bubbles.

Remember: up to 23% over 1F1B (31% with relaxed memory)
Ring Attention with Blockwise Transformers for Near-Infinite Context
Liu et al. · ICLR 2024 · cited in Context parallelism

Splits the sequence across devices and passes key-value blocks around a ring while computing blockwise attention, overlapping communication with compute; exact.

Remember: context up to device-count times longer
DeepSpeed Ulysses: System Optimizations for Enabling Training of Extreme Long Sequence Transformer Models
Jacobs et al. · arXiv 2023 · cited in Context parallelism

Sequence parallelism that uses all-to-all to switch between sequence-sharded and head-sharded layouts around attention.

Remember: 2.5× faster with 4× longer sequences
GShard: Scaling Giant Models with Conditional Computation and Automatic Sharding
Lepikhin et al. · ICLR 2021 · cited in Expert parallelism

Expert parallelism with all-to-all dispatch and automatic sharding annotations for a sparsely gated MoE translation model.

Remember: 600B+ parameters on 2,048 TPU v3 in 4 days
Switch Transformers: Scaling to Trillion Parameter Models with Simple and Efficient Sparsity
Fedus et al. · JMLR 2022 · cited in Expert parallelism

Top-1 expert routing, an auxiliary load-balancing loss α·N·Σfᵢ·Pᵢ, expert capacity, and tricks for stable bfloat16 training.

Remember: up to 7× faster pretraining; α = 10⁻²
Training Deep Nets with Sublinear Memory Cost
Chen et al. · arXiv 2016 · cited in Activation memory and recomputation

Activation checkpointing: store a subset of activations and recompute the rest during backward, giving O(√n) memory.

Remember: 1,000-layer network from 48 GB to 7 GB for ~30% more time
Mixed Precision Training
Micikevicius et al. · ICLR 2018 · cited in Mixed precision and FP8 training

Half-precision weights, activations and gradients with an FP32 master copy of the weights and loss scaling to avoid gradient underflow.

Remember: nearly 2× less memory
FP8 Formats for Deep Learning
Micikevicius et al. · arXiv 2022 · cited in Mixed precision and FP8 training

Defines the two FP8 encodings, E4M3 (more precision) and E5M2 (more range), and shows FP8 training and post-training quantization matching 16-bit results.

Remember: E4M3 max 448, E5M2 max 57,344
Gemini: Fast Failure Recovery in Distributed Training with In-Memory Checkpoints
Wang et al. · SOSP 2023 · cited in Reliability and stability

Checkpoints to CPU memory across machines instead of remote storage, with placement and traffic scheduling so checkpoints survive failures and don't slow training.

Remember: more than 13× faster failure recovery
PaLM: Scaling Language Modeling with Pathways
Chowdhery et al. · JMLR 2023 · cited in Reliability and stability

Reports ~20 loss spikes in its largest run, handled by restarting about 100 steps earlier and skipping 200–500 batches; uses a z-loss on the softmax normalizer.

Remember: 46.2% MFU across two TPU (tensor processing unit) v4 Pods
Scaling Vision Transformers to 22 Billion Parameters
Dehghani et al. · ICML 2023 · cited in Reliability and stability

Finds training divergence caused by very large attention logits and fixes it with LayerNorm on queries and keys (QK normalization).
