Distributed Training · 05

Pipeline parallelism

Split the layers into stages on different GPUs (graphics processing units) and stream micro-batches through them: GPipe, 1F1B, interleaved and zero-bubble schedules, and the bubble they all fight.

What is the problem?A model too deep for one node can be cut into stages, but a single batch flowing through stages leaves every GPU idle most of the time.
Core ideaSplit the batch into m micro-batches so stages work on different micro-batches at once. The fill-and-drain bubble costs (p − 1)/m of the ideal time; 1F1B bounds activation memory, interleaving divides the bubble by v, and zero-bubble schedules fill the remaining gaps.

2 · Limitation

The costs of splitting by layers

Fill and drain

The first stage waits for nothing at the start but everything at the end; the last stage the reverse. That idle time is the bubble.

Activation memory

Each stage keeps activations of every in-flight micro-batch until its backward pass.

Balance

Stages must take equal time; uneven layers or the loss computation make one stage the bottleneck.

4 · Core idea

Four schedules

GPipe

All forwards, then all backwards. Bubble (p−1)/m; must store activations for all m micro-batches.

1F1B (PipeDream)

After a short warm-up, alternate one forward and one backward. Same bubble, but at most p micro-batches in flight.

Interleaved 1F1B (Megatron)

Each GPU holds v non-adjacent chunks of layers; the bubble shrinks to (p−1)/(v·m) at the cost of more sends.

Zero bubble

Split backward into input-gradient and weight-gradient parts and schedule the weight part into the gaps: up to 23% faster than 1F1B.

At a glance

Activations flow forward, gradients flow back; micro-batches keep every stage busy except while the pipeline fills and drains.

5 · How it works

Worked example: bubbles for an 8-stage pipeline

p = 8 stages; bubble fraction = (p − 1)/m of the ideal time, or (p − 1)/(v·m) with v interleaved chunks per GPU (Megatron; also quoted by Llama 3).

Worked example

  1. m = 8 micro-batches: (8 − 1)/8 = 87.5% extra time.
  2. m = 32: 21.9%.
  3. m = 32 with v = 4 interleaved chunks: 7 / (4 × 32) = 5.5%.
  4. GPipe holds activations for all 32 micro-batches at once; 1F1B holds at most p = 8 on the first stage (notebook 04 simulates both).
Many micro-batches and interleaving shrink the bubble; 1F1B keeps the memory bounded while doing so.
6 · Why it works

Why pipelines are worth it

Little communication

Only activations at stage boundaries move, point to point, so pipelines work across nodes over the network.

Memory per GPU ÷ p

Each GPU stores only its stages' weights and optimizer state.

The price is the bubble

It needs many micro-batches, which needs a large enough global batch.

Combines with TP and DP

Typical: TP (tensor parallelism) inside the node, PP (pipeline parallelism) across a few nodes, DP (data parallelism) outermost (Megatron PTD-P: 52% of peak on 3,072 GPUs).

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"1F1B removes the bubble."

It has the same bubble as GPipe; it reduces activation memory.

"More stages always help."

More stages increase the bubble ((p−1)/m) unless micro-batches grow too.

"Pipelines change the gradient."

With synchronous flushes (GPipe, 1F1B) gradients are exactly those of the full batch.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Bubble = (p − 1)/m. Interleaved: (p − 1)/(v·m).
  • GPipe guideline: m ≥ 4p. Bubble becomes negligible.
  • 1F1B memory ≤ p micro-batches. Instead of m with GPipe.

⚠ Traps

  • Unbalanced stages. The slowest stage sets the pace; embeddings and the loss layer make first and last stages heavy (Llama 3 removes a layer from each).
  • Too few micro-batches. A small global batch with deep pipelines wastes most of the time in bubbles.
  • Forgetting point-to-point latency. Interleaving multiplies the number of sends by v.

What does 1F1B improve over GPipe, and what does it not?

It bounds in-flight activations to about p micro-batches instead of m; the bubble is unchanged.

Why is pipeline parallelism usually placed across nodes rather than tensor parallelism?

It only sends activations between neighbouring stages once per micro-batch, far less traffic than TP's per-layer all-reduces.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Pipeline parallelism splits the layers into stages and streams micro-batches through them; the bubble (p−1)/(v·m) and activation memory decide the schedule.
Say it in 30 seconds

Pipeline parallelism gives each GPU a contiguous block of layers. One batch would leave every stage idle while others work, so the batch is split into micro-batches that flow through the stages at once. The remaining idle time while the pipeline fills and drains is the bubble, (p minus 1) over m of the ideal time. GPipe runs all forwards then all backwards and stores every micro-batch's activations; 1F1B alternates forwards and backwards to bound memory; interleaved schedules give each GPU several chunks and divide the bubble by v; zero-bubble schedules fill the gaps with weight-gradient work. Because only boundary activations move, pipelines work well across nodes.

Problem: deep models across nodes; naive staging leaves GPUs idle.
Core idea: micro-batches; bubble (p−1)/(v·m); 1F1B bounds memory.
Practice: balance stages; many micro-batches; interleave or zero-bubble.

Further reading

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

GPipe: Efficient Training of Giant Neural Networks using Pipeline Parallelism
Huang et al. · NeurIPS 2019

Splits a model into stages and the batch into micro-batches; the bubble becomes negligible with enough micro-batches.

Remember: bubble negligible when micro-batches ≥ 4 × stages
PipeDream: Fast and Efficient Pipeline Parallel DNN (deep neural network) Training
Narayanan et al. · SOSP 2019

Introduces the one-forward-one-backward (1F1B) schedule that interleaves forward and backward passes to bound in-flight activations.

Remember: up to 5× faster time-to-accuracy than data parallelism
Efficient Large-Scale Language Model Training on GPU Clusters Using Megatron-LM
Narayanan et al. · SC 2021

Combines tensor, pipeline and data parallelism (PTD-P) with an interleaved pipeline schedule, placing tensor parallelism inside nodes.

Remember: 1T parameters at 502 PFLOP (quadrillion floating-point operations)/s on 3,072 GPUs, 52% of peak
Zero Bubble Pipeline Parallelism
Qi et al. · ICLR 2024

Splits the backward pass into input-gradient and weight-gradient parts and schedules them to fill pipeline bubbles.

Remember: up to 23% over 1F1B (31% with relaxed memory)
