Reliability and stability

Keeping a months-long, 16,000-GPU run alive: fast checkpoint and restart, straggler detection, and the loss-spike playbook (rollback, skip data, z-loss, QK-norm).

What is the problem?At 16K GPUs (graphics processing units) something fails every few hours, a single slow GPU slows every step, and the loss can suddenly spike and diverge, each threatening weeks of compute.
Core ideaMake every failure cheap: frequent, fast (async or in-memory) checkpoints and automated restart; detect and evict stragglers; and handle instability with rollback-and-skip, FP32 (32-bit floating point) reductions, z-loss and QK normalization.
1 · Problem

Goodput vs checkpoint interval

Expected fraction of wall-clock time spent on useful training, from a simple model: overhead ≈ C/τ (saving) + τ/(2·MTBF (mean time between failures)) (lost work) + R/MTBF (restarts). Move the checkpoint cost and interval.

Analytic approximation with a 10-minute restart; notebook 08 checks it with a Monte Carlo simulation of a 54-day run.

2 · Limitation

Three ways a big run loses time

Hard failures

GPU, memory, network and host faults: Llama 3 saw 419 unexpected interruptions in 54 days (~78% hardware). See GPU deck 06.

Stragglers

A GPU that is slow but alive (thermal throttling, a bad link) delays every synchronous step for everyone.

Instability

Loss spikes and divergence: PaLM saw about 20 spikes in its largest run.

4 · Core idea

Make failures cheap, slow nodes visible, training stable

Fast checkpoint and restart

Async writes, in-memory copies on peer hosts (Gemini: >13× faster recovery), sharded checkpoints and automated restart.

Detect stragglers

Per-rank step-time and heartbeat monitoring; MegaScale built diagnosis tools and recovered its run 100+ times.

Stabilize the math

FP32 gradient reductions (Llama 3), z-loss on the softmax normalizer (PaLM), QK normalization against exploding attention logits (ViT-22B (ViT = Vision Transformer)), rollback-and-skip.

At a glance

Goodput = useful training time ÷ wall-clock time.

5 · How it works

Stragglers: the slowest GPU sets the pace

Each GPU's step time varies slightly; a synchronous step waits for the slowest. Increase the GPU count and watch the expected slowdown grow, then add one degraded GPU.

Illustrative: each GPU's step time is 1 + noise with 2% standard deviation; the step takes the maximum.

5 · How it works

Worked example: what checkpoint speed buys

Llama 3 405B: 419 interruptions in 54 days → MTBF (mean time between failures) ≈ 3.1 h. Restart cost 10 minutes. Simulated in notebook 08 (5 seeds each).

Worked example

  1. Synchronous checkpoint to storage, 5 min: Young's interval √(2 × 5 min × 3.1 h) ≈ 43 min → goodput 74.5%.
  2. Async checkpoint, 30-second pause: interval ≈ 14 min → goodput 88.3%.
  3. In-memory checkpoint, 5 seconds (Gemini-style): interval ≈ 5.6 min → goodput 92.2%.
  4. Llama 3 reported more than 90% effective training time over its 54-day run.
The checkpoint cost, not the failure rate, is the lever you control; cutting it from minutes to seconds recovers most lost goodput.
6 · Why it works

Why these measures work

Lost work scales with the interval

Each failure costs about half an interval plus restart; cheaper checkpoints allow shorter intervals.

Automation beats heroics

Llama 3: only three interruptions needed significant manual intervention; the rest were handled automatically.

Spikes are often transient

Rolling back and skipping the triggering batches let PaLM continue; architectural fixes like QK-norm prevent some spikes entirely.

Stable training is possible

DeepSeek-V3 reported no irrecoverable loss spikes and no rollbacks over its whole run.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"With reliable hardware you can skip checkpoints."

Even very reliable GPUs fail every few hours in aggregate at 16K scale.

"A loss spike means the data is bad."

PaLM found spikes weren't reproduced by the same data alone; they depend on data and model state together.

"Stragglers only slow their own work."

In synchronous training every GPU waits for the slowest at each collective.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Interval ≈ √(2·C·MTBF). Young's approximation.
  • Rollback and skip. PaLM restarted ~100 steps before a spike and skipped 200–500 batches.
  • Stability knobs. z-loss 10⁻⁴·log²Z (PaLM), QK-norm (ViT-22B), FP32 reductions (Llama 3).

⚠ Traps

  • Synchronous saves at scale. Every GPU stalls for the whole write.
  • Treating slow nodes as healthy. A straggler slows all 16K GPUs every step.
  • Restarting into the same failure. Drain and test suspect hardware; skip the data that triggered a spike.

Why do in-memory checkpoints help so much?

They take seconds instead of minutes, so you can checkpoint far more often and lose less work per failure, and recovery reads from fast memory instead of remote storage.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

At frontier scale failures, stragglers and loss spikes are routine; fast checkpoints, automated recovery, straggler detection and stability tricks keep goodput above 90%.

Key papers

The primary sources behind this deck, each with what it contributed. Links go to the paper (arXiv or the conference page).

The Llama 3 Herd of Models
Llama Team, AI (artificial intelligence) @ Meta · arXiv 2024

Dense Transformer with GQA (8 KV (key-value) heads), RoPE (rotary position embedding) base 500,000, a 128K vocabulary and a document mask; staged long-context pretraining to 128K; post-training with SFT (supervised fine-tuning), rejection sampling and DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) in six rounds.

Remember: 128K context after six stages over ~800B tokens
MegaScale: Scaling Large Language Model Training to More Than 10,000 GPUs
Jiang et al. · NSDI 2024

Production training system at ByteDance: algorithm-system co-design plus diagnosis tools for stragglers and failures at 10,000+ GPU scale.

Remember: 55.2% MFU (model floating-point-operation utilization, the share of peak compute spent on the model's math) on 12,288 GPUs (175B model), 1.34× Megatron-LM
Gemini: Fast Failure Recovery in Distributed Training with In-Memory Checkpoints
Wang et al. · SOSP 2023

Checkpoints to CPU memory across machines instead of remote storage, with placement and traffic scheduling so checkpoints survive failures and don't slow training.

Remember: more than 13× faster failure recovery
PaLM: Scaling Language Modeling with Pathways
Chowdhery et al. · JMLR 2023

Reports ~20 loss spikes in its largest run, handled by restarting about 100 steps earlier and skipping 200–500 batches; uses a z-loss on the softmax normalizer.

Remember: 46.2% MFU across two TPU (tensor processing unit) v4 Pods
Scaling Vision Transformers to 22 Billion Parameters
Dehghani et al. · ICML 2023

Finds training divergence caused by very large attention logits and fixes it with LayerNorm on queries and keys (QK normalization).
