Distributed Training · 07

Expert parallelism

Training Mixture-of-Experts models by placing experts on different GPUs (graphics processing units): the all-to-all dispatch, load imbalance, and how DeepSeek-V3 limits cross-node traffic.

What is the problem?An MoE layer with hundreds of experts can't live on one GPU, but tokens must reach whichever experts the router picks, wherever they live.
Core ideaPlace different experts on different GPUs (expert parallelism, EP). Each MoE layer does an all-to-all dispatch (send tokens to their experts), local expert compute, and an all-to-all combine (send results back). Balance routing and limit how many nodes each token touches.

2 · Limitation

Why other axes don't fit MoE well

Too many experts per GPU

DeepSeek-V3 has 256 routed experts per layer; replicating them all on every GPU would waste memory.

Data parallelism replicates experts

Each copy would also get only a fraction of the tokens per expert, making expert matmuls tiny.

Tensor parallelism splits small matrices

Each expert FFN (feed-forward network) is relatively small (hidden 2,048 in DeepSeek-V3); splitting it further makes it inefficient.

4 · Core idea

Dispatch, compute, combine

Dispatch (all-to-all)

Each GPU sends each token's hidden state to the GPUs hosting its chosen experts.

Expert compute

Each GPU runs its experts on all tokens it received, as dense, efficient matmuls.

Combine (all-to-all)

Results go back to each token's home GPU and are weighted by the router; the residual stream continues.

At a glance

Two all-to-alls per MoE layer in forward, two more in backward.

5 · How it works

How DeepSeek-V3 makes it practical

Bounded fan-out

Each token goes to at most 4 nodes, cutting InfiniBand traffic; within a node, NVLink carries the rest.

DualPipe

A pipeline schedule that overlaps forward and backward computation with the all-to-all communication of other micro-batches.

Custom kernels

Cross-node all-to-all kernels that use IB and NVLink bandwidth fully while dedicating few SMs (streaming multiprocessors) to communication.

FP8 dispatch

Activations are dispatched in FP8 (8-bit floating point) to halve the bytes moved; 16-way PP (pipeline parallelism), 64-way EP across 8 nodes and ZeRO-1 (ZeRO = Zero Redundancy Optimizer) DP (data parallelism) on 2,048 H800s.

5 · How it works

Worked example: DeepSeek-V3's dispatch traffic

DeepSeek-V3: hidden size 7,168, 8 routed experts per token (of 256), 64-way expert parallelism across 8 nodes, dispatch in FP8 (1 byte). Take 8,192 tokens on one GPU.

Worked example

  1. Each token is sent to up to 8 experts: 8,192 × 8 × 7,168 bytes = 448 MiB dispatched per GPU per MoE layer (forward).
  2. Combine sends the expert outputs back: a similar amount again (BF16 (bfloat16, a 16-bit floating-point format with the exponent range of 32-bit floats) in DeepSeek-V3, so about twice the bytes).
  3. DeepSeek-V3 limits each token to at most 4 nodes, so most of this rides NVLink inside a node and less crosses InfiniBand (IB).
  4. DualPipe overlaps this all-to-all with compute in the forward and backward passes.
MoE trades FLOPs (floating-point operations) for communication: every MoE layer moves each token's hidden state to its experts and back.
6 · Why it works

Why expert parallelism works

Experts are independent

Each expert only needs its own tokens, so placing experts on different GPUs adds no compute coupling.

Big matmuls per expert

Gathering every token routed to an expert from all GPUs keeps its matmuls large and efficient.

Communication heavy

Two all-to-alls per layer forward and two backward; latency- and imbalance-sensitive.

Proven at scale

GShard: 600B+ parameters on 2,048 TPU (tensor processing unit) v3 in 4 days; DeepSeek-V3: 671B parameters in 2.788M H800 GPU-hours.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"EP is like data parallelism for experts."

DP replicates; EP partitions experts, and tokens must be moved to them with all-to-all.

"MoE layers are cheap to train because few parameters are active."

FLOPs per token are low, but the all-to-all traffic and imbalance can dominate step time.

"All-to-all scales like all-reduce."

Each GPU exchanges data with many peers; latency and the busiest link matter, not just total bytes.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • All-to-all volume ≈ tokens × k × hidden × bytes. Per direction, per MoE layer.
  • Limit node fan-out. DeepSeek-V3: at most 4 nodes per token.
  • Overlap all-to-all. DualPipe overlaps it with computation of another micro-batch.

⚠ Traps

  • Imbalanced routing. The busiest GPU sets the step time for everyone.
  • Assuming all-reduce-like scaling. All-to-all cost grows with participants and is latency-sensitive.
  • Ignoring expert memory. All experts' weights and optimizer states must live somewhere.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Expert parallelism places MoE experts on different GPUs and moves tokens to them with two all-to-alls per layer; balance and overlap decide its speed.
Say it in 30 seconds

A Mixture-of-Experts layer has too many experts for one GPU, so expert parallelism spreads them across GPUs. In every MoE layer, an all-to-all dispatch sends each token's hidden state to the GPUs hosting its chosen experts, those GPUs run their experts as dense matmuls, and an all-to-all combine sends the results back. The busiest GPU sets the pace, so routing must be balanced. DeepSeek-V3 makes this practical with 64-way expert parallelism, a limit of four nodes per token, FP8 dispatch, custom kernels and the DualPipe schedule that hides communication behind computation.

Problem: hundreds of experts; tokens must reach them anywhere.
Core idea: partition experts; all-to-all dispatch and combine.
Practice: balance load, limit fan-out, overlap communication.
