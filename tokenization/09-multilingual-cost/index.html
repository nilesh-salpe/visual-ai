Tokenization for LLMs · 09

The multilingual token tax

The same sentence can cost very different numbers of tokens depending on its language, which affects price, latency and how much fits in context (Petrov et al., 2023).

What is the problem?Tokenizers trained mostly on English text split other languages, especially non-Latin scripts, into many more tokens.
Core ideaMeasure the tokenization premium (tokens needed relative to English for the same content) and reduce it with multilingual tokenizer training and vocabulary.
1 · Problem

Same meaning, different bill

"The weather is nice today." in nine languages, tokenized for real with GPT-2's tokenizer. Many models charge per token, generate one token at a time, and have a fixed token budget.

2 · Limitation

Byte-level coverage isn't the same as efficiency

English-heavy training

Merges are learned from the tokenizer's training data, so frequent English strings become single tokens and rarer scripts stay split into byte-level pieces.

Up to 15× more tokens

Petrov et al. found the same text translated into different languages can differ in token length by up to 15×.

Bytes aren't fair either

Even character- and byte-level models show over 4× differences in encoding length for some language pairs, because scripts use different numbers of bytes per character.

4 · Core idea

The tokenization premium

premium(L) = tokens(text in L) ÷ tokens(same text in English)

Cost

Pay-per-token APIs charge the premium directly.

Latency

Generation is one token per step, so more tokens means slower responses.

Context

A fixed token window holds less content.

Petrov et al. (NeurIPS 2023) argue these disparities lead to unequal cost, latency and context room, and recommend multilingually fair tokenizers.

6 · Why it works

Why it happens, and what reduces it

Merges follow the data

BPE merges the most frequent pairs in its training data. If most of that data is English, most merges serve English.

Scripts cost different numbers of bytes

UTF-8 uses 1 byte per ASCII character but 2–3 for many other scripts, so byte-level fallback is uneven.

Fix: multilingual vocabulary

Llama 3 added 28K non-English tokens, improving compression and downstream performance with no effect on English. In this sample, o200k_base brings every language to 5–6 tokens.

Fix: balanced tokenizer training

Train the tokenizer on balanced multilingual data and target parity, as Petrov et al. recommend.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Byte-level BPE treats all languages equally."

It guarantees coverage, not efficiency. Petrov et al. found even byte-level models differ by over 4× across some language pairs.

"The premium is only about price."

It also means higher latency (more decoding steps) and less usable context for the same content.

"A language's premium is a fixed property of the language."

It depends on the tokenizer: the Hindi sample costs 27 tokens with gpt2, 21 with cl100k_base and 5 with o200k_base.

Give two consequences of a high tokenization premium besides price.

Higher latency (more tokens to generate) and less content per context window.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

The same content can cost several times more tokens outside English. That premium is set by the tokenizer, and it shows up as price, latency and lost context.
