Tokenization for LLMs · 06

One text, many tokenizations

The same string can be written as many different token sequences. Subword regularization (Kudo, 2018) and BPE-dropout (BPE = byte-pair encoding) (Provilkov et al., 2020) turn that ambiguity into training noise that makes models more robust.

What is the problem?With one vocabulary, a sentence can be split many ways, and the model treats each split as a completely different input. A deterministic tokenizer only ever shows it one.
Core ideaDuring training, sample a different valid segmentation each time a sentence is used: from a unigram LM (language model), or by randomly dropping BPE merges.

2 · Limitation

A deterministic tokenizer hides this from the model

One view of each sentence

BPE and Viterbi always pick the same split, so the model never learns that different splits mean the same thing.

Brittle to noise

Typos, unusual spacing and other domains create splits the model rarely saw. Kudo argues that multiple candidates make a model "robust to noise and segmentation errors".

Compositionality left implicit

Seeing "books" sometimes as "book" + "s" helps a model learn how words are built (§1).

How could training make a model robust to different segmentations of the same text?

B. This is data augmentation for text: the content stays the same and only the segmentation changes. Kudo samples from a unigram LM; BPE-dropout randomly skips merges. Inference still uses one deterministic segmentation.
4 · Core idea

Two ways to sample segmentations

Subword regularization (Kudo, 2018)

Uses the unigram LM, which can score every segmentation. For each parameter update, sample one segmentation from P(x|X), smoothed by α over the l-best candidates (or all of them, with FFBS (forward-filtering backward-sampling)).

BPE-dropout (Provilkov et al., 2020)

Keeps ordinary BPE but, while segmenting training data, randomly drops some merges, so the same word comes out in different splits. Standard BPE is used at inference.

At a glance

Subword regularization (unigram sampling) and BPE-dropout (skip merges) are training-time only.

5 · How it works

BPE-dropout: randomly skip merges toy merges

The merges are learned from a toy corpus (low ×5, lower ×2, newest ×6, widest ×3, lowest ×3). With dropout p, each merge application is skipped with probability p. p = 0 is ordinary BPE, and a larger p gives finer, more varied splits.

5 · How it works

Worked example: BPE-dropout on one word

Learned merges in order: e+s, es+t, l+o, lo+w, n+e, ne+w. Dropout probability p = 0.1 per merge application.

Worked example

  1. Normal BPE on "newest": n e w e s t → n e w es t → n e w est → ne w est → new est.
  2. With dropout, each merge is skipped with probability 0.1; skipping "ne+w" leaves ne · w · est.
  3. Probability that all 4 merges apply: 0.94 = 0.656; so about 34% of the time the model sees a different split.
  4. At inference, use normal BPE (p = 0).
The model learns that "new + est" and "ne + w + est" mean the same, which helped translation by up to +3 BLEU (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy, an n-gram overlap score for translation) in the paper.
6 · Why it works

Why it works, and the measured gains

Data augmentation

Each sentence is seen under many segmentations, much like random crops and flips for images, which the paper uses as its own analogy.

Reversible, so usable on both sides

Segmentation can be undone exactly, so it can be applied to source and target. Table 6 shows both sides together beat either side alone.

Realistic noise

Sampling guided by the LM beats uniform sampling: α = 0 (uniform) hurt BLEU (Kudo §5.5).

Results (machine translation)

Subword regularization: about +1 to 2 BLEU on most pairs, and +2 or more out of domain (Kudo). BPE-dropout: up to +3 BLEU over BPE and up to +0.9 over subword regularization (Provilkov et al.).

Scope note: both papers evaluate machine translation. The LLM (large language model) tokenizers covered in this guide (GPT-2/tiktoken, Llama 2, Llama 3) are documented as deterministic BPE, and their papers don't describe stochastic segmentation during pretraining.
7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"Sampling happens at inference too."

It is a training-time regularizer. Kudo decodes from the best (or n-best) segmentation, and BPE-dropout uses standard BPE at inference.

"BPE can only ever produce one segmentation."

Standard BPE is deterministic, but BPE-dropout makes it stochastic by randomly skipping merges during training.

"Fully random segmentation is the best noise."

Kudo found α = 0 (uniform sampling, ignoring the LM) hurt performance. Noise that resembles real segmentation variation works better.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Train with noise, test without. Both methods use deterministic segmentation at inference.
  • Sampling temperature α. Unigram sampling sharpens or flattens the distribution over splits.
  • Biggest wins out of domain. And on low-resource pairs.

⚠ Traps

  • Sampling at inference. It adds noise to outputs with no benefit.
  • Uniform sampling. Kudo found α = 0 (uniform) hurt; LM-guided sampling helps.
  • Assuming LLM pretraining uses it. The papers evaluated machine translation; most LLMs tokenize deterministically.
8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Answer from memory. If you can't, revisit the slide named in the answer.

Which method needs a unigram language model?

Subword regularization samples from P(x|X), which the unigram LM provides. BPE-dropout works with ordinary BPE.

What does a larger α do in Kudo's sampling?

A larger α moves toward the Viterbi segmentation; a smaller α moves toward uniform.

Why does it matter that segmentation is reversible?

Because the original text can always be recovered, the noise can be applied to the target side as well as the input, which Kudo shows helps (Table 6).
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

A sentence has many valid tokenizations. Sampling them during training, via subword regularization or BPE-dropout, acts as data augmentation and makes models more robust.
Say it in 30 seconds

With a fixed vocabulary, the same text can be split many ways, and each split is a different id sequence to the model. Kudo's subword regularization samples a segmentation from a unigram LM, smoothed by α, every time a sentence is used in training. BPE-dropout does the same with ordinary BPE by randomly skipping merges. Both are training-time only and use deterministic segmentation at inference. They gave +1 to 3 BLEU in machine translation, with larger gains out of domain.
