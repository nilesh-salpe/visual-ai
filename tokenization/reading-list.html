Tokenization for LLMs: key papers

Every paper cited on the decks' Key papers slides, in the order the guide introduces them. Each entry links to the paper and to the decks that use it.

Neural Machine Translation of Rare Words with Subword Units
Sennrich et al. · ACL 2016 · cited in Why tokenize?, BPE from scratch

Byte-pair encoding for open-vocabulary translation: rare words become sequences of subword units.

Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners (GPT-2)
Radford et al. · OpenAI 2019 · cited in Why tokenize?, BPE from scratch, Byte-level BPE, Vocabulary size trade-offs, Tokenizers in real LLMs, Tokenization quirks

Decoder-only language model that moved layer normalization to the input of each sub-block (pre-norm) and added a final norm.

Remember: context 1,024 tokens, vocabulary 50,257
BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers): Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding
Devlin et al. · NAACL 2019 · cited in Why tokenize?, WordPiece and unigram LM, Tokenizers in real LLMs

Encoder-only model pretrained with masked language modelling; bidirectional context, but not built to generate text.

Remember: masks 15% of tokens
ByT5: Towards a token-free future with pre-trained byte-to-byte models
Xue et al. · TACL 2022 · cited in Byte-level BPE, Multilingual cost, Tokenization quirks

A standard Transformer on raw bytes: no tokenizer, more robust to noise and spelling, at the cost of longer sequences.

Subword Regularization: Improving Neural Network Translation Models with Multiple Subword Candidates
Kudo · ACL 2018 · cited in WordPiece and unigram LM, SentencePiece, One text, many tokenizations

Introduces the unigram language-model tokenizer and trains on sampled segmentations for robustness.

Remember: consistent gains, especially low-resource and out-of-domain
Google's Neural Machine Translation System (GNMT)
Wu et al. · arXiv 2016 · cited in WordPiece and unigram LM

The production deep-LSTM translation system with attention and wordpieces that the Transformer was compared against.

Remember: 60% fewer translation errors than phrase-based production in side-by-side evaluation
SentencePiece: A simple and language independent subword tokenizer and detokenizer for Neural Text Processing
Kudo & Richardson · EMNLP (demo) 2018 · cited in SentencePiece

Trains subword models directly from raw sentences (whitespace becomes a symbol), making tokenization lossless and language-independent.

Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer (T5)
Raffel et al. · JMLR 2020 · cited in SentencePiece, Tokenizers in real LLMs

Systematic study of encoder-decoder pretraining with a span-corruption objective, framing every task as text-to-text.

Remember: corrupts 15% of tokens
BPE-Dropout: Simple and Effective Subword Regularization
Provilkov et al. · ACL 2020 · cited in One text, many tokenizations

Randomly skips BPE merges during training to expose the model to many segmentations; standard BPE at inference.

Remember: up to +3 BLEU over BPE
The Llama 3 Herd of Models
Llama Team, AI (artificial intelligence) @ Meta · arXiv 2024 · cited in Vocabulary size trade-offs, Tokenizers in real LLMs, Multilingual cost

Dense Transformer with GQA (8 KV (key-value) heads), RoPE (rotary position embedding) base 500,000, a 128K vocabulary and a document mask; staged long-context pretraining to 128K; post-training with SFT (supervised fine-tuning), rejection sampling and DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) in six rounds.

Remember: 128K context after six stages over ~800B tokens
Llama 2: Open Foundation and Fine-Tuned Chat Models
Touvron et al. · arXiv 2023 · cited in Vocabulary size trade-offs, Tokenizers in real LLMs

Adds grouped-query attention for the 34B and 70B models and documents the chat post-training: SFT, two reward models and iterative RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) with rejection sampling and PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization).

Remember: 27,540 SFT annotations; over 1M human comparisons
Language Model Tokenizers Introduce Unfairness Between Languages
Petrov et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · cited in Multilingual cost

The same text translated into different languages can need very different numbers of tokens, which changes cost, latency and context.

Remember: up to 15× more tokens
Efficient Training of Language Models to Fill in the Middle
Bavarian et al. · arXiv 2022 · cited in Tokenization quirks

Moves a middle span to the end with special sentinel tokens so a left-to-right model learns infilling, without hurting normal generation.
