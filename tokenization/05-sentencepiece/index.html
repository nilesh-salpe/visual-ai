Tokenization for LLMs · 05

SentencePiece

A language-independent tokenizer and detokenizer that trains directly on raw text, treats whitespace as an ordinary symbol, and round-trips losslessly (Kudo and Richardson, 2018).

What is the problem?Existing tools assumed text was already split into words, and relied on hand-written, language-specific rules to tokenize and detokenize.
Core ideaTreat the input as a raw Unicode sequence. Escape whitespace as ▁ (U+2581), learn BPE (byte-pair encoding) or unigram subwords on top, and decode exactly.
1 · Problem

Tokenizing and detokenizing raw text is language-dependent

English

Raw: Hello world.
Pre-tokenized: [Hello] [world] [.]

The pre-tokenized form has lost the fact that there was no space between "world" and ".", so the detokenizer has to guess with language rules.

Japanese

Raw: こんにちは世界。 (Hello world.)
Pre-tokenized: [こんにちは] [世界] [。]

There are no spaces at all, so splitting into words needs a separate word segmenter, and detokenizing must not insert spaces.

Examples from the SentencePiece paper, §3.1.
2 · Limitation

Hand-written pre- and post-processors don't scale across languages

Hand-crafted rules

Moses, the standard toolkit at the time, uses language-dependent rules "whose effectiveness for NMT (neural machine translation) has not been proven".

European bias

These tools were "mainly designed for European languages where words are segmented with whitespaces".

Multilingual pain

Every language needs its own pre/post-processor configuration, which makes multilingual models hard to build (§1).

How can a tokenizer be exactly reversible on raw text in any language, with no language-specific rules?

B. SentencePiece escapes each space as the visible symbol ▁ (U+2581) and tokenizes the whole string as a character sequence. Because the spaces are inside the tokens, detokenizing is just joining the tokens and turning ▁ back into a space.
4 · Core idea

Raw text in, whitespace as a symbol, lossless out

Decode( Encode( Normalize(text) ) ) = Normalize(text)

Raw sentences

"SentencePiece can train subword models directly from raw sentences", with no word segmenter needed.

▁ for whitespace

Spaces are escaped as ▁ (U+2581), so Hello▁world. → [Hello] [▁wor] [ld] [.].

Two algorithms

Implements both BPE (Sennrich et al.) and the unigram LM (Kudo), selected as a training option.

At a glance

Language-independent: no hand-written rules for where words start.

5 · How it works

Four components and id management

Pipeline (§2)

The Normalizer maps equivalent Unicode to a canonical form (NFKC (Unicode Normalization Form KC (compatibility composition)) by default). The Trainer learns the subword model from the normalized corpus. The Encoder normalizes and tokenizes, and the Decoder turns pieces back into normalized text.

Vocabulary and ids (§3.3)

You set the final vocabulary size (--vocab_size) rather than a number of merges, which works for both BPE and unigram. Ids are reserved for meta symbols: <unk>, <s> (BOS (beginning of sequence)), </s> (EOS (end of sequence)), <pad>, plus custom ones such as language tags <2ja> and <2de>.

5 · How it works

Worked example: lossless round trip

Text: "Hello world." with a unigram SentencePiece model.

Worked example

  1. Replace spaces with ▁ and prefix one: "▁Hello▁world."
  2. Segment the stream, e.g. [▁Hello, ▁world, .].
  3. Decode: concatenate pieces → "▁Hello▁world." → replace ▁ with a space → " Hello world." → strip the leading space.
  4. No information about spacing was lost, so detokenization needs no language-specific rules (also works for Japanese or Chinese without spaces).
Treating whitespace as an ordinary symbol is what makes SentencePiece reversible across languages. Llama 2 and T5 use it.
6 · Why it works

Why it works, and where it is used

Lossless by design

Whitespace lives inside the tokens, so detokenization is ''.join(tokens).replace('▁', ' '), with no language rules.

Language-independent

Chinese, Japanese and Korean need no external word segmenter: the model learns pieces from raw characters.

Efficient

BPE training uses an O(N log N) heap-based algorithm instead of a naive O(N²) one, and unigram training and segmentation are linear in input size (§3.2).

Used by LLMs

Llama 1 and 2: BPE via SentencePiece, 32K vocabulary, digits split individually, bytes for unknown UTF-8 (UTF = Unicode Transformation Format) characters. T5: SentencePiece with a 32,000-wordpiece vocabulary.

Llama 2 paper §2.2 (Tokenizer); T5 paper §3.1.3.
7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"SentencePiece is a new segmentation algorithm."

It is a library that implements two existing algorithms, BPE and the unigram LM, with raw-text training and lossless detokenization.

"▁ is just an underscore."

It is U+2581 (LOWER ONE EIGHTH BLOCK), chosen so it won't collide with real underscores in the text.

"SentencePiece reproduces the exact raw input."

It is lossless with respect to the normalized text. NFKC normalization (the default) can change characters, e.g. fullwidth "ＡＢＣ" becomes "ABC" (§3.4).
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • ▁ marks a word start. It is U+2581, not an underscore.
  • One library, two algorithms. SentencePiece implements both BPE and unigram LM.
  • Byte fallback. Unknown characters become UTF-8 byte tokens (as in Llama 2).

⚠ Traps

  • Calling SentencePiece an algorithm. It is a toolkit and pipeline; BPE or unigram does the segmentation.
  • Stripping ▁ incorrectly. Leads to doubled or missing spaces on decode.
  • Forgetting normalization. NFKC changes some characters before tokenization.

Check yourself

Which symbol does SentencePiece use for whitespace?

▁, U+2581. subword-nmt instead uses @@ as an intra-word boundary marker.

What does lossless tokenization guarantee?

Decode(Encode(Normalize(text))) = Normalize(text) (§3.1).

Why does treating whitespace as a symbol matter for Japanese and Chinese?

These languages have no spaces between words, so a tokenizer that assumes space-split input needs an external segmenter, and detokenizing must not add spaces. SentencePiece needs neither.
9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

SentencePiece tokenizes raw text in any language: whitespace becomes ▁, BPE or unigram pieces are learned on top, and decoding is exactly lossless on normalized text.
Say it in 30 seconds

Earlier tokenizers assumed space-separated words and relied on language-specific rules to tokenize and detokenize, which breaks for Japanese or Chinese and complicates multilingual models. SentencePiece trains directly on raw sentences: it normalizes with NFKC, escapes spaces as ▁ (U+2581), and learns BPE or unigram pieces. Because whitespace is inside the tokens, decoding is just joining them and restoring spaces, so Decode(Encode(Normalize(text))) equals Normalize(text). Llama 1 and 2 and T5 use it.

Problem: language-specific pre-/post-processing; text without spaces; detokenization that isn't reversible.
Core idea: a raw-text tokenizer with ▁ for spaces, BPE or unigram inside, and lossless decoding.
