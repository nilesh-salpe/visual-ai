Tokenization for LLMs · 04

WordPiece and the unigram language model

Tokenizers that choose subwords by likelihood instead of raw frequency. The unigram LM (Kudo, 2018) also gives every segmentation a probability.

What is the problem?BPE (byte-pair encoding) merges greedily by frequency and gives exactly one segmentation, with no score for how good it is.
Core ideaUse a probabilistic model of subwords. WordPiece picks merges by likelihood; the unigram LM starts large, prunes by likelihood, and segments with Viterbi.
1 · Problem

What makes one segmentation better than another?

"tokenizers" could be token|izers, token|izer|s, tok|en|izers, or t|o|k|e|n|i|z|e|r|s. BPE always returns the same one because it replays fixed merges. It never scores the options.

Candidate segmentations are illustrative.

2 · Limitation

BPE's limits, as Kudo describes them

Greedy

BPE is "based on a greedy and deterministic symbol replacement". Early merges are fixed and never reconsidered.

No probabilities

It "can not provide multiple segmentations with probabilities" (Kudo 2018, §3.1).

Frequency, not fit

Merges are chosen by raw pair counts, not by how well the resulting vocabulary explains the data.

4 · Core idea

Two likelihood-based tokenizers

WordPiece

Builds the vocabulary incrementally like BPE, but "uses a likelihood instead of frequency" to choose merges (Kudo 2018, footnotes 1–2; Schuster & Nakajima, 2012). BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) uses "WordPiece embeddings with a 30,000 token vocabulary".

Unigram language model

Each subword has a probability p(x), and a segmentation's probability is the product: P(x) = Π p(xi). The best segmentation is found by Viterbi. The vocabulary is learned by starting big and pruning (Kudo, 2018).

At a glance

Unigram LM goes top-down (prune); BPE and WordPiece go bottom-up (merge).

5 · How it works

Unigram LM: score every segmentation, pick the best toy probabilities

With P(x) = Π p(xi), each way of splitting "tokenizers" gets a probability. Viterbi finds the most probable one in linear time. Drag the probability of the piece "token" and watch the winner change.

5 · How it works

Learning the unigram vocabulary: EM, then prune

The vocabulary itself is unknown, so the unigram method (Kudo §3.2) starts from a large seed vocabulary and repeatedly removes the least useful pieces. Step through the rounds toy loss values.

(a) EM

Fix the vocabulary and estimate p(x) with expectation-maximization.

(b) Loss

For each subword, compute how much the likelihood drops if it is removed.

(c) Keep top η%

Keep the top η = 80% by loss. Single characters are always kept, so nothing becomes unknown.

5 · How it works

Worked example: scoring segmentations with a unigram LM

Toy piece probabilities: un 0.10, believ 0.02, able 0.08, unbeliev 0.001, u 0.03, n 0.03, b 0.02.

Worked example

  1. Unigram LM probability of a segmentation = product of piece probabilities.
  2. un · believ · able: 0.10 × 0.02 × 0.08 = 0.00016.
  3. unbeliev · able: 0.001 × 0.08 = 0.00008.
  4. u · n · believ · able: 0.03 × 0.03 × 0.02 × 0.08 = 0.0000014. Viterbi picks the first.
The unigram model keeps a probability for every segmentation, which also lets you sample alternatives (deck 06).
6 · Why it works

Why likelihood-based tokenizers help

Principled segmentation

Viterbi returns the single most probable split under the model, not just the result of replaying merges.

Probabilities for alternatives

The unigram LM can output several segmentations with probabilities, which deck 06 uses to sample them during training.

An entropy-coding view

Kudo frames the unigram model as an entropy encoder: the optimal code length for a symbol s is −log ps, so likely pieces get short codes (§3.4).

No unknowns

All characters stay in the vocabulary, so character-level segmentation is always one of the candidates.

7 · Misconceptions

Common misconceptions

Each statement below is a mental model people often get wrong. Decide why it is wrong, then reveal.

"WordPiece and the unigram LM are the same algorithm."

WordPiece grows a vocabulary by likelihood-chosen merges, BPE-style. The unigram LM shrinks a large seed vocabulary by pruning, then segments with Viterbi.

"Unigram tokenization picks the longest matching piece first."

It picks the segmentation with the highest product of probabilities, found by Viterbi, which isn't always the one with the longest pieces.

"Pruning can remove characters and create unknowns."

Subwords consisting of a single character are always kept (§3.2), so every string stays encodable.
7 · Tricks and traps

Tricks and traps

Rules of thumb that make this topic quick to reason about, and the mistakes people most often make with it.

✓ Tricks

  • Unigram: prune down; BPE: merge up. Different directions, similar final sizes.
  • Viterbi finds the best split. Dynamic programming over positions.
  • WordPiece marks continuations. BERT's "##" prefix means "joins the previous piece".

⚠ Traps

  • Calling WordPiece = BPE. WordPiece picks merges by likelihood gain, not raw frequency.
  • Assuming one valid split. Many segmentations exist with the same vocabulary.
  • Forgetting probabilities are learned. EM (expectation-maximization) re-estimates them as pieces are pruned.

9 · Key takeaway

Key takeaway

Likelihood-based tokenizers score segmentations: WordPiece chooses merges by likelihood, and the unigram LM prunes a big vocabulary with EM and segments with Viterbi.
Say it in 30 seconds

BPE merges by raw frequency and replays merges greedily, so it gives one segmentation with no probability. WordPiece, used by BERT with 30,000 tokens, grows the vocabulary like BPE but chooses merges by likelihood. The unigram LM gives each subword a probability, scores a segmentation as the product of its pieces' probabilities, finds the best with Viterbi, and learns the vocabulary by running EM and pruning the 20% of pieces whose removal hurts likelihood least. Single characters are always kept.

Problem: BPE is greedy and deterministic, with no probabilities.
Core idea: model subwords probabilistically: WordPiece merges by likelihood; the unigram LM uses EM, pruning and Viterbi.
